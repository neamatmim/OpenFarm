# Bangladesh law on pooling investors' money into a cattle project

## Question

**Research (AFK).** What does Bangladesh law allow and require when a farm takes money from many investors (not only friends and family) to buy and fatten cattle and shares the profit or loss?

- Is taking money from the public this way deposit-taking, a collective investment scheme, crowdfunding or a partnership? Which regulator is involved (Bangladesh Bank, BSEC, MRA, RJSC), and what licence or registration is needed? Are there limits on how many investors or how much money?
- What happened in the government crackdowns on online cattle and agro investment schemes, and which lines did those schemes cross?
- What form must the contract take: partnership deed, a written agreement on stamp paper, notarisation? Is there anything the investor must be given?
- Tax: is withholding due on profit paid to an investor, is there VAT on a management share, and how does it land in the farm's own accounts?
- Shariah structures investors commonly expect (Mudarabah vs Musharakah): how each splits profit, how each bears loss, and what each makes the manager liable for (negligence).
- What records must be kept, and for how long?

Primary sources only (acts, BSEC/Bangladesh Bank rules and circulars, NBR, AAOIFI standards), each claim cited.

## Answer

Researched 2026-09-17. Findings on branch `research/bangladesh-pooled-investment` at `docs/research/bangladesh-pooled-investment.md`. They are cited to bdlaws, BSEC and AAOIFI; the 2026 Bangladesh Bank crackdown comes from news reports only.

No licence exists for "a farm pooling investors' money into cattle". The law draws lines around it:

- **Never repay capital or promise a return.** Repayable public money is banking business (Bank Company Act 1991). A receipt promising profit is finance business (Finance Company Act 2023).
- **No platform takes investment from the public** without Bangladesh Bank approval (Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2024 s.15(2)). Bangladesh Bank cited it against apps in Aug 2026.
- **More than 20 people in one business for gain must be a company** (Companies Act 1994 s.4). A private company allows at most 50 members and no public invitation.
- **BSEC routes don't fit.** There are no crowdfunding rules in force, and a regulated fund needs Tk 10 crore.
- **No referral commissions** (anti-pyramid law, 2013).
- **What is allowed:** genuine profit-and-loss sharing among a closed, known group.
- **Contract:** written and stamped at signing. The duty is Tk 2,000–4,000 as a partnership deed or Tk 300 as an agreement; the rates come from a 2022 schedule and need re-checking. No notarisation is required. An unregistered partnership cannot sue on its contracts.
- **Tax:** income from raising animals is agricultural. 10% is withheld from an individual's profit if the contract reads as a deposit; no withholding section was found for a genuine profit share. Live cattle sales are VAT-exempt; a management fee probably is not.
- **Shariah (AAOIFI Standards 12 and 13):** profit is a percentage of actual profit and nobody guarantees capital.
  - _Mudarabah:_ investors bear capital loss; the farm loses its effort unless negligent. No fee on top, and the farm's ordinary staff costs are its own.
  - _Musharakah:_ loss is shared by capital share; a managing partner gets a bigger share rather than a fee.
- **Records:** keep them at least 12 years.

**Constraints on the design:**

- No capital or return promise anywhere, projections included.
- An investor's result can be negative.
- Percentages are fixed at signing, changed only by dated amendment.
- No distribution until capital is intact; interim payouts must be reversible.
- Bank channels only.
- Warn at 20 and 50 investors.
- Store each investor's stamped contract.
- No sign-up, pay-in or referral features until a lawyer clears them.

**A lawyer's opinion is needed** on:

- whether the contract counts as a deposit;
- whether investor-facing software needs Bangladesh Bank approval;
- whether a profit-share right is a security;
- whether separate mudarabah investors count towards the 20-person limit;
- which stamp rate applies.
