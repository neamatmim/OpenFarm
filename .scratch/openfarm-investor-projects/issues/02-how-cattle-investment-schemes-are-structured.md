# How cattle-investment and agri-crowdfunding schemes in Bangladesh are structured

## Question

**Research (AFK).** How do existing schemes structure a pooled fattening investment, so our contract starts from known practice rather than a blank page? Look at agri-crowdfunding platforms such as iFarmer and WeGro, published Qurbani cattle-investment offers, and published Mudarabah livestock contracts.

- **The unit of investment:** a fixed share price or any amount; minimum and maximum; per project or per animal.
- **Profit and loss:** typical split percentages between investors and farm, whether a management fee comes first, and how loss is shared.
- **What the farm's side covers** (shed, labour, utilities) and what is charged to the project.
- **Animal death:** insurance, replacement, or loss to the pool.
- **Unsold cattle** at the end of the window, early exit by an investor, and a project that is not fully funded.
- **What investors are sent:** statements, photos, updates, cadence, and the settlement document.
- **How payouts are made** (bank, bKash/Nagad) and when.

Cite the published terms and pages each fact comes from. Say where a scheme failed or was shut down and why.

## Answer

Researched 2026-09-17. Findings on branch `research/cattle-investment-schemes` at `docs/research/cattle-investment-schemes.md`. It has a comparison table. Some foreign prospectus terms and some search-snippet figures were not re-checked, and are labelled as such.

- **The common structure:** investors buy fixed-price shares in a batch and supply all the capital. The farm manages and shares the profit; where a split is published, investors get 30–50%. Examples:
  - **WeGro:** Tk 200,000 per unit, 4 months, 40% to investors, paid 3–6 business days after the end.
  - **iFarmer's Mudarabah farm:** Tk 135,000 shares, 20 cattle, 40% to investors.
  - **Freshie Farm "Project Borga" (the closest to ours):** Tk 50,000 shares, 7 months. Feed, salaries, treatment and electricity come out of the pool; only the shed is the farm's. A dead cow is the batch's loss.
- **Loss:** borne by capital unless the farm was negligent. Payout is one sum at the end into a bank account. Investors get a certificate at the start and periodic photo and weight updates.
- **Fixed returns collapse.** No surviving scheme guarantees a return; every scheme that promised fixed monthly returns collapsed (Nazran, Ponorogo, FURT). iFarmer moved from fixed to "projected" returns.
- **Rare rules:**
  - *Unsold animals:* only nusaQu (Indonesia) has one — the farm buys them at the Eid price.
  - *Early exit:* only WeGro has one — a full refund in the first 20% of the term, then a charge of 2% or Tk 2,500 and no profit.
  - *Insurance:* Green Delta sells 4–6-month fattening cover.
- **Nobody publishes** a rule for an underfunded batch, or a settlement statement.
- **Forks for us:**
  - Mudarabah, Musharakah (the Owner puts in cash too), or a fixed return (deposit-like, ruled out by the law research);
  - the split, and pooled shares against named animals;
  - death: pool loss, insurance, or a mutual pool;
  - unsold cattle, early exit and underfunding, which we must design ourselves.
- **Our charges match a published fork.** Our decision that staff and utilities are the Farm's (What a project is charged for) is the "farm bears them for a bigger share" fork, not Freshie's model.
