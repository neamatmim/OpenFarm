# What a nominee is in Bangladeshi law and in Shariah

Status: open

Type: research

Blocked by: —

Map: [OpenFarm: an Investor may name several Nominees](../map.md)

## Question

An Investor in a **Venture** (a mudarabah, the Owner as mudarib; see `docs/research/bangladesh-pooled-investment.md`) names a nominee on the farm's Investment Agreement. Before OpenFarm lets them name several, find out what a nominee *is* in Bangladesh:

- **Collector or beneficiary?** Do Bangladeshi statutes and courts treat a nominee as a trustee who collects and hands on to the heirs under Muslim personal law (faraid), or as someone who takes in their own right? Cover the Bank Company Act 1991, the Insurance Act 2010, the Companies Act 1994 and CDBL/BO-account nominations, the Government Savings Certificates rules, and the leading Supreme Court decisions.
- **A private contract.** Does anything govern nomination in a private mudarabah or partnership agreement, or is it only what the contract says? Could a contract that pays a nominee in their own right be unenforceable against the heirs?
- **Shariah.** What do Islamic finance practice and scholars in Bangladesh (the Islamic banks' Shariah boards, AAOIFI) say about nomination: a nominee as wasi or amin, the one-third bequest limit (wasiyyah), and bequests to an heir?
- **Several nominees.** How do banks, insurers and CDBL handle more than one: shares as percentages, a cap on the count (CDBL allows two?), an order, minors and guardians? Collect the forms' actual fields.

Primary sources first: the Acts, Bangladesh Bank circulars, BSEC/CDBL rules, reported judgments, and AAOIFI standards.
