# How much a fattening bull should gain

**Question:** What do published standards and trials say a healthy growing or fattening bull should gain a day, by breed type (indigenous/deshi, dairy crossbred, Brahman crossbred) and by weight? What goes with it: dry matter intake, feed conversion, the energy equations behind gain, compensatory growth in thin bought cattle, what pulls gain down, what researchers call a slow gainer, and how far apart two weighings must be for a gain figure to mean anything? The figures are for an Owner-facing reference page and for the default "expected daily gain" OpenFarm compares each animal against.

**Researched:** 29 September 2026. Primary sources read in full:

- **Bangladeshi fattening trials:** Siddque et al. 2015 (indigenous and L×HF bulls, BLRI/HSTU); Rashid et al. 2015 (F1 L×Brahman bulls, LRRD); Rahman et al. 2009 (emaciated bulls, UMS + concentrate); Sultana et al. 2017 (Pabna bulls by age, BLRI); Roy et al. 2025 (Red Chittagong bulls by age, BLRI); Hossain et al. 2024 (Red Chittagong growing bulls, BLRI); Mustafa et al. 2021 (L×Sahiwal bulls, BAPARD/BAU).
- **Energy systems:** Cabezas-Garcia et al. 2021 (a review that tabulates the AFRC, CSIRO, INRA, NASEM and BR-Corte equations side by side); Salah et al. 2014 (meta-analysis of 590 publications on growing ruminants in warm climates, abstract); Silva et al. 2021 (Holstein × Zebu steers); Francis et al. 2024 (worked use of the NASEM equations); University of Arkansas MP391 (tables generated from NASEM 2016).
- **Compensatory growth:** Keogh et al. 2016; Hornick et al. 2000 (abstract).
- **Weighing and slow gainers:** MacNeil et al. 2021; Sainz 2019 (abstract); University of Tennessee and University of Georgia bull-test rules.

## Answer for the ticket

1. **Bangladeshi trials put a deshi bull on a fattening ration at about 0.45–0.60 kg a day.** The ration is green grass or silage, straw, and concentrate at about 1–1.5% of live weight. The figure rises with weight: about 0.3–0.45 kg/d under 100 kg, and 0.52–0.60 kg/d at 180–290 kg (Sultana 2017; Roy 2025; Hossain 2024; Siddque 2015). **On straw and grass alone a deshi bull gains only 0.16–0.29 kg/d** (Rahman 2009; Siddque 2015; [SECONDARY] studies quoted by Rashid 2015).
2. **Crossbreds gain more, but the two Bangladeshi dairy-cross trials disagree threefold.**
   - Siddque 2015 found L×HF bulls of 220–290 kg gained **1.0–1.17 kg/d** on an improved ration.
   - Mustafa's two on-station trials found L×HF (213 kg) and L×Sahiwal (169 kg) bulls gained only **0.31–0.35 kg/d**. That held whether the ration was concentrate, concentrate with UMS, or grass alone.

   **F1 L×Brahman bulls gained 0.87–0.98 kg/d on high-concentrate rations** at 170 kg and at 343 kg (Rashid 2015, two trials). They gained **0.21 kg/d on UMS alone**.

3. **Dry matter intake was 1.9–3.1% of live weight in every Bangladeshi trial read.** It falls as the animal gets heavier: from 2.94% at 98 kg to 2.36% at 288 kg in Pabna bulls (Sultana 2017). Breed did not change intake as a share of weight (Siddque 2015).
4. **Feed conversion runs from about 5 to 20 kg of dry matter per kg of gain.** Dearer feed buys better conversion:
   - 5–8 on good rations for young or light bulls;
   - 9.5–16 for grass-and-straw rations with 1.5% concentrate;
   - 20–35 on straw or UMS alone.
5. **The energy method.**
   - **NASEM:** maintenance is NEm = 0.077 × SBW^0.75 Mcal/d. Bulls are multiplied by 1.15, and breed factors run from 0.9 (Brahman) to 1.2 (Holstein).
   - **Warm-climate meta-analysis:** Salah 2014 puts cattle maintenance at **631 kJ ME per kg LW^0.75 a day**, and **24.3 kJ ME per gram of gain**.
   - **CSIRO:** it multiplies maintenance by 1.2 for _Bos indicus_ against 1.4 for _Bos taurus_, and by 1.15 for bulls.
6. **Compensatory growth.** Holstein-Friesian bulls held to 0.6 kg/d for 125 days then gained **2.6 kg/d against 1.3 kg/d** for bulls fed fully all along, over the next **55 days** (Keogh 2016). Hornick 2000 says the lean-tissue phase "lasts for some weeks". A thin bought bull's first one to two months will overstate what he will gain later.
7. **What pulls gain down (quantified):**
   - worms: deworming added **0.05 kg/d** (95% CI 0.03–0.07) in US stocker calves;
   - digital dermatitis: **0.11–0.17 kg/d** less;
   - lung lesions: **0.075 kg/d** less;
   - disease at pen level: ADG fell **0.036 kg/d for every 1% of the pen that died**;
   - heat: shade or water cooling raised ADG from **0.88 to 0.94–0.98 kg/d**.

   **No quantified effect of water shortage or of lameness on tropical bulls was found.**

8. **Slow-gainer thresholds in published use are relative to the group, not fixed kg figures:**
   - the University of Tennessee bull test excludes a bull whose ADG is **below 80% of his contemporary breed group's average** (ratio under 80);
   - the Georgia test sells bulls in about the **top two-thirds** on gain and weight per day of age;
   - Nebraska's baseline is a ratio of **90** [SNIPPET].

   **No Bangladeshi source gives a slow-gainer threshold.**

9. **Weighing.**
   - Day-to-day gut fill moved individual weights by **−18 to +22 kg (SD 5.34 kg)** in 1,018 cattle [SECONDARY, Kelly 2019 via MacNeil 2021].
   - Over **14 days** that alone is **±0.38 kg/d** of ADG error, one SD. Over **28 days** it is **±0.19 kg/d**, and over **56 days ±0.10 kg/d** (my arithmetic).
   - The Beef Improvement Federation standard for measuring ADG is **70 days** with weighings every two weeks [SECONDARY via MacNeil 2021].
   - The Tennessee test gives a **14-day settling period**, averages **two consecutive days' weights** at start and end, and weighs every **28 days**.
   - **A gain figure between two single weighings less than about 28 days apart is mostly noise** for a deshi bull gaining 0.5 kg/d.

### 1.4 International reference points (not for use as defaults)

- **NASEM-derived tables.** The University of Arkansas tables (MP391) set growing bull calves of 500–900 lb (227–408 kg) at 1–3 lb/d (0.45–1.36 kg/d). Those are targets the tables solve for, not what cattle achieve.
- **Temperate Holstein-Friesian bulls** on a 70% concentrate ration gained **1.9 kg/d** from about 400 to 600 kg, and **1.3 kg/d** from 601 to 673 kg (Keogh 2016).

None of these is a Bangladeshi figure, and none is used in the defaults below.

## 4. The energy method

For reference text only. None of these is needed to flag a slow gainer.

### 4.1 NASEM (2016), North American beef system

As quoted by Cabezas-Garcia et al. 2021, Francis et al. 2024 and MP391:

- **Maintenance:** NEm (Mcal/d) = 0.077 × SBW^0.75.
  - Cabezas-Garcia gives it in MJ as 0.322 × SBW^0.75, plus 0.0293 × (20 − Tp) when the air is not at 20 °C, where Tp is air temperature.
  - SBW is shrunk body weight: **full weight × 0.96** (MP391).
  - MP391 applies **× 1.15 for bulls**.
  - The breed factor "ranges from 0.9 (e.g., Brahman) to 1.2 (e.g., Holstein)" (Cabezas-Garcia).
- **Gain:** retained energy = 0.0557 × EQSBW^0.75 × SWG^1.097 (Mcal/d). EQSBW is equivalent shrunk body weight and SWG is shrunk weight gain (Francis 2024).
  - EQSBW scales the animal's weight to a standard mature size: SBW × 478 ÷ its final shrunk weight (Francis 2024). A small-framed deshi bull at a given weight is therefore "further along" than a Holstein cross at the same weight.
  - On an empty-body basis the same equation is 0.266 MJ × EBW^0.75 × EBG^1.097 (Cabezas-Garcia), where EBW is empty body weight and EBG empty body gain.
- **Linking the two:** DMI = retained energy ÷ (0.877 × NEm of the diet − 0.41) (Francis 2024). This is how gain is back-calculated from what was eaten.
- **Worked example (my arithmetic):** a 300 kg bull has SBW 288 kg, and 288^0.75 = 69.9.
  - NEm = 0.077 × 69.9 = **5.4 Mcal/d**, or 6.2 Mcal/d with the bull factor.
  - Taking EQSBW = SBW, 0.8 kg/d of shrunk gain needs 0.0557 × 69.9 × 0.8^1.097 = **3.0 Mcal/d** of NEg.

### 4.2 CSIRO (2007), Australian system, used for tropical _Bos indicus_

As tabulated by Cabezas-Garcia et al. 2021:

- **Maintenance:** ME (MJ/d) = C × K × M × 0.28 × LW^0.75 × e^(−0.03A) + 0.1 × MEp × km. The terms:
  - **K = 1.2 for _Bos indicus_, 1.4 for _Bos taurus_**;
  - C = 1.15 for bulls;
  - A is age in years;
  - MEp is the ME used for production;
  - M adjusts for milk in the diet (1 for weaned cattle).
- **Gain:** energy value of gain (MJ/kg) = 0.92 × [(6.7 + R) + (20.3 − R) ÷ (1 + e^(−6(P − 0.4)))], where P is live weight ÷ standard reference weight and R adjusts for rate of gain.
- **Compensatory growth:** CSIRO "accounts for compensatory growth of immature animals after a period of sub-optimal nutrition" (Cabezas-Garcia).

### 4.3 Warm-climate meta-analysis (Salah, Sauvant & Archimède 2014, _Animal_)

This is the only figure found that is built from tropical data (590 publications).

- **Maintenance: 631.26 kJ ME per kg LW^0.75 a day** for cattle.
- **Gain: 24.3 kJ ME per gram of gain**, the same for sheep, goats and cattle.
- Per LW^0.75, "there was no difference among genotypes intra-species" for maintenance or gain.
- **Worked example (my arithmetic):** a 200 kg deshi bull (200^0.75 = 53.2) gaining 0.5 kg/d needs 53.2 × 631 = 33.6 MJ for maintenance, plus 500 × 24.3 = 12.2 MJ for gain, **45.7 MJ ME a day**.
  - At 10.5 MJ ME per kg DM, the density of Rashid 2015's calf rations, that is 4.4 kg DM, or 2.2% of live weight.
  - That agrees with the intakes in §2.

### 4.4 Dairy × Zebu crossbreds, measured (Silva et al. 2021, Brazil)

For Holstein × Gyr steers of about 324 kg: NEm = **0.083 Mcal per kg EBW^0.75 a day**, and NEg (Mcal per kg empty gain) = 0.2973 × EBW^0.4336.

### 4.5 ICAR (2013), India

**Not read.** The one ICAR number surfaced by search was the older ICAR (1998) maintenance figure of 122 kcal ME/kg W^0.75, in a search summary whose source was not opened. It is not used here.

## 5. Compensatory growth

- **Keogh et al. 2016** studied 20 Holstein-Friesian bulls on a 70% concentrate ration.
  - Bulls restricted to **0.6 kg/d for 125 days**, against 1.9 kg/d for bulls fed freely, then gained **2.6 kg/d over the next 55 days**. The bulls fed freely gained 1.3 kg/d over the same 55 days.
  - The weight gap between the groups shrank from 157 kg to 86 kg. That is about 45% of the gap recovered in 55 days (my arithmetic).
- **Hornick et al. 2000** (abstract): at first compensatory growth deposits "very lean tissue… This lasts for some weeks. Then, protein synthesis decreases and high feed intake leads to increased fat deposition."
- **Cabezas-Garcia et al. 2021**, quoting NRC (1996) [SECONDARY]:
  - ME for maintenance "decreases up to 20% in animals experiencing compensatory growth";
  - net energy for gain is reduced by up to 18%.
- **Garcia (Engormix, 2025, extension article):** "Expect the strongest rebound in the first one to two months of refeeding". This is qualitative only.
- **Bangladeshi evidence is indirect.** Rahman 2009's **emaciated** 109 kg bulls gained **0.57–0.80 kg/d** with 10–30% concentrate. Roy 2025's well-kept 112 kg RCC bulls gained 0.55 kg/d. These are different trials and breeds, not a controlled comparison.

**For the app:** a thin, newly bought bull's gain over his first four to eight weeks will read high, and then fall. If the expected gain is set from the first month, he will later look like a slow gainer when he is not. Keogh's rebound lasted the whole 55 days measured; how much longer it would have run is not reported.

- **Slayi & Jaja's percentages do not match their own table.** The abstract says cooling raised ADG "by 18%", but their Table 2 shows 0.88 → 0.98 kg/d, which is 11% (my arithmetic).
- **Every Bangladeshi trial in §1 dewormed its bulls before starting** (Siddque, Mustafa, Hossain, Roy, Sultana). Their gains are for dewormed animals.

## 7. How often to weigh

- **Gut fill.** Weighing 1,018 growing cattle on two consecutive days, "the difference in body weight per animal between both days [was] in the range of −18 to 22 kg with a standard deviation of 5.34 kg… attributed primarily to variation in gut fill" [SECONDARY, Kelly et al. 2019, quoted by MacNeil 2021].
  - MacNeil adds that this "cannot be overcome by weighing the animals multiple times".
  - Weight also changes with the time of day [SECONDARY, Hughes 1976; Heitschmidt 1982].
- **What that noise does to a gain figure (my arithmetic).** One weighing at each end, SD 5.34 kg on the difference, gives an ADG error (one SD) of:
  - 14 days: ±0.38 kg/d;
  - 28 days: ±0.19;
  - 42 days: ±0.13;
  - 56 days: ±0.10;
  - 70 days: ±0.08.

  A deshi bull expected to gain 0.5 kg/d cannot be told from one gaining 0.2 over 14 days. Over 56 days he usually can. Kelly's cattle were 17-month-old growing cattle (breed and weight not given in MacNeil), so the kg noise on a 200 kg deshi may differ. **No source measured it for Bangladeshi cattle.**

- **Standards.**
  - The Beef Improvement Federation has adopted **70 days** as "its de facto standard for measuring ADG in growing cattle", with "bi-weekly weighing… at approximately equally spaced intervals" [SECONDARY, BIF 2021 and Crews & Carstens 2012, via MacNeil 2021].
  - Some work found 42–56 days adequate. A 56-day test was also documented to underestimate ADG [SECONDARY, via MacNeil 2021].
  - Sainz 2019 (abstract): Nelore bulls weighed by hand **every 14 days for 70 days**, ADG from a regression, gave "95% of the animals… se below 0.2 kg/d".
- **Practice at a bull test (University of Tennessee):**
  - "a minimum 14-day adjustment period after delivery";
  - start and end weights "the average of weights taken on… two consecutive days";
  - weights on days 28, 56 and 84.
- **Practice in the Bangladeshi trials:**
  - weighed weekly before the morning feed (Roy 2025; Mustafa 2021, by tape);
  - fortnightly (Sultana 2017);
  - only at start and end (Siddque 2015; Rashid LRRD 2015 is not stated in the part read).

**What follows for OpenFarm** (my reading of the above, not a published rule):

- Compute a gain only from weights **at least 28 days apart**.
- Prefer a straight-line fit through **three or more weights spanning 56 days or more**.
- Weigh **before the morning feed**.
- Do not start the clock until **14 days after arrival**.

## Confidence and gaps

- **Bangladeshi data is thin everywhere and absent at the heavy end.** The whole table rests on about ten trials, most with 3–5 bulls per group and 70–120 days on feed.
  - No deshi bull above about 330 kg was found.
  - No dairy cross above about 405 kg was found.
  - No Brahman cross under 170 kg or above about 420 kg was found.
- **The dairy-cross figure is the least certain.** The two Bangladeshi sources disagree threefold (0.31–0.35 against 1.0–1.17 kg/d), and neither can be shown wrong from its paper.
- **The Brahman-cross figures come from high-concentrate rations** (50–100% concentrate). A farm feeding grass, straw and 1–1.5% concentrate should expect less. How much less is not measured in Bangladesh; Vietnam's Quang 2015 gives 0.59–0.79 kg/d at 0.6–1.2% LW concentrate.
- **Weighing methods vary and one is weak.** Mustafa weighed by heart-girth tape; Siddque weighed only at start and end. None of the trials reports gut-fill control beyond weighing before the morning feed.
- **The weighing-error figures are not Bangladeshi.** Kelly 2019's 5.34 kg day-to-day SD came from 1,018 non-Bangladeshi growing cattle.
- **Standards not read:** NASEM 2016 (only through secondary quotations), ICAR 2013, CSIRO 2007 (only through the Cabezas-Garcia review), Kearl 1982. No DLS, FAO or Merck growth figure for fattening bulls was found.
- **No Bangladeshi slow-gainer rule exists** in anything read. The 80%-of-group rule is from a US bull test.
- **Flagged inconsistencies in read sources:**
  - Mustafa 2021's FCRs cannot be reproduced from its own intake and gain figures;
  - Slayi & Jaja 2025's "18%" ADG gain does not match their own table;
