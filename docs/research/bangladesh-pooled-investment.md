# Bangladesh law on pooling many investors' money into a cattle-fattening project

**Question:** What does Bangladesh law allow and require when a farm takes money from many investors (not only friends and family) to buy and fatten cattle, the farm supplies sheds, staff and utilities, and profit or loss is shared at contract percentages? Which regulator, what licence, what contract form, what tax, which Shariah structure, what records?

**Researched:** 17 September 2026. Primary sources: Acts on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd (Bengali originals read where no official English exists), BSEC rules and orders on sec.gov.bd, NBR's Authentic English Texts of the Income Tax Act 2023 and VAT Act 2012 (hosted on bdlaws), AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards 12 and 13 (aaoifi.com). News is used **only** for what happened in crackdowns and is marked **[SECONDARY]**. English renderings of Bengali sections are mine. **This is research, not legal advice.** Several questions below can only be settled by a Bangladeshi lawyer and a tax adviser, and they are flagged.

**Headline:** No licence exists for "a farm that pools investors' money into cattle". What the law does is draw lines around it, and three of them bite hard:

1. **Promise the money back, with a return, and it is a deposit.** Taking money from the public that is repayable, whether "on demand or otherwise", is banking business and needs a Bangladesh Bank licence (Bank Company Act 1991 ss.5(ত), 31, 109). The penalty is up to 7 years' prison. A fixed-term deposit receipt promising "interest or profit" is finance business (Finance Company Act 2023 ss.2, 4, 58).
2. **Run a platform that collects investment from the public, online _or offline_, and you need Bangladesh Bank approval.** This is the Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2024 s.15(2), with up to 5 years' prison (s.37(1)). In August 2026 Bangladesh Bank cited this exact section against unlicensed apps **[SECONDARY]**.
3. **Go beyond 20 people and the business must be a company.** More than 20 persons carrying on a business for gain must register as a company (Companies Act 1994 s.4(2)). If they do not, every member is personally liable for the business's debts (s.4(4)). A private company may have at most 50 members and may not invite the public to subscribe (s.2(1)(ট)).

Real profit-and-loss sharing, where capital is at risk, returns are a share of actual profit and the investors are a small, identified group, is the ground the law leaves open. BSEC's only regulated pooled-fund route is the Alternative Investment Rules 2015. It is built for fund managers with Tk 5 crore paid-up capital and investors putting in Tk 10 lakh each, and does not fit a farm. No BSEC crowdfunding rules were found in force. The crackdowns of 2020–2026 all crossed the same lines: fixed monthly "profit", principal guaranteed, collection from the general public through agents or apps, referral commissions, and cooperatives used to take deposits.

## 1. What the arrangement is, legally

The same economic deal (investors put in cash, the farm fattens cattle, the proceeds are split) can land in five different legal boxes. Which box it lands in depends on the terms, not the name. The farm must design the terms so that it lands in a box it is allowed to be in.

### 1.1 Deposit-taking: Bank Company Act 1991 (Act 14 of 1991), [bdlaws act-751](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-751.html)

- **s.5(ত), definition of banking business** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-751/section-30708.html)): "ব্যাংক ব্যবসা" means _accepting deposits of money from the public for the purpose of lending or investment, repayable on demand or otherwise, and withdrawable by cheque, draft, order or otherwise_.
- **s.31(1)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-751/section-30813.html)): no **person, institution or company** may carry on banking business in Bangladesh without a Bangladesh Bank licence. The words "person, institution or company" replaced "company" in the 2013 amendment, so the section is not limited to companies.
- **s.109(1)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-751/section-31051.html)): carrying on banking business without a licence is punishable with **up to 7 years' imprisonment and a fine of Tk 5 lakh to Tk 50 lakh**. s.109(8) extends guilt to every director, manager, secretary or officer in charge, unless they prove lack of knowledge or due diligence.
- **s.115** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-751/section-31057.html)): only a bank company, Bangladesh Bank or a notified banking institution may accept deposits withdrawable by cheque.

### 1.2 Term deposits and finance business: Finance Company Act 2023 (Act 59 of 2023), [bdlaws act-1478](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1478.html)

This Act replaced the Financial Institutions Act 1993.

- **s.2(1)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1478/section-53704.html)): "অর্থায়ন ব্যবসা" (finance business) includes _accepting term deposits not repayable on demand_, lending, investment and lease financing.
- **s.2(3)**: "আমানত" (deposit) means _money received through a receipt containing all terms of repayment on the basis of interest or profit_. Excluded from the definition are share capital, borrowing from institutions BB names, and business receipts such as contract security, dealership deposits, earnest money and advances for goods or services.
- **s.4(1)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1478/section-53706.html)): no company may carry on finance business without a BB licence.
- **s.58(1)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1478/section-53760.html)): any **person** who carries on finance business unlicensed faces up to **7 years' imprisonment or a Tk 1 crore fine, or both**.

**Where the line sits.** Both Acts turn on money that is _repayable_: principal promised back, whether on demand or at term, with interest or profit. Taking money as **share capital** is expressly outside "deposit" (FCA s.2(3)(ক)). A contract under which the investor's capital is genuinely at risk, and the return is a share of realised profit that may be zero or negative, is the opposite of that definition. **No source found says in terms that a profit-and-loss-sharing livestock contract is not a deposit.** Recital-level labels ("investment", "partnership") will not help if the paperwork or the sales pitch promises principal back or a fixed return. **Lawyer's opinion needed** on the specific contract.

### 1.3 Investment platforms: Payment and Settlement Systems Act 2024 (Act 9 of 2024, 4 July 2024), [bdlaws act-1496](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1496.html)

- **s.15(2)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1496/section-54115.html)), in my translation: _without approval from Bangladesh Bank, no one may operate any online or offline platform through which any kind of investment is accepted from the public, loans are given, money is held, or financial transactions arise_.
- **s.15(4)–(5)**: BB sets the policy by regulation. **Where the scope is unclear, BB's interpretation applies.**
- **s.37(1)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1496/section-54137.html)): breach of s.15 is an offence punishable with **up to 5 years' imprisonment or a Tk 50 lakh fine, or both**. s.37(6) gives the same penalty to anyone who aids the offence.
- **Enforcement [SECONDARY]:** on 27 August 2026 Bangladesh Bank named 31 unauthorised lending apps. It cited s.15(2) and s.37(1) and directed banks and MFS providers to stop payment services to them (The Daily Star, updated 16 Sep 2026, [link](https://www.thedailystar.net/business/news/banks-mfss-asked-suspend-payment-services-illegal-loan-apps-4274606); Dhaka Tribune Bangla, [link](https://bangla.dhakatribune.com/economy/108431)). Bangladesh Bank's own press-release page (bb.org.bd) blocked automated retrieval, so the primary notice was not read.

The Act does not define "platform" (checked s.2, [section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1496/section-54102.html)). An app or website where members of the public sign up and pay money into cattle projects is squarely what s.15(2) describes. Even an _offline_ scheme of agents and receipts may be caught. **Lawyer's opinion needed** before any OpenFarm feature lets investors onboard or pay in.

### 1.4 Securities and collective investment (BSEC)

**Securities and Exchange Ordinance 1969** ([bdlaws act-355](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-355.html)):

- **s.2(l)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-355/section-20517.html)): "securities" means instruments issued "by or for the benefit of a **company**". The list includes any "stock, transferable share, … note, debenture, … bond, **investment contract** … and, in general, any interest or instrument commonly known as a 'security'; and any certificate of … **participation in**" any of those. **s.2(g)**: "issuer" means any person who has issued or proposes to issue any security. **s.2(j)**: "person" includes a firm and an association of persons.
- **s.2A(2)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-355/section-20518.html)): no issuer or company may, without BSEC consent, (a) make an issue of capital in Bangladesh or (b) make a public offer of securities for sale.
- **BSEC Order, 20 June 2019** (under s.2D; [PDF](https://sec.gov.bd/storage/laws/Order_20.06.2019.pdf)): exempts all issuers except listed ones from s.2A(2)(a) **only "in respect of raising capital through issuance of equity security"**. Profit-sharing investment contracts are not covered by that exemption.

**BSEC (Alternative Investment) Rules 2015**, updated to August 2021 ([PDF](https://sec.gov.bd/storage/laws/Notification_24.05.20221.pdf)). This is the only regulated pooled-fund vehicle found that is short of a mutual fund:

- r.2(2): an AIF must be a **trust**, a "privately pooled investment vehicle" raising money by **private placement only**, closed-end with a fixed tenure, and a type of collective investment scheme.
- r.2(12): **eligible investors** are institutions, foreign individuals, or "high net worth" individuals. r.2(16): an HNI has net worth of at least Tk 2 crore, certified by the tax authority.
- r.3: the **fund manager** must be registered with BSEC and have paid-up capital of at least Tk 5 crore.
- r.8(5): the **corpus** must be at least Tk 10 crore, with sponsors subscribing ≥10% and the fund manager investing ≥2%.
- r.17: **minimum subscription Tk 10 lakh per investor** (amended 2020; the original 2015 text on BSEC's law-book page says Tk 50 lakh, [PDF](https://sec.gov.bd/storage/laws/11_2026_08_17_043216.pdf)). Offers go only through a **placement memorandum**, and "no public offer shall be allowed". **No more than 200 investors.**

**Crowdfunding:** BSEC's current list of acts, rules and regulations ([page](https://sec.gov.bd/act-rules-and-regulations)), its law book ([page](https://sec.gov.bd/law-book)) and its draft rules ([page](https://sec.gov.bd/draft-rules-and-regulation)) were searched on 17 Sep 2026 for "crowd", "ক্রাউড" and "Alternative". None lists crowdfunding rules. A law-firm blog claims 2021 "guidelines" exist, but no primary text was found. **Treat crowdfunding rules as not in force.**

**Reading.** If the farm (or a project vehicle) is a **company** and sells investors a right to share in project profit, that right fits the words "investment contract … participation in" profit. Its issue would then need BSEC consent, which in practice points to the AIF regime and puts the farm out of reach. If the farm is a sole proprietor or a partnership, s.2(l)'s "company" wording suggests the Ordinance does not reach it, but "issuer" means any person. **Lawyer's opinion needed.**

### 1.5 Partnership: Partnership Act 1932 (Act IX of 1932), [bdlaws act-157](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-157.html)

- **s.4** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-5507.html)): partnership is "the relation between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all".
- **s.6** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-5509.html)): the "real relation" governs. Receiving a share of profits "does not of itself make him a partner". Examples are a lender, or a servant or agent paid by profit share.
- **s.9** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-5522.html)): partners must "render true accounts and full information of all things affecting the firm to any partner". **s.12(d)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-5525.html)): every partner may "access … inspect and copy any of the books".
- **s.13** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-5526.html)), default rules that the contract can change: no pay for working partners; profits and losses shared equally; interest on capital only out of profits; each partner indemnifies the firm for loss from "wilful neglect".
- **s.25** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-5539.html)): every partner is liable jointly and severally for acts of the firm. **Investors who are partners share unlimited liability for the farm's debts.**
- **s.58** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-5600.html)): registration "may be effected at any time" with the Registrar. RJSC (যৌথমূলধন কোম্পানি ও ফার্মসমূহের পরিদপ্তর, [roc.gov.bd](https://roc.gov.bd/)) is the Registrar of Firms. The statement lists every partner's name, address and joining date and is signed by all partners.
- **s.69** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-157/section-25278.html)): if the firm is **unregistered**, no partner can sue the firm or co-partners on the contract, and the firm cannot sue third parties. Suits for dissolution and accounts are still allowed.

### 1.6 The 20-person ceiling: Companies Act 1994 (Act 18 of 1994), [bdlaws act-788](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788.html)

- **s.4(2)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32008.html)): unless registered as a company or formed under another law, no company, association or **partnership of more than twenty persons** may be formed to carry on a business for gain. s.4(1) sets the limit at ten for banking business.
- **s.4(4)**: if such a body carries on business in breach, **every member is personally liable** for its debts. **s.4(5)**: every member is also liable to a fine of up to Tk 5,000.
- **s.2(1)(ট)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32006.html)): a **private company** must restrict share transfers, **prohibit any invitation to the public** to subscribe for its shares or debentures, and have **no more than 50 members** (excluding employees).
- **s.181(5)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-33570.html)): see §6.

Whether investors who sign separate mudarabah contracts with the farm count as "persons" in one association or partnership under s.4(2) is **not answered by any source found → lawyer**.

### 1.7 Not the right box, but check

- **MRA:** the Microcredit Regulatory Authority Act 2006 ([bdlaws act-947](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-947.html)) s.15 requires an MRA certificate for **microcredit** activity, meaning loans for poverty alleviation and small enterprise (s.2(22)). A farm that takes investment and does not lend is outside it.
- **MLM:** under the Multi-Level Marketing Activities (Control) Act 2013 ([bdlaws act-1128](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1128.html)), s.15 bans "pyramid-like" selling, where people pay in advance expecting commission from recruiting more buyers. It also bans selling "intangible or illusory" products, or products that will only become marketable in future through a time-based process. The penalty under s.26 is **5–10 years' imprisonment and up to Tk 50 lakh fine**. Any **referral commission** to investors who bring other investors steers towards this Act.

**The pattern.** Every case promised a **fixed or minimum return**, usually monthly and often with **capital doubling or guaranteed**. Money came from the **general public** through **commission agents, offices, apps or religious networks**. A **cooperative or a "partnership" label** stood in for a licence. And nothing the investor could check (audited accounts, the actual animals or assets, bank-held funds) sat behind the promise. Where cases went to court, reports mention money-laundering and misappropriation charges, not failure to hold a particular investment licence. The sources are silent on the exact sections charged.

## 3. What form the contract must take

### 3.1 Stamp duty: Stamp Act 1899 (Act II of 1899), [bdlaws act-77](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77.html)

- **s.17** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22716.html)): instruments executed in Bangladesh "shall be stamped before or at the time of execution".
- **s.35** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22775.html)): an unstamped or under-stamped instrument cannot be admitted in evidence or acted on. It can be admitted on payment of the duty plus a penalty of Tk 5 or **ten times the deficient duty**, whichever is more.
- **Schedule I**, as substituted by Finance Act 2022 w.e.f. 1 July 2022 ([PDF on bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/upload/act/2022-07-18-11-25-48-77___Schedule.pdf)):
  - **Art. 45, Partnership, A: Instrument of**: **Tk 2,000** where capital does not exceed Tk 1,00,000; **Tk 4,000** in any other case. B: Dissolution of partnership, Tk 2,000.
  - **Art. 5(c), Agreement or memorandum of agreement "not otherwise provided for"**: **Tk 300**.
  - **Art. 24, Counterpart or duplicate**: same duty as the original where that duty is ≤ Tk 10,000.
  - **Art. 41, Notarial act**: **Tk 300**.
- Which article a **mudarabah or investment agreement** falls under (Art. 45 partnership or Art. 5(c)) is **not settled by any source found → lawyer**. So is whether each investor's separate contract is its own instrument. The schedule on bdlaws dates from 2022; **check whether Finance Act 2023–2026 changed these amounts.**

### 3.2 Notarisation and registration

- **Notarisation:** no provision found in the Partnership Act, Contract Act-related sources or the Stamp Act that requires a partnership or investment agreement to be notarised. If done, Art. 41 duty applies.
- **Registration of the firm:** optional (s.58), but see s.69 (§1.5). AAOIFI SS 12 3/1/1/1 says a Sharikah contract "should, if necessary, be documented and registered officially".
- **Company route:** incorporation at RJSC under the Companies Act 1994.

### 3.3 What the investor must be given

- **No general statutory disclosure document** was found for a partnership or a private profit-sharing contract.
- **Partnership:** true accounts and full information (s.9); access to inspect and copy the books (s.12(d)).
- **AIF:** a placement memorandum and the constitutive documents (AIF Rules r.17(5)–(6)).
- **If tax is withheld:** a certificate of tax deduction showing the payee's TIN, amount, section and treasury deposit particulars (ITA 2023 s.145, see §4.2).
- **AAOIFI SS 13 3/2** (a Shariah requirement, not law): the memorandum of understanding should state the profit ratio and the guarantees the mudarib gives against negligence, misconduct or breach.

## 4. Tax

### 4.1 The farm's own income: Income Tax Act 2023 (Act XII of 2023)

Authentic English Text, SRO 404-Law/2025 of 8 Oct 2025, incorporating Finance Act 2024 amendments ([PDF](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/upload/act/2026-01-18-15-24-07-123.-Income-tax-Act,-2023.pdf); [NBR copy](https://nbr.gov.bd/uploads/acts/Income_tax_act_2023.pdf)).

- **s.40(1),(3):** income from activities related to agriculture is "income from agriculture", and agriculture includes "**animal husbandry**", "poultry and fish farms", and "egg and milk production".
- **s.42:** allowable deductions include expenses "relating to rearing of cattle".
- **s.43:** if books are not kept or cannot be verified, 60% of the market value of the produce is deemed expenditure. **s.43(2)** says this does not apply where income is derived "through adhi, barga, bhaga or share-basis". The Act recognises traditional share-rearing, but only for this deemed-expense rule.
- **Rates and exemptions** are not in the Act. They come from the annual Finance Act and SROs (s.76). The reduced livestock and fisheries rates (0% on the first Tk 10 lakh, then stepped) were reported as withdrawn or reshaped by the Finance Ordinance 2025 from 1 July 2025 **[SECONDARY: [Bonik Barta, 23 Apr 2025](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/sg2zhD3RJ1MdwHcp); ACNABIN summary of Finance Ordinance 2025 ([PDF](https://bti-global.files.svdcdn.com/production/network/bangladesh/documents/Reports/Summary-of-Important-Changes-Introduced-by-the-Finance-Ordinance-2025-of-BD-ACNABIN.pdf?dm=1749037656))]**. **Not verified against the Ordinance text. - **If the vehicle is a firm or association of persons:**
  - s.55(b): interest, salary, commission or remuneration paid **by a firm to its partners is not deductible**.
  - s.80: a partner's share of firm income is taxed at the partner's average rate.
  - s.2(58): "firm" means a firm under the Partnership Act 1932.
  - s.52: interest or "share of profit" on capital borrowed for the business is deductible.
  - **s.73:** any person other than an individual (a firm, AOP or company) must file with its return an income statement and financial statement **certified by a Chartered Accountant** as IAS/IFRS-compliant.

### 4.2 Withholding on what is paid to investors

- **A firm, AOP, company or trust is a "specified person"** (s.140(3)(a)) and so carries the withholding duties that apply to specified persons. A sole-proprietor farmer is not a specified person.
- **s.102** (substituted by Finance Act 2024): banks, finance companies, cooperatives and MFS providers, "**or any person responsible for paying any sum by way of interest or share of profit on deposit to a resident**", must deduct tax when the sum is credited or paid. The rate is **10% for individuals** and 20% for a trust, AOP or company.
- **s.104:** a specified person paying **interest on a loan** (other than to a bank or finance company) deducts **10%**.
- **s.143:** failure to deduct makes the payer an "assessee-in-default", liable for the undeducted tax and more. **s.145:** the payer must give a deduction certificate. **s.146:** deducted tax must be deposited to the Government.
- **No withholding section was found for a partner's share of firm profit or a mudarib's distribution of profit to rab-al-mal investors** where the arrangement is neither a deposit nor a loan. Characterisation matters twice here. If the contract reads as a deposit, it is both a **banking-law problem** (§1.1) and a **s.102 withholding case**. **Tax adviser needed** before the first payout.

### 4.3 VAT: VAT and Supplementary Duty Act 2012 (Act 47 of 2012)

Authentic English Text, with amendments to the Finance Ordinance 2025 ([PDF](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/upload/act/2026-01-18-15-14-21-72.-Value-Added-Tax-and-Supplementary-Duty-Act,-2012.pdf); Bengali [act-1106](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106.html)).

- **s.26** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1106/section-42320.html)): supplies listed in the First Schedule are exempt.
- **First Schedule Part One**, heading **01.02 "Live bovine animals"**: **exempt goods**. Selling the fattened cattle is an exempt supply. "Milk and cream, not condensed…" is also listed.
- **First Schedule Part Two** (exempt services): agricultural services cover land preparation, irrigation, pesticide work, storage of agricultural goods, and preservation and warehousing of meat. **Rearing or managing someone else's livestock for a fee is not listed.** Nor is investment management. The only finance-related exemptions are deposit-taking by banks and financial institutions, life insurance, stock exchanges, and share purchase and sale.

If it takes only a **share of profit as mudarib or partner**, whether that is "consideration" for a supply was **not answered by any source found → tax adviser**.

## 5. Shariah structures: AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards 12 and 13

Official AAOIFI PDFs: **SS 12 Sharikah (Musharakah) and Modern Corporations** ([PDF](https://aaoifi.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/SS-12-Sharikah-Musharakah-and-Modern-Corporations.pdf); issued 16 May 2002) and **SS 13 Mudarabah** ([PDF](https://aaoifi.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/SS-13-Mudarabah.pdf); issued May 2002). AAOIFI standards have no force of law in Bangladesh. They are what Shariah-minded investors and scholars will measure the contract against. AAOIFI has since circulated an **unofficial** draft English translation of Standards 1–61 for comment (comments due 30 June 2026) and says the official version governs ([announcement](https://aaoifi.com/announcement/draft-english-translation-of-aaoifi-shariah-standards-1-61/?lang=en)).

| --- | --- | --- |
| Who brings what | Investors (rab al-mal) bring capital; the farm (mudarib) brings work and expertise (SS 13 §2) | Every partner brings capital (SS 12 §3/1). Cash in principle; tangible assets allowed once valued in currency by agreement (3/1/2/1) |
| Profit | An agreed **percentage of actual profit**, fixed at contract. Never a lump sum or a % of capital; a lump-sum stipulation voids the contract (8/1, 8/3, 8/5). Ratio may be changed by agreement (8/3). A ceiling is allowed: profit above X goes to one party (8/5) | An undivided **percentage of profit**, not a sum or % of capital (3/1/5/1), fixed at contract (3/1/5/2). **May differ from capital shares, but extra share may not go to a sleeping partner** (3/1/5/3). | Manager liability | A trustee. Liable for losses **only for misconduct, negligence or breach of contract**, and then "for the amount of the Mudarabah capital" (4/4) | Every partner holds assets on trust; liable only for misconduct, negligence or breach (3/1/4/1) |
| Guarantees | Investors may take adequate guarantees from the mudarib, **enforceable only for misconduct, negligence or breach** (6). | Manager's pay | **No fee on top of a profit share** (8/2). Work not customarily the mudarib's may be hired at market rate against the pool (9/4). **Mapped to this farm.** The farm contributes sheds, staff and utilities. Under **mudarabah**, staff time and ordinary shed and utility use look like "customary work" of the mudarib under SS 13 9/4. **They would be the farm's cost, repaid through its profit share, not charged to the pool.** Feed, medicine and cattle purchases are project costs. Under **musharakah**, the farm could bring sheds (or their use) as valued capital in kind (SS 12 3/1/2/1) and so bear loss in proportion. Or it could take an independent, terminable management contract with a fee (3/1/3/5), which then raises the VAT question in §4.3. **A Shariah scholar should confirm the treatment of shed use and staff.**

## What this means for the design

### Constraints the contract and the software must respect

1. **No promise of principal back, and no fixed or minimum return. Anywhere.** Not in the contract, the dashboard, a WhatsApp message or a projection shown as a promise. Returns are a **percentage of realised project profit**. A loss reduces the investors' capital. (BCA 1991 s.5(ত)/31/109; FCA 2023 s.2(3)/58; SS 12 3/1/5/1; SS 13 8/1, 8/5, 8/7.) The software must be able to show a **negative** result per investor.
2. **Profit shares are percentages fixed at signing**, stored per contract. Changes must be recorded as dated amendments (SS 12 3/1/5/2; SS 13 8/3). Projections must be labelled as estimates and kept apart from contract terms.
3. **No distribution until capital is intact.** Losses carry forward within a project. Final settlement happens on sale of the animals (actual valuation) or at fair value. Interim payouts are "on account" and must be reversible in the ledger (SS 13 8/7–8/8; SS 12 3/1/5/6, 3/1/5/9).
4. **Keep project money separate and traceable.** Record every inflow and outflow per project, and the farm's own money separately if it co-invests (SS 13 8/9). Receive and pay investors through **bank channels**, not cash through agents (crackdown pattern, §2).
5. **Record what the farm contributes and how it is paid for**, and keep the split rules stable. That covers sheds, staff and utilities, and whether they are mudarib cost, capital in kind, or a separate fee under a separate contract (SS 13 8/2, 9/4; SS 12 3/1/3/3–5). Do not charge both a fee and a profit share in one mudarabah.
6. **Track investor head-count per project and per legal vehicle.** Warn before a single business goes past **20 persons** (Companies Act s.4(2)). If the vehicle is a private company, warn at **50 members** (s.2(1)(ট)).
7. **No public solicitation or onboarding in the software.** No sign-up page for strangers, no "invest now" button, no payment collection, no referral or commission scheme. PSSA 2024 s.15(2)/37 covers "online or offline platform[s]" taking investment from the public, and MLM Act s.15/26 covers recruitment commissions. **OpenFarm should be an internal ledger and a read-only report for already-contracted investors until a lawyer clears more.**
8. **Contract documents:** store the signed, stamped instrument (stamp value, date and serial) against each contract. Refuse to mark a contract "active" without it (Stamp Act ss.17, 35).
9. **Investor reporting:** give each investor true accounts of their project, meaning cost of animals, feed, medicine, deaths, sale proceeds, farm contribution and profit split (Partnership Act s.9, s.12(d)). Also record any tax withheld and issue the s.145 certificate if withholding applies.
10. **Retention:** keep project, investor, payment and tax records for **at least 12 years** (the longest applicable period, Companies Act s.181(5)). That also covers VAT's 5 years and income tax's 6-assessment-year reach. Never hard-delete contract or payment history.
11. **Negligence and misconduct is the manager's only liability.** Keep the evidence that the farm was not negligent: mortality and treatment records and vet visits, which the regulatory note already covers (SS 13 4/4, 6; Partnership Act s.13(f)).

### Choices still open

- **Legal vehicle:** (a) the Owner as sole mudarib contracting with each investor separately; (b) a registered partnership firm (≤20 persons); (c) a private limited company per project or for all projects (≤50 members, no public invitation, 12-year books, CA-certified accounts).
- **Structure:** mudarabah (investors bear capital loss; the farm's contribution is effort and its customary costs) or musharakah (farm puts in valued sheds or cash and bears loss pro rata), or musharakah plus a separate management agreement.
- **Who counts as an investor:** friends, family and known people only, or wider. The law's 20/50 caps and PSSA s.15(2) push towards a closed, known circle.
- **Profit split and "ceiling" clause** (SS 12 3/1/5/8; SS 13 8/5), for example the farm takes all profit above a stated rate.
- **Settlement basis:** actual sale only, or fair-value valuation at a cut-off date.
- **Whether the Owner invests alongside** (mixing rules, SS 13 8/9).
- **Whether investors ever see the software directly** (a read-only portal) or only receive statements. The first edges closer to a "platform".

### Get a lawyer's opinion before proceeding

1. Whether the chosen contract is a **deposit** under BCA 1991 s.5(ত) and FCA 2023 s.2(3).
2. Whether any investor-facing software, or even an offline agent network, is a **"platform" needing BB approval** under PSSA 2024 s.15(2).
3. Whether a profit-share right is an **"investment contract"/security** under the 1969 Ordinance s.2(l), especially if a company is used, and so needs BSEC consent under s.2A.
4. Whether separate mudarabah investors count towards the **20-person limit** in Companies Act s.4(2), and whether investors risk **partner liability** under Partnership Act s.25.
5. **Stamp duty article** (Art. 45 or Art. 5(c)) and the current Schedule I amounts.
6. **Tax adviser:** withholding on profit distributions (ITA ss.102/104 or none), VAT on any management fee, and the FY2026-27 tax treatment of livestock income after Finance Ordinance 2025.
7. **Shariah scholar:** treatment of sheds, staff and utilities under SS 13 9/4, or as capital in kind under SS 12 3/1/2/1.
