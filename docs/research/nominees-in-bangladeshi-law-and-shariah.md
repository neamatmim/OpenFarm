# What a nominee is in Bangladeshi law and in Shariah

**Question:** An Investor in a Venture (a mudarabah, the Owner as mudarib) names a nominee on the Investment Agreement. Before OpenFarm lets them name several, what is a nominee in Bangladesh? Is the nominee a collector who hands the money on to the heirs under faraid, or a beneficiary who takes in their own right? What governs a nominee in a private contract? What does Shariah say? And how do banks, insurers, CDBL and the savings certificates handle more than one nominee? (Ticket: `.scratch/openfarm-several-nominees/issues/01-what-a-nominee-is-in-bangladeshi-law-and-in-shariah.md`.)

**Researched:** 26 September 2026. Primary sources: the Acts on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, read in the Bengali original where there is no English text; the judgments on supremecourt.gov.bd; the CDBL Bye-Laws, Depository (User) Regulations 2003 and CDBL forms, read from the Internet Archive's copies because the current cdbl.com.bd no longer serves them; the Family Savings Certificate Policy 2009 as published by the Internal Resources Division, with its gazetted form; banks' and an insurer's own current forms; and AAOIFI Shari'ah Standards 13 and 26 from aaoifi.com. bb.org.bd refused automated access, so **no Bangladesh Bank circular was read in the original**. The circulars below come from news reports and are marked **[SECONDARY]**. English renderings of Bengali text are mine. **This is research, not legal advice.** The questions a lawyer or a Shariah scholar must settle are flagged.

## Answer for the ticket

1. **In Bangladesh a nominee is a collector, not an owner.** The statutes do two things. They tell the payer (bank, insurer, depository, savings office) whom to pay, and they discharge the payer once it has paid. The only reported judgment that decides the question holds that the nominee "does not become absolute owner" and holds the money "in trust" for the heirs under Muslim law. That judgment is **Md. Monjurul Hoque Chowdhury v. Bilkis Ara Begum**, High Court Division, Civil Revision 1682 of 2015, decided 3 April 2016. It read Bank Company Act 1991 s.103 the same way, because s.103(4)'s proviso keeps every other person's claim against the person paid. Two things push the other way. News reports say the Appellate Division stayed that judgment and has not decided the appeal. And Bangladesh Bank's 2017 circulars tell banks to pay the nominee. Neither says the nominee keeps the money **[SECONDARY]**. The settled part is the payer's discharge. Who ends up owning the money is contested, and the only reasoned answer favours the heirs.
2. **Nothing governs a nominee in a private mudarabah except the contract.** Section 103 binds only bank companies, s.61 of the Finance Company Act 2023 binds only finance companies, and no Act covers a farm's Venture. The contract cannot turn a nominee into an owner as against the heirs. A term that gives the nominee the money in their own right, taking effect on the Investor's death, is a bequest (wasiyyah) under Muslim law. A bequest is valid only up to one third of the estate, and a bequest to an heir needs the other heirs' consent. No statute here says "notwithstanding any other law" for a private contract, so the heirs can recover from the nominee.
3. **Shariah agrees with the collector reading.** The shares of the heirs are fixed (Qur'an 4:11–12). A bequest is capped at one third (Bukhari 2742), and there is "no bequest to an heir" (Abu Dawud 2870; Tirmidhi 2120). AAOIFI has no standard on nomination or on wills. Its nearest rule is SS 26 (Islamic Insurance, issued 2 May 2005) 6/2/1/4: a participant's **investment balances** "should be distributed among the inheritors according to the Islamic rules of inheritance." An Investor's capital and profit share in a Venture are exactly that kind of money. Read this way, the nominee is an _amin_ (trustee) or agent who collects and hands on. They are not a _musa lahu_ (legatee).
4. **OpenFarm's own words already take the collector side, but the glossary does not.** The standard Agreement says "their capital and share are paid through their nominee to their lawful heirs". The nominee line says the farm holds the nominee's details "only to pay the Investor's heirs". CONTEXT.md says the nominee is the person named "to receive their capital and share", which reads as a beneficiary. **Fix the glossary to the collector meaning** before modelling several nominees, or shares will be read as inheritance.
5. **With several nominees, a share decides who collects which part. It does not decide who inherits.** Under the collector reading a percentage split changes nothing about the heirs' entitlement. It only decides which nominee must account for which part. Banks, CDBL and insurers still ask for a percentage, and the savings certificate asks for a taka amount. Families will likely read a percentage as a statement of who inherits. The grilling must choose one of two designs. **(a)** Several nominees who collect the whole together or singly. **(b)** A share per nominee as the market does, with words that say the share is not inheritance.
6. **What the market allows.** Every statute says "one or more" with no cap: BCA s.103 since the 2013 amendment, Insurance Act 2010 s.57, FCA 2023 s.61, the Government Savings Banks Act 1873 s.4 and the Sanchayapatra Rules 1977 r.14. The forms cap the count:
   - CDBL Form 23 (revision 03/08/2004): **2 nominees**, each with a **percentage**. The older Bye-Laws Form 02 had three.
   - Family Savings Certificate form (gazette, 22 November 2009): **2 nominees**, each with a **taka amount**.
   - MetLife Bangladesh beneficiary form: **3 rows**, each with a **share %**.
   - IFIC Bank form (2023): one nominee block with a **Percentage** field.

   **No form or statute has an order, or a nominee who takes only if another fails.** Nominees are concurrent. A nominee who dies before the holder simply drops out (GSB Act s.4(3); Sanchayapatra Rules r.14(2); Family Savings Policy para 10(5)). If every nominee is dead, the money goes to the heirs (Insurance Act s.57(5)).

7. **Minors.** Every statute lets the holder name **the person who receives for a nominee who is still a minor** (BCA s.103(2); Insurance Act s.57(1) proviso; FCA s.61(2)). CDBL records that guardian's name, relationship to the nominee, the minor's date of birth and the **maturity date of minor**. OpenFarm's minor line today only records a guardian's consent to holding the data. It names no one to receive. **If nominees can be minors, "who receives for this minor" is a field of its own.**
8. **Take to the lawyer** (the meeting already pending):
   - Does paying the nominee(s) under a private contract give the farm a good discharge against the heirs? Or should the farm, like the savings and CDBL rules, pay above a threshold only against a succession certificate? The Government Savings Banks Act 1873 threshold is Tk 25,000. CDBL's Board may waive representation up to Tk 1 lakh.
   - The wording for several nominees and for a minor's receiver.
   - Whether a term paying the nominee in their own right would be void or only unenforceable against the heirs (Contract Act s.23).

   **Take to the Shariah scholar:** confirm the amin/collector reading. Confirm that naming an heir as nominee is unobjectionable when the nominee only collects.

## 1. What the statutes say

### 1.1 Bank Company Act 1991, s.103 ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-751/section-31045.html))

Heading: _আমানতী অর্থ পরিশোধের জন্য মনোনয়ন দান_ (nomination for payment of deposit money). As amended by the Bank Company (Amendment) Act 2013 (Act 27 of 2013) s.68. bdlaws' text also carries the 2023 amendment (Act 13 of 2023) elsewhere in the Act, so this is the current text; checked 26 September 2026.

- **(1)** A sole depositor, or joint depositors jointly, may in the prescribed manner nominate **"one or more"** persons (_একজন বা একাধিক_, substituted for "one" in 2013) to whom the deposit may be paid after the death of the sole depositor or of all the joint depositors. The proviso lets them cancel the nomination at any time and nominate "another person or persons".
- **(2)** If a nominee is a minor, the depositor may specify in the prescribed manner **who will receive the money** if the depositor dies while the nominee is still a minor.
- **(3)** "Notwithstanding anything in any other law for the time being in force, or in any will or other instrument disposing of property", the person nominated or specified **acquires all the rights of the depositor** in respect of the deposit after the death of the depositor(s), "**and every other person shall be deprived of those rights**."
- **(4)** Payment under this section **discharges all the bank's liability** for the deposit. **Proviso:** "if any other person has any right or claim against the person to whom the deposit money has been paid under this section, nothing in this sub-section shall affect it."

On its face (3) is the strongest pro-nominee wording in Bangladeshi law: a non-obstante clause that names wills. The proviso to (4) cuts back. The High Court Division read the two together to mean the bank pays the nominee and the heirs claim against the nominee (§2.1).

### 1.2 Finance Company Act 2023, s.61 ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1478.html))

Heading: _আমানতকারীর প্রতিনিধি মনোনয়ন সংক্রান্ত বিধান_. The provision is shorter than s.103.

- (1) The deposit **"may be paid"** after the death of the depositor(s) to the "person or persons" they nominated. The nomination can be cancelled and replaced at any time.
- (2) The depositor may specify who receives if a nominee is still a minor.
- (3) Bangladesh Bank may give directions on nomination and deposit claims.

**There is no "every other person deprived" clause and no "notwithstanding any other law".** It is a payment instruction only.

### 1.3 Insurance Act 2010, ss.57 and 86 ([bdlaws print](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1037.html))

Section 57, _বীমা পলিসি গ্রাহক কর্তৃক মনোনয়ন_ (nomination by the policyholder):

- **(1)** A policyholder on their own life may nominate **"one or more persons"** (_এক বা একাধিক ব্যক্তিকে_) to receive the sum assured on their death. **Proviso:** if a nominee is a minor, the policyholder may lawfully appoint a person to receive the money if the policyholder dies during the minority.
- **(2)** To take effect, the nomination must be in or endorsed on the policy and notified to and registered by the insurer. It may be cancelled or changed before maturity by endorsement or **by will**. Until the insurer has notice of a change, it "shall not be liable" if it pays the registered nominee in good faith.
- **(3)** The insurer acknowledges registration in writing and may charge a fee.
- **(4)** Assignment of the policy cancels the nomination, with exceptions for loans against the policy.
- **(5)** If the policy matures in the holder's lifetime, or **all the nominees die before it matures**, the sum is payable to the holder, "or their heir or lawful representative or holder of a succession certificate".
- **(6)** "In the case of a nominee or several nominees, if the nominees are alive at the policyholder's death, the sum assured shall be payable to **such surviving person or persons**."
- **(7)** The section does not apply to a policy under the Married Women's Property Act 1874.

Section 86 gives an insured person under a **group** life policy the same power to nominate "one or more persons", with a minor's receiver set by regulation.

**What the Act does not say:** that the nominee takes beneficially. There is no "others deprived" clause. **No Bangladeshi judgment on s.57 was found.** For comparison, the Supreme Court of India read the similar s.39 of the Insurance Act 1938 as making the nominee a collector (Sarbati Devi v. Usha Devi (1984) 1 SCC 424; persuasive only, not read here).

### 1.4 Companies Act 1994 ([bdlaws print](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-788.html))

- **No general nomination for shareholders.** Section 40 lets a deceased member's legal representative transfer their shares. Otherwise the Act's uses of মনোনীত are about directors and nominee holders.
- **One Person Company, s.392খ(3)–(8)** (Part X-A, inserted by the Companies (Second Amendment) Act 2020, Act 24 of 2020, s.9). The memorandum must name **one** nominee, with their written consent, who "**shall become the shareholder**" if the sole shareholder dies or becomes incapable. The nominee may withdraw consent, and the shareholder may change the nominee. This is a succession-to-management device, and it is singular.

### 1.5 CDBL: the depository

Sources are the **Depository (User) Regulations 2003**, the **CDBL Bye-Laws** in force from 16 July 2003, the **BO Account Terms & Conditions** (Bye-Law 7.3.3(c)), and **Forms 02, 19 and 23**. All were read from the Internet Archive's copies of cdbl.com.bd (captured 2004–2010). **The current cdbl.com.bd serves none of these files.** A broker's current kit (Oshadhi Securities) still uses Form 23 unchanged, which suggests the form is still in use.

- **Regulations 2003, reg. 42(7):** "On the death of any Account Holder … his **legal heir** can apply … for transmission of the securities by filling up the form specified in the Bye-Laws."
- **Bye-Law 11.8.1:** every account holder may nominate "**a person** who shall be entitled, upon the death of the Account Holder, to **succeed to** the Securities". Joint holders nominate jointly, and may revoke or substitute at any time. The bye-law says "a person", singular, but the forms take two or three.
- **11.8.3:** in a joint account the securities vest in the **surviving holders**, "notwithstanding any nomination".
- **11.8.4:** for a sole holder the securities "**shall vest in the nominee**". Where there is no nomination, succession is "determined in accordance with law in favour of the heirs … against production of … Probate, Letters of Administration or Succession Certificate". The proviso: up to **Tk 1,00,000** market value, CDBL's Board may permit transmission to the heirs without representation.
- **BO Account T&C cl.6** (form revision 03/08/2004):
  - (a) on death "the nominee shall receive/draw the securities";
  - (b) if the nominee is a minor, "the **legal guardian** is authorised to receive/draw";
  - (c) "The nominee so authorised, shall be entitled to all my/our account **to the exclusion of all other persons i.e., my/our heirs, executors and administrators** … and delivery of securities to the nominee … shall be binding on all other persons."
- **Form 19 (Transmission Request Form), CDBL certification:** "Securities standing to the credit of a deceased Account Holder shall vest in his/her **nominee/s** …".

This is the most beneficiary-sounding regime in Bangladesh. It rests on bye-laws and a signed account contract, not on a statute with a non-obstante clause. **No Bangladeshi judgment on CDBL nominees was found.** India has near-identical depository bye-laws ("vest in the nominee"). Its Supreme Court held that nomination under the Companies Act 1956 and the depository bye-laws **does not override succession law**, and that the nominee holds for the heirs (**Shakti Yezdani v. Jayanand Jayant Salgaonkar**, Civil Appeal 7107 of 2017, 14 December 2023; persuasive only; read through [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/166607072/)'s listing, not in full).

### 1.6 Savings certificates and Post Office savings

- **Government Savings Banks Act 1873, s.4** ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-28/section-506.html)):
  - (1) A depositor may nominate "**any person or persons**" to receive "**the whole or any part**" of the deposit on death.
  - (2) The nominees, "notwithstanding anything contained in any other law … or in any disposition, whether testamentary or otherwise", are "entitled, **to the exclusion of all other persons, to receive**" it.
  - (3) A nominee who dies before the depositor, or before receiving the money, drops out; the nomination "shall cease to have any effect".
  - (4) The depositor may vary or cancel the nomination at any time.
  - (5) Without a nomination, up to **Tk 25,000** may be paid without probate or a succession certificate after three months.

  "Entitled … to receive" is a right to be paid, not a grant of title.

- **Post Office National Savings Certificates Ordinance 1944, ss.4–5** ([bdlaws print](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-212.html)): applies the GSB Act ss.4–9 to certificates. For a certificate held for a **minor**, payment goes to a parent or guardian nominated in the application, or else to a court-appointed guardian, or else to either parent.
- **Sanchayapatra Rules 1977**, as amended to 30 June 2015. Quoted in full in the 2016 judgment (§2.1); the Rules themselves were not found online.
  - **r.14(1):** nomination "may be made by the purchaser(s) … specifying **the amount, whether whole or in part**, receivable by the nominee".
  - **r.14(2):** it "shall cease to have effect in case the nominee dies before the death of the holder".
  - **r.14A** (inserted 20 August 2008): "'Nominee' means an individual or **group of individuals**, and includes any company or association".
  - **r.15:** the holder may vary or cancel.
  - **r.16:** where there is no nominee, up to Tk 25,000 is paid without representation.
  - **r.17:** "It shall be open to a nominee … to receive the amount due to him on the death of the holder either immediately or on maturity."
- **Family Savings Certificate Policy 2009** (পরিবার সঞ্চয়পত্র নীতিমালা, ২০০৯, amended to 2015; [IRD PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-ird/2024/12/b5bdbf7c57ae4f09a6ae808167f91865.pdf)), para 10 _মনোনয়ন_:
  - (1) The investor may nominate "**any one or more persons**", effective on the investor's death.
  - (2) The nomination may be for **all or part** of the investment.
  - (3) It may be made after purchase.
  - (4) It may be changed or cancelled at any time.
  - (5) It lapses as to a nominee who dies first.
  - (6) **Without a proper nomination, the heir or heirs take "under the personal law applicable to them".**
  - (7) After the death, "**the nominee or heir**" may encash or keep drawing profit to maturity.

  The same line (the nominee may encash at once or keep drawing monthly profit) is repeated on the National Savings Directorate's current page for the certificate ([nationalsavings.gov.bd](https://nationalsavings.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922df2e933eb65569e20660), read 26 September 2026).

### 1.7 The general law the nominee sits under

- **Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act 1937, s.2** ([bdlaws print](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-173.html)): "Notwithstanding any custom or usage to the contrary, in all questions (save questions relating to agricultural land) regarding **intestate succession** … **gifts, trusts and trust properties** … the rule of decision in cases where the parties are Muslims shall be the Muslim Personal Law (Shariat)." Wills are added only for a person who files a declaration under s.3.
- **Succession Act 1925, s.58(1)** ([bdlaws, Part VI](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-138/part-details-153.html)): the Act's testamentary Part "shall not apply to testamentary succession to the property of any Muslim". A Muslim's will is therefore governed by Muslim law, including the one-third rule and the rule against a bequest to an heir (§4).
- **Contract Act 1872, s.37** ([bdlaws print](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-26.html)): "Promises bind the representatives of the promisors in case of the death of such promisors before performance, unless a contrary intention appears from the contract." **s.201:** an agency ends on the death of the principal. A nominee acting as the Investor's agent has no authority after the death, so any authority they have must come from the contract or from the heirs.
- **Partnership Act 1932, s.42(c)** ([bdlaws print](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-157.html)): "Subject to contract between the partners", a firm is dissolved by the death of a partner. s.37 gives a deceased partner's **estate** a share of later profits or interest, unless a contract says otherwise. Both sections speak of the estate, not a nominee.

## 2. What the courts have said

### 2.1 Md. Monjurul Hoque Chowdhury v. Bilkis Ara Begum, HCD, Civil Revision 1682 of 2015, judgment 3 April 2016 ([PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov.bd/resources/documents/861423_C_R_1682_of_2015.pdf))

Naima Haider J, with Khizir Ahmed Choudhury J agreeing. The court called it "a case of the first impression in our jurisdiction".

- **Facts.** A Muslim man died intestate in 2013. Tk 30 lakh was in savings certificates with his second wife as "**100 percent nominee**". His children by his first wife applied for a succession certificate for Tk 26,25,000 and conceded the widow's 4/32 Quranic share. The trial court and the appeal court held the nominee had the absolute right, relying on BCA s.103 and Sanchayapatra Rules r.17.
- **Held:**
  - A nominee "does not inherit any title". The Rules show no intention to pass the beneficial interest. "A nominee, therefore, has assumed the character of a **trustee under sections 80 and 81 of the Trust Act, 1882** and is liable to those who have beneficial interest."
  - The Shariat Act 1937 governs Muslim succession. It "cannot be presumed to have been altered or superseded unless there is specific law contrary to the Shariat law of succession or the law with non obstante clause. There is nothing in the statute book to presume that a nominee has superseded the Shariat Law of succession."
  - **BCA s.103 does not apply to savings certificates, but it points the same way:** quoting s.103(3)–(4), "Bank Company Act 1991 also does not confer any title to the nominee over the Deposit … A nominee is liable for the amount to the rightful successors under the Bank Company Act as well."
  - The purpose of nomination "is to make certain the person with whom the bank has to deal, and not to create interest in the nominee to the exclusion of those who in law will be entitled to the estate … The nomination only indicates **the hand which is authorized to receive the amount**, on the payment of which the bank gets a valid discharge."
  - Following **Vishin N. Khanchandani v. Vidya Lachmandani** (2000) 6 SCC 724 (Supreme Court of India, Government Savings Certificates Act 1959).
  - Order: a succession certificate to issue. Bangladesh Bank to distribute among the heirs on its basis.

- **Bangladesh Bank, 2017.** BRPD Circular 06/2017 (banks) and DFIM Circular 02, 15 June 2017 (financial institutions) are reported to tell institutions to pay the nominee under s.103, and to stop taking affidavits from nominees that they may not be entitled. [Dhaka Tribune, 20 April 2017](https://archive.dhakatribune.com/business/banks/2017/04/20/banks-asked-pay-nominees-depositors-death); the pieces above. **Not read in the original**, because bb.org.bd blocked automated access and the Internet Archive has no copy. As reported, they are about **whom the bank pays**. They do not say the nominee keeps the money against the heirs.
- **2019.** New Age's report of the full text, [9 January 2019](https://www.newagebd.net/article/61266/nominee-only-authorised-hc), names the same parties and bench as Civil Revision 1682/2015. Dhaka Tribune's "full judgment in 2019" therefore appears to be the 2016 decision's written release, not a second case.

### 2.3 Asma Rahman v. Most. Rabeya Akhter, HCD, Civil Revision 5852 of 2022, judgment 4 February 2024 ([PDF](https://www.supremecourt.gov.bd/resources/documents/2333319_CIVILREVISIONNo.5852of2022.pdf))

A bank-account nominee who was not an heir (the deceased wife's sister) asked to have the heirs' title suit thrown out under s.103(1). The court **refused**. Whether s.103 bars the heirs "should be considered by the trial Court", on evidence. **The heirs' suit against a nominee is maintainable.** The nominee's counsel cited **Ziauddin Ahmed v. Arab Bangladesh Bank, 6 MLR (AD) 188** and **52 DLR (HCD) 36** for the nominee's right to withdraw. **Neither report was found online, so neither was read.** They may be Appellate Division authority on the bank's side of s.103, and the lawyer should be asked.

### 2.4 A pending writ, 2025 [SECONDARY]

On 13 May 2025 the High Court Division issued a rule asking why a deceased government servant's lump-sum pension and provident fund should not be distributed "**not to the nominee but among the lawful heirs under Muslim faraid**" ([Bangla Tribune, 13 May 2025](https://www.banglatribune.com/others/898325/)). The case number is not given, and no result was found.

### 2.5 Persuasive courts next door

- **Pakistan:** **Mst. Amtul Habib v. Mst. Musarrat Parveen, PLD 1974 SC 185.** A nomination is neither a gift nor a bequest. It "merely confers a right to collect the money", and it does not deprive the heirs. Read as quoted in later Pakistani judgments, e.g. [Peshawar High Court, 2013 CLC 1834](https://peshawarhighcourt.gov.pk/PHCCMS//judgments/09-2013-CLC-1834.pdf); not read in the original.
- **India:** Sarbati Devi (1984, insurance), Vishin Khanchandani (2000, savings certificates) and Shakti Yezdani (2023, shares and depository) all hold that the nominee collects and does not inherit. India changed this for insurance only by statute, with the "beneficial nominee" of the Insurance Laws (Amendment) Act 2015. **Bangladesh's Insurance Act 2010 has no such clause** (§1.3).

### 2.6 What this adds up to

There are two layers, and they should not be confused:

1. **The payer's layer.** Statutes, bye-laws and Bangladesh Bank tell the payer to pay the nominee, and they discharge the payer when it does. This part is settled.
2. **The ownership layer.** Who keeps the money is decided by the law of succession: faraid for Muslims. The only reasoned Bangladeshi judgment (2016) says the nominee is a trustee for the heirs, including under s.103. The Appellate Division has not ruled. CDBL's "to the exclusion of … my heirs" is the strongest contrary wording, and it is contract and bye-law, not statute.

**A private Venture has only the second layer.** No statute gives the farm a statutory discharge.

## 3. A private contract: the Venture

- **No Act governs nomination in a private mudarabah or partnership.** BCA s.103 applies to "a bank company". FCA s.61 applies to "a finance company". The Insurance Act applies to insurers, and CDBL rules to depository accounts. A farm's Investment Agreement is governed by the Contract Act, the Partnership Act where it is a partnership (see `bangladesh-pooled-investment.md` §1.5), and the Investor's personal law of succession.
- **The Investor's death does not end the Venture by itself.** AAOIFI SS 13 (Mudarabah, issued 16 May 2002) 10/1 lists how a mudarabah ends: unilateral termination, agreement, maturity, exhaustion of funds, and "**the death of the Mudarib**". The capital provider's death is not on the list. A mudarabah with an agreed term cannot be ended early except by mutual agreement (4/3/2). Under the Contract Act s.37, the Investor's rights pass to their representatives. Under the Partnership Act s.42(c) death dissolves a firm only "subject to contract". The Investor's capital and profit share, when the Venture settles, are **part of their estate**.
- **A term paying the nominee in their own right is a bequest.** A promise that on the Investor's death the farm will pay the nominee for the nominee's own benefit disposes of property on death. Under Muslim law that is a wasiyyah:
  - it is valid only up to **one third** of the net estate;
  - a bequest to an **heir** needs the **consent of the other heirs** after the death (§4).

  It cannot be a gift (hiba) either. A hiba needs delivery of possession in the donor's lifetime, and a gift to take effect at death is treated as a will. That rule is from Mulla, _Principles of Mahomedan Law_; **[SECONDARY]**, not re-read. The 2016 judgment's point applies with more force here. Without a statute with a non-obstante clause, nothing displaces the Shariat Act, and a private contract is not a statute. **Lawyer:** would such a term be void under Contract Act s.23, as defeating the provisions of a law, or merely ineffective against the heirs?

- **The farm's own exposure.** A bank that pays a nominee is discharged by s.103(4). The farm has no such section. It may argue that the Investor's instruction binds the Investor's representatives under Contract Act s.37. But **whether paying a nominee discharges the farm against heirs who dispute it is untested**. The safe patterns in the statutes are:
  - pay the nominee as a collector, with the nominee's written undertaking to account to the heirs;
  - above a threshold, pay only against a succession certificate. The GSB Act's no-representation ceiling is Tk 25,000. CDBL's Board may waive representation up to Tk 1 lakh.

  **Lawyer** question. Settling a dead Investor's share is out of scope for this map. The Agreement's wording is not.

- **OpenFarm today.**
  - The standard Agreement, `packages/domain/src/standard-templates.ts`: "If the Investor dies, their capital and share are paid **through their nominee to their lawful heirs**".
  - The nominee line: "only to pay the Investor's heirs".

  Both are the collector reading, and they match §2.1 and §4. The CONTEXT.md **Nominee** entry, "to receive their capital and share", does not.

## 4. Shariah

- **Fixed shares.** Qur'an 4:11–12 and 4:176 fix the heirs' shares (faraid). They are paid after debts and any valid bequest ("after any bequest he made or debt", 4:11–12).
- **One third.** Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas asked to bequeath all, then half, of his property. The Prophet said: "one third, yet even one third is too much. It is better for you to leave your inheritors wealthy than to leave them poor" ([Sahih al-Bukhari 2742](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2742)).
- **No bequest to an heir.** "Allah has appointed for everyone who has a right what is due to him, and no bequest must be made to an heir" ([Sunan Abi Dawud 2870](https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2870), graded hasan sahih). The same words appear in the Farewell Sermon ([Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2120](https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2120)). Hadith texts were read from the hadith-api mirror of these collections because sunnah.com refused the fetch.
- **Hanafi rule, as applied in Bangladesh.** A bequest over one third, or any bequest to an heir, takes effect only if the heirs (the other heirs, for a bequest to an heir) **consent after the testator's death** (Mulla, _Principles of Mahomedan Law_ ss.117–118). **[SECONDARY]:** the standard text Bangladeshi courts use; not re-read for this note.
- **Wasi, amin, wakil.**
  - A _wasi_ is an executor appointed by the deceased to pay debts, carry out bequests within one third, and look after minors' property.
  - An _amin_ holds another's property on trust.
  - A _wakil_ acts as an agent.

  Read the collector way, a nominee is an amin: they hold the money for the heirs and must hand it on. If the Investor also means the nominee to carry out the settlement, they act much as a wasi. Neither role gives the nominee any ownership. Read the beneficiary way, the nominee is a legatee (_musa lahu_), and every bequest limit applies. The 2016 judgment's "trustee" and Pakistan's "right to collect" match the amin reading.

- **AAOIFI.** There is **no AAOIFI standard on nomination, wills (wasaya) or gifts**. The Shari'ah Standards page lists no such title ([aaoifi.com](https://aaoifi.com/shariah-standards-3/?lang=en), read 26 September 2026).
  - The nearest text is **SS 26 Islamic Insurance** (issued 2 May 2005; [PDF](https://aaoifi.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/SS-26-Islamic-Insurance.pdf)), 6/2/1/4: "In case of death, the Takaful entitlements should be distributed among the deserving persons, parties or purposes as indicated in the documents, and according to the regulatory rules issued by the Shari'ah Supervisory Board. **In case the deceased was entitled to some investment balances, then the same should be distributed among the inheritors according to the Islamic rules of inheritance.**" The standard separates two kinds of money:
    - the takaful benefit, which comes from the donated pool, may go to whoever the documents name;
    - the participant's **own invested money goes to the heirs**.

    An Investor's capital and profit in a Venture are the participant's own invested money.

  - **SS 13 Mudarabah** 4/4: a mudarabah is "one of the trust-based contracts". The mudarib holds on trust for the capital provider and, after the provider's death, for their estate.
- **Bangladesh's Islamic banks.** Their account forms follow s.103 and add no faraid wording. Global Islami Bank's form (2019; [PDF](https://www.globalislamibankbd.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Account-Opeing-Form.pdf)) reads "nominate the following person(s) to receive the entire amount deposited … in the event of my/our death", with a receiver for a minor nominee. **No published ruling of an Islamic bank's Shariah board, or of the Central Shariah Board for Islamic Banks of Bangladesh, on nominees was found.** **Scholar** question.
- **Elsewhere, for the design** [NOT VERIFIED FROM PRIMARY]. Malaysia's regulator makes the form say which kind of nominee is meant:
  - by default a Muslim policyholder's nominee receives as an **executor** for the estate;
  - they take beneficially only under an express **conditional hibah**.

  This is from Bank Negara Malaysia's Shariah Advisory Council, as described in [IIUM papers](http://irep.iium.edu.my/28902/1/Is_aNominee.pdf). It shows a regulator requiring the form to state the nominee's role in words.

### 5.2 What the forms have in common

| Question             | What the market does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| How many             | Statutes: "one or more", no cap. Forms: CDBL **2** (older: 3), Family Savings **2**, MetLife **3**, IFIC **1 block** with a % field                                                                                                                         |

## 6. What this means for OpenFarm

### Constraints the spec should respect

1. **Name the nominee a collector.** Change the CONTEXT.md **Nominee** entry to match the Agreement: the person the farm pays so that they can hand the money to the heirs. Several nominees do not change that.
2. **Don't let a share read as inheritance.** If Nominees carry a share, the Agreement must say the share is what each nominee **collects**, not what they **own**. Otherwise the paper promises something the law will not enforce against the heirs (§3), and Shariah forbids (§4).
3. **A minor nominee needs a receiver**, not only a consent line. This is the one field every Bangladeshi regime has that OpenFarm lacks. Recording a nominee's date of birth, as CDBL does, would let the paper know who is a minor. Today the standard template strikes the minor line through by hand because the farm records no age.
4. **A nominee who dies first drops out.** The spec must say what happens to their share. The only statutory model is insurance: it goes to the surviving nominees, and if none survive, to the heirs.
5. **Signed Agreements keep their nominees as signed** (standing constraint). Every regime treats a nomination as the holder's instruction at the time, changeable only by a fresh written instruction.

### Choices for the grilling (ticket 03)

- **Joint collectors or shares.** Joint collectors are the most faithful to the law and to Shariah, and have the fewest fields. Shares are what banks, CDBL and insurers print, and what an Investor will expect from their bank.
- **The cap.** The market uses two or three. Nothing in law requires a cap.
- **The fields.** Name, relationship, phone and share are the minimum. Date of birth, and a receiver for a minor, cover what every market form collects. NID and photo are what banks and CDBL add.

### Take to the lawyer and the Shariah scholar

- **Lawyer:** see answer bullet 8. Also ask whether 6 MLR (AD) 188 and 52 DLR (HCD) 36 bear on s.103, and whether the Appellate Division has decided the appeal from CR 1682/2015.
- **Scholar:** the amin/collector reading. Naming an heir as a collector-nominee. Whether a share split among nominees needs any wording to avoid reading as a bequest.
