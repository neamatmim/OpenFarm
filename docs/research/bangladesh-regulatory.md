# Bangladesh regulatory requirements for a commercial dairy + beef-fattening cattle farm (100–500 head)

**Question:** What does Bangladeshi law and the Department of Livestock Services (DLS) actually require such a farm to _record_, _keep_, and _report_?

**Researched:** 10 September 2026. Primary sources only (Acts on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, gazetted Rules, DLS/MoFL/BFSA/BSTI official documents, DLS office memos, World Bank project documents). Where only a secondary source could be found it is marked **[SECONDARY]**. Bengali statutes were read in the original; English paraphrases are mine unless an official English text is cited.

**Headline:** Bangladeshi law imposes very few _explicit_ farm-level record-keeping duties on a cattle farm. The hard legal obligations are: (a) register the farm with DLS and renew it; (b) report suspected notifiable disease to DLS in writing, without delay; (c) not use steroids/hormones/antibiotics in feed; (d) buy antibiotics only on a registered practitioner's prescription; (e) keep supplier/buyer names, addresses and invoices for food (milk) sold. Everything else that a software system would want to evidence — animal IDs, treatment and withdrawal records, vaccination, mortality, milk quality — is required only indirectly (to obtain a slaughter fitness certificate, to satisfy DLS registration inspection, to survive a BFSA residue test) or by the DLS _National Guidelines on Good Livestock Production Practices_ (2023), which state in their own text that they are **not mandatory**.

## 1. Animal identification

**Is there a national cattle ID / ear-tag scheme?** No statute or rule mandates individual identification of cattle. The Animal Disease Act 2005 and the Meat Rules 2021 assume animals can be described (the Schedule-7 slaughter certificate has a "ট্যাগ নং" (tag no.) column, see §2) but neither requires a tag to be fitted.

**BINLI pilot (Bangladesh Integrated Network for Livestock Information).** Under the World Bank-financed _Livestock and Dairy Development Project_ (LDDP, P161246, implemented by DLS), a contracted JV firm built a "customized animal identification and recording software", trained 176 LDDP staff and was to ear-tag and register **50,000 dairy cattle** as a pilot. The October–November 2024 Implementation Support Mission recorded "not much progress"; the firm "did not have adequate supply of the ear tags from abroad"; only "ear tagging of 80 cows" had been completed; revised targets were 50,000 tags by 30 Dec 2024 and software hand-over by Feb 2025. Results indicator 13 is explicitly labelled "Cattle included in the **pilot** animal identification and recording system (Dairy cattle only)", target 50,000. Source: World Bank, _LDDP Implementation Support Mission Oct 22–Nov 17 2024_, paras 22, 24 and results annex ([PDF](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099113024222037734/pdf/P161246-e7c213f2-3838-4f92-9cc7-22c6725454df.pdf); document page [here](https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099113024222037734)). LDDP's own portal lists the project period as 1 Jan 2019 – 31 Oct 2025 ([lddp.portal.gov.bd](https://lddp.portal.gov.bd/)).

**What BINLI records** (per Daily Star, 14 May 2024, quoting BINLI deputy project manager — **[SECONDARY]**): barcode ear tag; breed, diet, health records incl. vaccination status; disease history "until its death"; location and owner; newborn calves added "within a few days"; 16 pilot upazilas (Agailjhara, Nazirpur, Karnaphuli, Cumilla Adarsha Sadar, Tungipara, Mollahat, Dumuria, Madarganj, Trishal, Shahjadpur, Chirirbandar, Pirganj, South Sunamganj, Sylhet Sadar, …); 4,800 of the registered cattle to be insured ([article](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/barcode-cattle-3609746)).

**Number format:** not published anywhere I could find. **Post-October-2025 status of BINLI:** no completion report found.

**What DLS recommends (non-binding).** NG-GLPP §6.2: identification by ear tag, RFID, tattoo, branding, biometrics; "the records of animal identification and animal traceability should be kept properly, if possible, electronically for inspection by the competent authority"; "if the veterinary authority of Bangladesh feels required, the animal identification and traceability records could be reported and recorded in the respective local DLS office". §6.2.3.1(h): "At a minimum, information is collected and stored concerning the animal's place and date of birth, name and address of the owner, date and location of movements between the animal's origin, and its place, date and location of slaughter." §16.2.4.1.2(a) lists an animal identification/history record with 18 fields (ID number, place of purchase, sex, DOB, breed, calving interval, lactation length, milk yield, drying date, offspring ID, disposal date, etc.). Legal status: the guideline itself says "the guidelines are not mandatory to abide by the stakeholders" (Chapter I). Published June 2023 by DLS/MoFL with USDA Bangladesh Trade Facilitation project support ([PDF](https://dls.portal.gov.bd/sites/default/files/files/dls.portal.gov.bd/page/61d70f88_045d_4205_b5ed_2bfe65623e3f/2025-03-06-16-08-6165641f39ac4af51b27a3149f41366e.pdf) — dls.portal.gov.bd was unreachable on 10 Sep 2026; retrieved via the Internet Archive).

**Software implication:** support an optional per-animal ID with a free-text tag field (BINLI barcode, private tag) and the GLPP minimum data set; do not assume a national numbering scheme.

## 2. Movement and sale records

### 2.1 Animal Disease Act 2005 (Act 5 of 2005) — [bdlaws act-925](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-925.html)

Movement controls only bite once the DG-DLS gazettes an **infected area** (s.5; up to 3 months, extendable 3 months):

- s.6(1)(ক): no person may move any animal (live or dead), animal product, feed or bedding out of the infected area, or bring animals in (exceptions: religious rites, government farms, cases specified by the DG/authorised Veterinary Officer).
- s.6(1)(খ): meat, milk, eggs or other products of diseased or in-contact animals may not be bought or sold.
- s.9(3): an animal called for examination may not be moved without the Veterinary Officer's permission.
- s.12: animals or products from an infected area may not be marketed without the permission of an authorised Veterinary Officer.
- s.15: no animal market, fair, exhibition or gathering in an infected area without the Veterinary Officer's written permission.
- Penalty s.25: up to 2 years' imprisonment or fine up to Tk 10,000 or both; s.22 makes directors/managers of a company liable.

Outside an infected area the Act imposes **no** movement permit, no sale record and no transport certificate on the farm.

### 2.2 Animal Slaughter and Meat Quality Control Act 2011 (Act 16 of 2011) and Rules 2021

Act: [bdlaws act-1079](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1079.html). Rules: _পশু জবাই ও মাংসের মান নিয়ন্ত্রণ বিধিমালা, ২০২১_, SRO 349-Law/2021 dated 21 Nov 2021, Bangladesh Gazette (Extraordinary) 11 Dec 2021, pp. 18639–18678 ([MoFL PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-mofl/2024/12/28b3b4ab81654cb7aac0d63a9339bdfa.pdf); listing page [mofl.gov.bd](https://mofl.gov.bd/pages/policies/6940313ac4774958d7b41e3f)). The Act licenses slaughterhouses, meat shops and meat-processing plants (ss.8–10) — not farms — but the Rules create data the _farm_ must be able to supply:

- **Rule 10 (ante-mortem inspection).** (1) The Veterinary Officer/veterinarian examines the animal within 24 hours before slaughter. (3) Before slaughter the officer may (ক) analyse the disease situation of "the farm or area where the animal was reared for the past 6 months", and (খ) "if the animal was under treatment within 30 days before slaughter, analyse the prescription (ব্যবস্থাপত্র) for the drugs administered and the drug withdrawal period (নিরাপদকাল)", and send blood/hair/faeces/saliva samples to a government-recognised laboratory. (4) If free of zoonotic/infectious disease, the officer issues a fitness certificate in **Schedule-7**. (5) "In the case of commercial meat production the person concerned shall preserve the Schedule-7 certificate in the manner directed by the Director General."
- **Schedule-7 certificate columns:** serial no.; common name of animal; length, height and weight; **tag no.**; **the farm or area where the animal was for the 30 days before being brought for slaughter**; date; vet's signature and seal.
- **Rule 18 (transport).** (1) Vehicles must be cleaned and disinfected with a DG-specified disinfectant before loading and after unloading. (2) The driver must carry an **information card** with: (ক) names, personal identity, NID numbers, driving licence and contact numbers of the vehicle owner, driver and helpers; (খ) name and number of animals carried; (গ) **name and address of the place or farm from which the animals were collected**; (ঘ) name of slaughterhouse/meat shop/processing plant and date/time of slaughter (where applicable); (ঙ) nature and quantity of meat (where applicable); (চ) date and time of departure; (ছ) destination. (3) Animals must be moved (on foot, road, rail, water or air) with regard to animal welfare, avoiding stress, bruising, trampling, suffocation, heart failure, heat stroke, sunburn, bloat, etc.
- **Rule 5** (poultry only) requires a 30-minute rest stop after 100 km or 3 hours; there is no equivalent numeric rule for cattle.
- **Schedule-6** lists the zoonotic and infectious diseases that disqualify an animal from slaughter (see §5).
- Act s.3: slaughter outside a licensed slaughterhouse is permitted for Eid-ul-Adha, Eid-ul-Fitr, religious/social occasions and family consumption — so Eid _qurbani_ animals sold from the farm are not subject to the Schedule-7 certificate.
- Penalties (Act s.24): up to 1 year simple imprisonment or fine Tk 5,000–25,000 (repeat: 2 years / Tk 10,000–50,000); tried under the Mobile Court Act 2009 (s.23).

### 2.3 Animal Welfare Act 2019 — [bdlaws act-1292](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1292/section-49028.html)

s.6(1)(ঘ): tying, confining or **carrying** an animal in a way that prevents it standing, sitting or lying naturally is "unnecessary cruelty"; s.6(1)(ট): offering for sale an animal suffering from injury, hunger, thirst or other cruelty without arranging treatment; s.6(1)(জ): administering harmful or non-applicable drugs or substances by mouth, injection, rectum or genitals. s.5 empowers the Government to make rules on rearing, transport and slaughter; no such rules were found.

### 2.4 Haats and Eid-ul-Adha

- No DLS movement permit or origin certificate is required by law for taking cattle to a haat. Haats are leased by city corporations/local government (e.g. DSCC lease notices) and buyers receive the lessee's _hasil_ (levy) receipt — this is the only routine sale document and it is a local-government instrument, not a DLS one. **[Not sourced to a primary DLS document.]**
- DLS forms Veterinary Medical Teams and monitoring committees at qurbani haats. Example: DLS office memo no. 33.01.0000.101.82.852.16-1283 dated 9 June 2024 constituting seven central/monitoring teams for Dhaka North and South haats "to provide veterinary services at qurbani animal haats" ([PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-dls/2024/12/3574d8845d65454bbbd266f6dbd41e9b.pdf); DLS page [পশুর হাট ও ভেটেরিনারি মেডিকেল টিম](https://dls.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922de08933eb65569e17b4e)). These teams examine animals at the haat; they do not issue farm-side documents.

### 2.5 Safe Food Act 2013 (Act 43 of 2013) — sale records — [bdlaws act-1127](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1127.html)

- s.38: "Every food business operator … shall, while conducting food business, preserve the **names, addresses and receipts or invoices (রশিদ বা চালান)** of the parties involved in the production, import, processing, storage, supply or sale of the food product … and shall be bound to show them to the Authority or a person empowered by it." No retention period is stated.
- s.2(9) defines "food business operator" (খাদ্য ব্যবসায়ী) as any person or entity conducting a food business; the Act does not carve out primary producers, so a farm selling milk is on its face covered. **Unclear:** whether BFSA in practice treats farms as FBOs; enforcement has focused on processors and retailers.
- s.39: no unregistered production/sale where registration is mandatory under another law; s.40: duty to cooperate with inspection and sampling.

### 2.6 Registration-condition sale rule (poultry-worded)

Animal Disease Rules 2008 Schedule 7(ঙ) "Conditions for establishing a commercial farm (other than GP and PS)" item 14: "চালান/রশিদ দিতে হইবে at the time of sale" (an invoice/receipt must be issued), and item 4: obtain a health-fitness certificate from the Veterinary Officer at the time of marketing. Reproduced in NG-GLPP Appendix 3; the schedule's other items refer to sheds and chickens, so **whether it applies to cattle farms is unclear** (see §6).

**Software implication:** for every animal that leaves the farm to slaughter, be able to print (i) the 30-day location history, (ii) any treatment in the last 30 days with prescription reference and withdrawal end-date, (iii) the farm's disease history for 6 months, and (iv) a Rule-18 transport information card. For every sale, keep buyer name/address and an invoice.

## 3. Veterinary medicine records

### 3.1 Who may prescribe/treat — Bangladesh Veterinary Council Act 2019 (Act 13 of 2019) — [bdlaws act-1293](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1293.html)

- s.2(15) "veterinary practice" = treatment, growth and conservation of animals, diagnosis, disease control and suppression, extension, quality control of animals and animal products, quarantine, research, AI, public health, education/training or **advice**.
- s.17: no person may practise or hold themselves out as a veterinarian without BVC registration and certificate. s.35: penalty up to 3 years' imprisonment or Tk 2 lakh fine or both. s.2(10) defines a "paravet" (≥3-year veterinary course after SSC) who is registered separately; the Act does not itself state what a paravet may prescribe (left to regulations).
- The Act contains no prescription-format or farm-record provision.

### 3.2 Prescription-only sale of antibiotics — Drugs and Cosmetics Act 2023 (Act 29 of 2023)

Official Bengali gazette 18 Sep 2023 ([DGDA page](http://www.dgdagov.info/index.php/laws-and-policies/3674-drug-and-cosmetics-act-2023); unofficial English translation [Asia Actual PDF](https://asiaactual.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Bangladesh-Drug-and-Cosmetics-Act-2023-English.pdf)):

- s.40(d): "No person or establishment shall sell … Antibiotics or any other drug except over the counter drug without a prescription from a registered physician." Penalty table item 22: fine up to Tk 20,000.
- s.72: no physician shall prescribe an unregistered drug.
- The Act covers "veterinary drugs" (s.6(2)(c) essential-drug list includes veterinary drugs) and defines "animal" (s.2(21)). Inspectors may examine "records and registers" at places of manufacture and **sale** (s.? — inspection powers, English text p.20) — this is a duty on pharmacies, not farms.
- **Unverified:** whether "registered physician" (নিবন্ধিত চিকিৎসক) in s.40(d) is read to include a BVC-registered veterinary practitioner. The 2023 Act contains no farm-level duty to keep treatment records.

### 3.3 Banned substances in feed and fattening — Fish Feed and Animal Feed Act 2010 (Act 2 of 2010)

English gazette text ([Bangladesh Trade Portal PDF](https://www.bangladeshtradeportal.gov.bd/kcfinder/upload/files/Fish%20Feed%20and%20Animal%20Feed%20Act,%202010.pdf); Bengali [bdlaws act-1029](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1029.html)):

- s.14(1): "Any harmful chemicals including antibiotic, growth hormone, steroid and insecticides cannot be used in fish feed and animal feed." s.14(2): violation is an offence. s.20: up to 1 year's imprisonment or fine up to Tk 50,000 or both. s.17: company officers liable.
- s.4/s.6: licence from the DG-DLS needed to "produce, process, import, export, market, sell, distribute" animal feed; licence valid 1 year (s.7). **Unclear:** whether a farm mixing feed solely for its own animals needs a licence — the Act does not address own-use.
- s.13: marketed feed must be labelled with manufacturer, registration number, weight, ingredients and nutrient %, **lot number**, source code, target species, production and expiry dates — useful fields for a farm's feed-purchase record.
- s.10: Government sets "ideal values" for feed ingredients by rule (Fish Feed and Animal Feed Rules 2013 — not retrieved).
- **DLS notice on steroids in fattening**: DLS memo no. শাখা-৩/বিবিধ-৯/(খন্ড-২)/২০১২/১৮৩৮ dated 1 Oct 2013, signed by the DG-DLS: use of _Steroid_ and _Hormone_ with cattle feed "or otherwise" for fattening "is completely prohibited and a punishable offence under the Fish Feed and Animal Feed Act 2010"; offenders face action under that Act and the Animal Disease Act 2005 ([DLS notice page](https://dls.gov.bd/pages/notices/6922e578dbfbab28ce090453); [scanned PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-dls/2024/12/d41e331b28cc4511bebb8ff6960ee769.PDF)).

### 3.4 Withdrawal periods (milk and meat)

- **No statutory withdrawal-period table for cattle drugs exists.** DLS has published _Standard Treatment Guidelines_ and _Antimicrobial Treatment Guidelines_ for **poultry** (2025) which state "the withdrawal period is product specific so review label" ([DLS poultry AMTG](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-dls/2024/12/269e007bf09c4e8aa04b7b27f4bc3b0c.pdf)); no cattle equivalent was found.
- Legal hooks: Meat Rules 2021 r.10(3)(খ) (vet analyses prescription and withdrawal period for treatment within 30 days pre-slaughter — §2.2); Safe Food Act 2013 s.30 (residue limits, §4.2).
- NG-GLPP §6.4.7(e): "All treated animals and/or their products (eggs, milk & meat) should not be allowed for human consumption/marketed for human consumption until drug withdrawal period is completed and certified fit for human consumption by a Registered Veterinarian." (non-binding)
- DLS _Veterinary Antimicrobial Stewardship Guideline_ (Feb 2026), section "2. Farmers": use antimicrobials only "with prescriptions of registered veterinarians"; adhere to "dosage, frequency of dosing, duration, and withdrawal periods"; "Refrain from marketing or selling eggs, milk, or animal for slaughter before the completion of the prescribed drug withdrawal period"; avoid self-prescription; maintain traceable health records ([PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-dls/2026/4/cbe9a173-b7f1-436a-b1a2-2372d324c7da.pdf)). Guideline, not law.

### 3.5 What DLS says the treatment record should contain (NG-GLPP §6.4.8, non-binding)

- Keep "complete records of animal health programs (disease monitoring, medication, vaccination, prevention and control measures)", "easily retrievable for traceability"; record types: vaccination program, deworming, disease condition, diagnosis, intervention/treatment, control measures, post-mortem findings, surveillance, disposal, mortality, fumigation.
- "The farm should maintain updated records of **medicine purchase and administration** that should be readily available for inspection."
- **Procurement record:** product name (generic), date of purchase, quantity, batch number, expiry date, supplier name.
- **Administration record:** type of drug, batch number, date of administration, reason, route, quantity, identification of animal/group treated, number treated, date of completion, **withdrawal period**, name of person who administered.
- Retention: "The records should be kept for a minimum of 3 years or as specified by the authority" (§16.2.4.1.2, note).

## 4. Milk

### 4.1 BSTI standards

- **Pasteurised milk – BDS 1702:2019 (1st Rev.)** is item 62 on BSTI's _List of 315 Products Brought Under Mandatory Certification Marks_ (Agricultural and Food Products) ([BSTI list PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-bsti/2024/12/089df4031c004dcabafb81ba5334d855.pdf); catalogue entry [ec.eservice.bsti.gov.bd](<https://ec.eservice.bsti.gov.bd/bds-detail/pasteurized-milk-(first-revision)>)). Related mandatory items: Pasteurized Low Fat Milk and Standardized Milk BDS 1866:2022 (item 75); UHT milk BDS 1805:2022 (71); Milk powder BDS 860:2020 (27); Flavoured milk BDS 1471:2012 (50); Fermented milks BDS CAC 243:2015 (100); Sweetened condensed milk BDS CAC A-4:2002 (112).
- **Raw milk:** no Bangladesh Standard for raw milk appears on the mandatory list, and none was found in the BSTI catalogue. A farm selling raw milk to a collection centre needs no BSTI licence; a farm that pasteurises and packs must hold a CM licence under BDS 1702.
- **Compositional/microbiological limits of BDS 1702:2019** (fat, SNF, acidity, total plate count, coliforms, antibiotic residue) could not be retrieved from a primary source: the BSTI draft PDF and bsti.portal.gov.bd were down on 10 Sep 2026 and the standard is sold, not published. **Flagged.**

### 4.2 Safe Food Act 2013 and BFSA regulations

- s.23: prohibited harmful chemicals (formalin etc.) in food; s.25: no production/sale of adulterated food; **s.30:** no person shall use or sell food containing "residues of pesticides, residues of animal or fish drugs, hormones, antibiotics or growth-promoter residues, solvent residues, active pharmaceutical substances, micro-organisms or parasites" above the limits set by regulation or other law ([bdlaws act-1127](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-details-1127.html)).
- s.38 supplier/buyer names, addresses, invoices (see §2.5).
- **Food Safety (Contaminants, Toxins and Harmful Residues) Regulations 2017** — in force and the current source of MRLs (USDA FAIRS report 2021/2025 **[SECONDARY]**; BFSA's own copy on [bfsa.gov.bd law page](https://bfsa.gov.bd/site/view/law/-) is a scanned image that could not be text-extracted). Values for milk not extracted.
- **Draft Food Safety (Residues of Veterinary and Fishery Drug) Regulations 2024** (BFSA, undated draft notification; notified to WTO as G/SPS/N/BGD/9 on 15 Mar 2024): reg.3 — no food may be sold with veterinary drug residues above Schedule-1 MRLs, and **no residue at all** of Schedule-2 drugs; reg.4 — Codex limits apply where not scheduled; reg.6 — supersedes the residue provisions of the 2017 Regulations and Pure Food Rules 1967; comes into force 6 months after gazette ([BFSA PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-bfsa/2024/12/e1526fed9af44638b46ecb56e66c95cd.pdf)). Still marked "DRAFT" on the BFSA site; gazette status unknown.
- **Food Safety (Registration and Licensing) Regulations 2026** — gazetted 16 July 2026; "dairy producers" must register; registrations valid 3 years; enforcement "will initially focus on large-scale industrial food producers" (Bangladesh Monitor, 25 Jul 2026 **[SECONDARY]**; [article](https://www.bangladeshmonitor.com.bd/en/all-food-businesses-to-require-bfsa-registration-under-new-rules)). Primary gazette text not located; the earlier draft was posted for comment on bfsa.gov.bd (notices dated 2024 and Sept 2025).

### 4.3 Milk record-keeping obligations

No statute prescribes a milk register for a farm. The only legal record duty is Safe Food Act s.38 (parties and invoices). DLS's non-binding NG-GLPP §16.2.4.1.2(c) lists milk production records: 24-hour yield, yearly yield, specific gravity, total solids, fat, SNF, protein, lactose, somatic cell count, lactation length and yield, number of lactations, calving interval. §10.1.3.3 sets milking hygiene (mastitic cows milked last, teat wash, foremilk strip cup, teat dip 10 ml/15 ml).

**Context:** in July 2019 the High Court briefly banned pasteurised milk from 14 companies (Milk Vita, PRAN, Aarong, Akij/Farm Fresh, Igloo, etc.) after tests found antibiotic and lead residues; the ban was lifted on appeal (Daily Star, 28–31 Jul 2019 **[SECONDARY]**). Processors have since tightened collection-point residue screening but publish no supplier policy (see §7).

## 5. Disease reporting, mortality and vaccination

### 5.1 Notifiable diseases

- Animal Disease Act 2005 s.2(ঙ): "disease" means any contagious or infectious disease listed in the **Schedule** of the Act plus any disease the Government declares by gazette. **The Schedule text is not reproduced on bdlaws** (the section pages end at s.33) and the FAOLEX English translation ([PDF](https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/bgd165089E.pdf)) also omits it. The Animal Disease Rules 2008 are described (NG-GLPP; Meat Rules 2021 r.2(10)) as containing seven schedules with disease names, isolation periods and samples, but no online copy of the Rules could be retrieved (upazila DLS pages linking it return "page not found"). **Flagged.**
- The closest gazetted list is **Meat Rules 2021 Schedule-6** (diseases disqualifying from slaughter): (ক) zoonotic diseases of cattle/buffalo/goat/sheep — Anthrax, Rabies, Brucellosis, Salmonellosis, E. coli O157:H7, Dermatophilosis, Leptospirosis, Pseudocowpox, Vesicular stomatitis, Streptococcal sepsis, Bovine tuberculosis, Campylobacter, Cryptosporidiosis, BSE, Cysticercosis, Encephalitis, Enzootic abortion, Giardiasis, Hydatid disease, Listeria, Pasteurellosis, Rinderpest, Q fever, Ringworm, Toxocariasis, Toxoplasmosis, Tularemia, West Nile virus, Zoonotic diphtheria; (গ) infectious diseases — **FMD**, PPR, Goat pox, Sheep pox, **Black Quarter**, **Haemorrhagic Septicaemia**, Johne's disease, CBPP, Vibriosis, BVD, MCF, **Lumpy Skin Disease**, IBR, Bovine viral leukosis. Anthrax is in (ক).

### 5.2 Reporting duty

- **s.3(1):** every owner, possessor, custodian or controller of an animal — and any veterinarian or DLS field worker who, while treating or otherwise, has reasonable cause to believe an animal is affected by a (scheduled) disease — "shall **without delay** (অনতিবিলম্বে) inform the Director General or a Veterinary Officer empowered by him **in writing** (লিখিতভাবে)". No hour limit and no form is prescribed in the Act; in practice the report goes to the Upazila Livestock Officer (ULO). s.3(2): the officer investigates and takes action "in the prescribed manner".
- **s.4:** the owner must isolate an animal he is satisfied is diseased and keep healthy animals away.
- **s.9–10:** owner must produce animals for examination, allow sampling (blood, milk, faeces) and post-mortem; s.14: written orders to isolate/treat, seizure on non-compliance.
- **Internal DLS surveillance:** DLS Epidemiology Cell memo no. 33.01.0000.000.113.32.0001.26.16 dated 22 April 2026 directs all 495 Upazila Livestock Offices/Veterinary Hospitals and 64 District Veterinary Hospitals to enter daily disease data into the **BAHIS** (Bangladesh Animal Health Intelligence System) Patient Registry, noting reporting had declined ([DLS notice](https://dls.gov.bd/pages/notices/bahis-bangladesh-animal-health-intelligence-system-%E0%A6%8F-%E0%A6%AA%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A3%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%B0%E0%A7%8B%E0%A6%97-%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%82%E0%A6%95%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%A8%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%A4-%E0%A6%A4%E0%A6%A5%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%AF-%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%AA%E0%A7%8B%E0%A6%B0%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9F%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%82-%E0%A6%AA%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%B8%E0%A6%99%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%97%E0%A7%87-w8qjco-69e9887e71e38d494d60beb5); [memo PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-dls/2026/3/8c950a54-c459-468c-9583-dc4bff1b9e10.pdf); dashboard [bahis.dls.gov.bd](http://bahis.dls.gov.bd/dash/app/)). Farms do not report into BAHIS; the ULO does, from cases presented to it.

### 5.3 Mortality

- No stand-alone mortality-reporting duty. s.11(1): the carcass of any animal suspected to have died of disease must be destroyed by burial at least six feet deep **with its hide**, or by burning, or another prescribed method; s.11(2) no exhumation except for post-mortem under s.10; s.11(3) no dumping of carcasses, straw or waste where they threaten public or animal health. Meat Rules 2021 r.10(6): zoonotic cases culled and buried ≥7 ft.
- NG-GLPP §6.4.8 lists mortality, post-mortem findings and disposal among the records to keep (non-binding).

### 5.4 Vaccination

- **Compulsory only in a gazetted infected area:** s.7(1) the DG arranges vaccination of all animals of the relevant class; s.7(2) owners "shall be bound to provide all necessary assistance and facilities".
- **No statutory vaccination record for cattle.** (Animal Disease Rules 2008 Schedule 7(ঘ) item 12 requires "all information relating to vaccination" to be preserved — but that schedule governs poultry _parent-stock_ farms.)
- **DLS recommended schedule (NG-GLPP Appendix 31, "Vaccination Schedules for Cattle and Buffalo"):** FMD — first dose at ≥4 months, booster 1 month later, then six-monthly; Anthrax — ≥4 months, annually in endemic areas; HS — ≥6 months, annually in endemic areas; BQ — ≥6 months, annually in endemic areas; Brucellosis — female calves 4–8 months, once; Theileriosis — ≥3 months, once (crossbred/exotic only); IBR — ≥3 months, booster 1 month, six-monthly; Rabies — post-bite only. **LSD is absent** from the 2023 schedule despite being endemic and listed in Meat Rules Schedule-6.

**Software implication:** store, per animal, vaccine name/batch/date/route/administrator and next-due date for at least FMD, anthrax, HS, BQ, brucellosis (heifers) and LSD; generate a written disease-suspicion report addressed to the ULO; log carcass disposal method and depth.

## 6. Farm registration / licensing

### 6.1 Statutory basis — Animal Disease Act 2005 ss.16–21

- **s.16(1)(খ):** subject to s.16(2), no person shall, for commercial purposes and without registration, "establish and operate a cattle farm (গবাদি পশুর খামার) or poultry farm". Also covered: animal hospitals, animal-product processing plants, semen collection/processing, and keeping bulls/bucks or donor cows for breeding/embryo work (s.16(1)(ক),(গ)–(ঙ)).
- **s.16(2)(গ):** exemption for farms "established and operated for family use" and a prescribed number of breeding males — a 100–500-head unit is plainly commercial.
- **s.17:** pre-registration inspection by DG/authorised Veterinary Officer. **s.18:** application in prescribed form with fee; decision within 60 days (+30); certificate states validity and renewal date; renewable on fee; criteria include financial capacity, consistency with purpose, public health and environment. **s.19:** "a copy of every registration certificate granted under s.18 shall be preserved." **s.20:** cancellation for breach/conviction after 15 days' show-cause; appeal within 30 days. **s.21:** existing farms had 6 months to register. **s.25:** penalty up to 2 years / Tk 10,000. **s.29:** Veterinary Officers may enter any farm, animal-keeping place, vehicle or processing plant at reasonable times.

### 6.2 Animal Disease Rules 2008 (পশুরোগ বিধিমালা, ২০০৮) — as evidenced

The full Rules could not be retrieved online (see "Unclear"). What is evidenced by primary documents:

The wording refers to chickens and sheds; **no cattle-specific schedule of conditions was found**.

## 7. Buyer / processor requirements

No processor publishes a supplier-requirements document. What official or first-party pages say:

- **Milk Vita (Bangladesh Milk Producers' Co-operative Union Ltd)** — Citizen Charter ([milkvita.org.bd](https://milkvita.org.bd/pages/static-pages/6922dd43933eb65569e14343)): farmers supply through a _primary milk producers' cooperative society_ (formed after a field survey; 90 working days); members' milk is bought "at a price fixed on the basis of the fat (ননী) content" against a **milk dispatch challan** submitted to the local Milk Vita office; members' cows get free vaccination, treatment and AI, feed at cost, interest-free cow loans. Milk collection is managed in the "Online Milk Collection and Distribution Management System" ([milkvita.rdcd.gov.bd](https://milkvita.rdcd.gov.bd/)). No published residue or hygiene acceptance criteria.
- **Aarong Dairy / BRAC Dairy** ([brac.net](https://www.brac.net/solutions/social-enterprise/brac-dairy/)): ~35,000 registered farmers, 180 chilling centres/collection points, ~150,000 L/day, ISO 22000-certified processing. No supplier criteria published.
- **PRAN Dairy** — 5 dairy hubs (Chatmohar, Gurudaspur, Shahjadpur, Baghabari, Rangpur), 101 collection/cooling centres, ~12,000 contract farmers, hub vets and extension staff (The Business Standard; Tetra Pak/UNIDO dairy-hub documents **[SECONDARY]**). No supplier code published.
- **Akij Dairy** — nothing published found.
- **Bengal Meat** — per a USAID Agrilinks post (fetch failed with 504; search excerpt **[SECONDARY, unverified]**): sources only from "registered farmers" through fixed Collection Hubs; requires regular health check-ups, deworming and vaccination protocols; ~19,000 cattle from ~7,000 trained farmers.
- **De facto requirements** a supplier system should support: individual-animal treatment/withdrawal status (post-2019 residue scandal; processors screen for antibiotics at intake — reported in press, not in policy documents), fat/SNF per delivery, challan per delivery, and for meat buyers a vaccination/deworming history and 30-day treatment history (which is also what the Meat Rules 2021 certificate needs).
