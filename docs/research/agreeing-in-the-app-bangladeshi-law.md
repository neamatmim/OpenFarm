# Agreeing in the app under Bangladeshi law

**Question:** Suppose an **Investment Agreement**, an **Amendment** or a **Nomination** (মনোনয়নপত্র) is agreed in the **Investor Portal**: the signed-in Investor reads the paper and enters a one-time code sent by SMS to their phone. Is that agreement valid under Bangladeshi law, and can it be proved? How is its stamp duty met? (Ticket: `.scratch/openfarm-sign-in-the-app/issues/02-an-agreement-made-in-the-app-under-bangladeshi-law.md`.)

**Researched:** 8 October 2026. Primary sources: the Acts as consolidated on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, read in the Bengali original where the Bengali text is authoritative (the ICT Act 2006, s.90); the Finance Act 2026 and the Cyber Security Act 2026 on bdlaws; the Stamp Duties (Additional Modes of Payment) Act 1974 and its 2001 and 2010 Rules, read from the Internal Resources Division's (IRD's) scanned gazettes; Bangladesh Bank and BFIU circulars on bb.org.bd and bfiu.org.bd; and the CCA's e-Sign user manual. Newspapers are used **only** for the A-challan plan, which has no gazette yet, and are marked **[SECONDARY]**. English renderings of Bengali sections are mine. **This is research, not legal advice.** It builds on [bangladesh-pooled-investment.md §3.1](bangladesh-pooled-investment.md), which already sets out ss.17 and 35 of the Stamp Act and the Schedule I amounts, and does not repeat them.

**Headline:** **The contract is not the problem. The stamp is.** Bangladeshi law does not ask a mudarabah, an amendment or a nomination to be signed on paper. Acceptance by an act in the app makes a contract. The ICT Act recognizes electronic records and has no list of documents excluded from electronic form. The Evidence Act, since 2022, admits a digital record when it comes with a certificate. But if an Agreement made in the app is an "instrument" that has been "executed", it is chargeable with duty. **No law in force lets duty be paid onto an electronic instrument:** there is no e-stamp, and the challan routes in force cover only documents being registered (or companies at RJSC). An Agreement made in the app is therefore unstamped. Under s.35 a court or an arbitrator cannot admit it or act on it until the duty and a penalty of ten times the duty are paid. That defect can be cured for a known, modest sum, but the cure is still a defect. **One side-finding reaches beyond the app:** the "e-challan" the farm already allows for paper Agreements has the same gap (§4.5).

---

## Answer for the ticket

1. **An agreement made in the app is a valid contract.** Under the Contract Act 1872, a proposal and its acceptance may be made "by any act … by which he intends to communicate" it (s.3). Acceptance is complete against the proposer once it is out of the acceptor's power (s.4). Every agreement made with free consent, for lawful consideration and a lawful object, is a contract (s.10). No Act found requires a mudarabah, a partnership, an amendment of one, or a private nomination to be in writing or signed. Only s.10's saving for "any law … by which any contract is required to be made in writing" could change that, and none applies. The Arbitration Act 2001 needs the arbitration clause "in writing", and it counts an instrument communicated "by … e-mail or any other means of exchange" (s.9(2)(খ)). So the named **Arbitrator** clause survives too.

2. **The ICT Act 2006 recognizes the record. It recognizes an SMS-code "signature" only arguably.**
   - **The record:** where a law requires writing, an electronic form will do, provided it stays accessible for later reference (s.6).
   - **The signature:** where a law requires a signature, an electronic signature will do, but only one affixed "in the prescribed manner" (নির্ধারিত পদ্ধতিতে, s.7). No rule found prescribes that manner for anything but certificate-based signatures.
   - **The definition is demanding.** Even a plain "electronic signature" must be uniquely linked to the signer, identify them, be created under the signer's control, and make any later change to the data detectable (s.2(1)).
   - **The farm need not rely on s.7,** because no law requires its Agreement to be signed. **What it needs is attribution: proof that this Investor agreed to this paper.** Section 13(3)(ক) gives that directly. The farm may treat a record as the Investor's if it checked it by a procedure **the Investor agreed to beforehand** for that purpose.
   - **The Act has no exclusion list.** Unlike India's IT Act, it names no instruments that cannot be made electronically. Its only carve-outs are a law that prescribes its own way of keeping a record (s.9(3)) and the government, which cannot be compelled to accept electronic documents (s.11).

3. **"Electronic signature" and "digital signature" are one concept in Bangladesh.** The Bengali text of the ICT Act says ইলেক্ট্রনিক স্বাক্ষর throughout. The Evidence Act defines "Digital Signature or electronic Signature" as the ICT Act's electronic signature (s.3, inserted 2022). The real distinctions are two. One is a **"secure" electronic signature** (ICT s.17: a security procedure agreed by the parties that shows the signature was the signer's alone, identified them and was under their sole control). The other is a signature backed by a **certificate** from a licensed Certifying Authority (s.2(15) "subscriber", s.36). The presumptions in the Evidence Act attach to "secure" signatures and to "subscribers" (ss.67A, 85B, 85C). An SMS-code agreement is neither one for certain. So the farm proves the agreement the ordinary way: the record, its s.65B certificate, and the trail.

4. **Proving it in court: Evidence Act as amended by Act 20 of 2022 (20 November 2022).** A "document" now "includes any digital record" (s.3). Its contents are proved under s.65B: a computer output is admissible "without further proof or production of the original" if the conditions in s.65B(2) are met, and a certificate signed by "a person occupying a responsible official position" may prove them (s.65B(4)). Unless the signature is "secure", whoever relies on it must prove it is the signer's (s.67A). The court may presume that a message reached the addressee as sent, but **"shall not make any presumption as to the persons by whom such message was sent"** (s.88A). So the farm must prove who entered the code. That proof is the first meeting (identity checked against the NID), the phone number recorded there, the Portal Consent's agreed procedure, and the log.

5. **Stamp duty: probably chargeable, and today it cannot be paid.**
   - **Chargeable:** an "instrument" is "every document" creating or recording a right or liability (s.2(14)). "Executed" means "signed" (s.2(12)). A chargeable instrument executed in Bangladesh "shall be stamped before or at the time of execution" (s.17).
   - **Not payable:** "duly stamped" means the instrument "bears an adhesive or impressed stamp" (s.2(11)). Nothing can be affixed to an electronic record. The challan routes in force are three. **Stamp Duties (Additional Modes of Payment) Act 1974, s.2:** only an instrument "required or intended to be registered under the Registration Act, 1908"; the registering officer's endorsement then makes it "duly stamped". **Stamp Duty Payment (Additional Methods) Rules 2001:** registrable documents. **2010 Rules:** company registration and share transfer at RJSC. **No e-stamping law or rule was found**, and IRD's own list of stamp rules has none.
   - **Coming:** IRD has decided to move all non-judicial stamp duty to **A-challan**. Reported: optional from 1 July 2026, the only way from 1 July 2027, with the challan number written on the first page and the printed receipt attached **[SECONDARY]**. No gazette was found. The Finance Act 2026 does not amend the Stamp Act.

6. **What s.35 means for an unstamped Agreement.**
   - **The bar:** it "shall not be admitted in evidence for any purpose" by anyone with authority to receive evidence, an arbitrator included, nor "acted upon" by them or a public officer, until it is "duly stamped".
   - **The cure:** proviso (a) admits it on payment of the duty plus a penalty of Tk 5 or **ten times the duty**, whichever is more. At Art. 5(c)'s Tk 300 that is **Tk 3,300**. At Art. 45's Tk 4,000 it is **Tk 44,000**.
   - **No way around it:** Evidence Act s.91 forbids proving a written contract's terms by anything but the document. Signing a chargeable instrument without a stamp is also an offense, fined up to Tk 500 (s.62(1)(b)).
   - **In short:** the risk is real, but it is a fiscal defect. It does not make the contract void.

7. **The Nomination is the easy case.** It is a one-sided direction, not an agreement, and no article of Schedule I describes it. Chargeability is fixed by Schedule I (s.3), and the farm already treats a Nomination as unstamped ("a letter, not a contract", CONTEXT.md). If that holds, s.35 never touches it, and a Nomination agreed in the app is simply a digital record proved under s.65B. **This needs the lawyer's yes** before it is built.

8. **What the regulators do: practice to show the lawyer, not law that binds the farm.**
   - **Bangladesh Bank's e-loan** (BRPD-1 Circular 11, 11 May 2026) replaces the **wet signature on the loan application, sanction letter and charge documents** with consent given on identity checked by **biometrics**. It requires the registered SIM, an OTP and 2FA/MFA (paras 2(ঙ), 2(ঢ)).
   - **Bangladesh Bank's and BFIU's e-KYC guidelines** (BRPD-1 Circular 08, 11 March 2026; BFIU Circular 29, 30 March 2026) accept a wet, device-drawn, digital or imaged signature. A PIN may stand in only for **low-risk** accounts. The OTP is a check on the phone number, never the signature.
   - **The pattern:** the regulators use an SMS code to prove the channel, not as the signature, and pair it with biometrics for consent. **No circular treats an SMS code alone as a signature.** None of them speaks to stamp duty.

## 1. Contract Act 1872: an agreement made in the app

([bdlaws act-26](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26.html))

- **s.2(a), (b), (h)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26/section-163.html)): a proposal accepted becomes a promise; an agreement enforceable by law is a contract.
- **s.3** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26/section-164.html)): communication, acceptance and revocation "are deemed to be made by any act or omission of the party … by which he intends to communicate such proposal, acceptance or revocation, or which has the effect of communicating it". Entering the SMS code and pressing agree is such an act.
- **s.4** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26/section-165.html)): an acceptance is complete "as against the proposer, when it is put in a course of transmission to him, so as to be out of the power of the acceptor". The ICT Act fixes when an electronic record is sent and received (s.15): sent when it enters a system outside the sender's control.
- **s.7** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26/section-168.html)): the acceptance must be absolute and expressed "in some usual and reasonable manner, unless the proposal prescribes the manner in which it is to be accepted". So the Offer itself may say it is accepted only by entering the code. The Agreement Offer's design meets the "absolute" limb: the Investor agrees to one fixed paper, identified by its `paperHash`, not to a version that can change.
- **s.10** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26/section-172.html)): all agreements made by free consent of competent parties, for lawful consideration and object, are contracts. The second paragraph saves any law that requires a contract "to be made in writing or in the presence of witnesses". None was found for a mudarabah, a partnership (see [bangladesh-pooled-investment.md](bangladesh-pooled-investment.md) §3.2) or a private nomination.
- **s.11** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-26/section-173.html)): a minor cannot contract. A minor Nominee's **Receiver** is an adult. What they give is a consent on the minor's behalf, not the minor's contract. Whether a Receiver may consent by SMS is a question for the lawyer (map: "a Receiver's SMS consent only if the advisers allow it").

**Arbitration Act 2001, s.9** ([bdlaws act-850](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-850.html)):

- **Writing:** an arbitration agreement "must be in writing". It is in writing if it is part of a document signed by the parties (ক), **or** if it is contained in an instrument "communicated by letter, telex, telegram, fax, e-mail or any other means of exchange" (খ).
- **Evidence:** the arbitral tribunal is not bound by the Evidence Act (s.24). The Stamp Act's s.35 still binds it, because an arbitrator is a "person having by … consent of parties authority to receive evidence".

## 2. ICT Act 2006 (Act 39 of 2006, 8 October 2006)

([bdlaws act-950](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950.html), amended 2009 and 2013; ss.54–57 and 66 repealed by the Digital Security Act 2018. The Bengali text prevails over any English one: [s.90](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28901.html).)

### 2.1 What the Act calls a signature

- **s.2(1) "electronic signature"** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28813.html)): data in electronic form that (ক) is directly or logically attached to other electronic data, and (খ) is authenticated by meeting four conditions:
  - (অ) it is uniquely linked to the signer;
  - (আ) it can identify the signer;
  - (ই) it is created by a secure means kept under the signer's control;
  - (ঈ) it is linked to the attached data so that any later change to that data can be detected.
- **s.2(7) "electronic record"**: data, records, or images or sound made from data, stored, received or sent in electronic form. **s.2(11) "data message"** names **SMS (সর্ট মেসিজ)** expressly.
- **s.2(15) "subscriber" (গ্রাহক)**: the person in whose name an electronic signature certificate is issued. **s.2(33)**: a Certifying Authority is licensed under s.22. **s.36** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28847.html)) sets the checks before a certificate is issued.
- **"Digital signature"** is not a separate term in the Bengali Act. A law firm notes the English translation uses "digital signature" where the Bengali says "electronic signature" ([VDB Loi, 27 April 2022](https://www.vdb-loi.com/bd_publications/rules-on-electronic-signatures-in-bangladesh/) **[SECONDARY]**). The Evidence Act now treats the two words as one (§3).

**What an SMS-code agreement can and cannot meet:**

- **Condition (ঈ)** is met if the code is bound to the paper's fingerprint. Send the fingerprint or the paper's number in the SMS, and store the verification against the `paperHash`.
- **Conditions (অ) and (আ)** rest on the phone number taken at the in-person first meeting.
- **Condition (ই) is the weak one.** In an OTP scheme the farm's server makes the code and knows it. A challenger can argue the signing means was never under the Investor's "sole control" (s.17(1)(গ)). SIM swap is the other line of attack.

### 2.2 Legal recognition: ss.5–7

- **s.5** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28816.html)): (1) a **subscriber** may authenticate an electronic record by affixing their electronic signature. (2) Authentication must use "a technology-neutral method or a recognized signature-creating device or means" (প্রযুক্তি নিরপেক্ষ পদ্ধতি বা স্বীকৃত স্বাক্ষর সৃষ্টিকারী যন্ত্র বা কৌশল). Sub-section (2) opens the door to non-certificate methods; sub-section (1) speaks only of subscribers.
- **s.6** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28817.html)): where any other law requires a thing to be handwritten, typed or otherwise in writing or print, it may be recorded electronically, "provided that it is accessible so as to be usable for a subsequent reference".
- **s.7** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28818.html)): where any other law requires information to be authenticated by affixing a signature, or a document to be signed, it may be authenticated by affixing an electronic signature **"in the prescribed manner"**. "Prescribed" means prescribed by rules (s.2(19)).
- **s.12** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28823.html)) and **s.88**: the government may make rules on the types of electronic signature, how they are affixed, and how the signer is identified. The rules found are the Information Technology (Certifying Authorities) Rules 2010 and the CCA's E-Sign Guidelines (2020, cited by VDB Loi **[SECONDARY]**). Both concern certificate-based signatures. **No rule was found that prescribes an OTP or click-to-agree manner under s.7.**
- **s.3** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28814.html)): the Act has effect notwithstanding any other law.

### 2.3 Attribution and acknowledgment: where the Portal Consent matters

- **s.13** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28824.html)):
  - (1) A record sent by the originator is theirs.
  - (3) As between originator and addressee, the addressee may treat a record as the originator's if **(ক) "to ascertain whether the electronic record was the originator's, the addressee properly applied a procedure previously agreed to by the originator for that purpose"** (পূর্বে স্থিরীকৃত পদ্ধতিতে).
  - (4) That stops applying once the originator gives notice that the record is not theirs and a reasonable time passes, or once the addressee knew or should have known it was not theirs.
- **s.14** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28825.html)): where the parties agreed that receipt would be acknowledged, an acknowledgment raises a presumption that the record was received. It raises none that the content matches.

**What this means for the farm:**

- **The paper Portal Consent** (signed at the first meeting, which stays) is the natural place for the Investor to agree, in writing, the procedure: "an offer I agree to from my own sign-in by entering a code sent to [this number] is my acceptance".
- **The confirmation SMS** the farm sends afterwards, naming the paper, is the acknowledgment s.14 describes.

### 2.4 Secure electronic signature: s.17

- **s.16** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28827.html)): a record becomes a secure electronic record once a security procedure is applied to it.
- **s.17** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28828.html)):
  - (1) **"With the consent of the parties concerned"**, a security procedure may show that, when the signature was affixed, it was (ক) unique to the signer, (খ) capable of identifying them, and (গ) under the signer's **sole control** as to how it was created or used. Such a signature counts as a secure electronic signature.
  - (2) It is void if the related record is altered.
- **No certificate is required by s.17.** That makes it the strongest footing an agreed OTP procedure could claim. The Evidence Act's presumptions for "secure" signatures (§3) would then follow. Condition (গ) is the doubt raised in §2.1. **No judgment applying s.17 to an OTP was found.**

### 2.5 No exclusions

The whole Act was read in Bengali. It has no section like India's IT Act s.1(4) and its First Schedule, which exclude wills, trusts, powers of attorney and contracts for the sale of immovable property. The only limits found are:

- **s.9(3)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28820.html)): s.9's rules on keeping records yield to a law that sets its own way of keeping them.
- **s.11** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28822.html)): no government body can be compelled to accept, issue or keep documents electronically.
- **s.87** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-950/section-28898.html)): extends "document" in the Penal Code s.29, the Evidence Act s.3 and the Bankers' Books Evidence Act to electronic ones. **It does not touch the Stamp Act or the General Clauses Act.**

### 2.6 Cyber Security Act 2026 (Act 81 of 2026, 10 April 2026)

([bdlaws act-1710](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1710.html)) This Act repeals and re-enacts the Cyber Security Ordinance 2025. It takes undefined words from the ICT Act (s.2(2)), and its proceedings apply the Evidence Act and the ICT Act (s.42). For the farm one point matters: s.22 makes cheating in cyberspace an offense. Clause (ঙ) includes taking on another person's identity or using their personal information as one's own. Someone entering an Investor's code would commit it, which helps deter such misuse. (The Evidence Act's definition of "digital record" still points to the repealed Digital Security Act 2018. The General Clauses Act s.8 reads such a reference as one to the re-enactment.)

## 3. Evidence Act 1872 as amended by the Evidence (Amendment) Act 2022

(Act 20 of 2022, 20 November 2022: [bdlaws act-1416](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1416.html); consolidated Act: [bdlaws act-24](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24.html))

- **s.3** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-4653.html)):
  - "Document" now "includes any digital record".
  - "Digital record" or "electronic record" includes "records from cell phone, hardware, software or any other digital device".
  - "Digital Signature" or "electronic Signature" means the ICT Act's electronic signature, and a "Digital Signature Certificate" means its electronic signature certificate.
- **ss.65A, 65B** ([65A](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-51388.html), [65B](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-51389.html)):
  - Digital records are proved under s.65B.
  - A computer output (printed, stored or copied) "shall be deemed to be also a document" and is admissible "without further proof or production of the original" if four conditions hold: (a) the computer was regularly used for the activity, (b) such information was regularly fed in, (c) the computer worked properly, (d) the output reproduces what was fed in.
  - A **certificate** identifying the record and how it was produced, "signed by a person occupying a responsible official position", is evidence of those matters (s.65B(4)). For the farm that person is the Owner.
- **s.67A** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-51391.html)): "Except in the case of a secure digital signature, if the digital signature of any subscriber is alleged to have been affixed … the fact that such digital signature is the digital signature of the subscriber must be proved."
- **s.85A** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-51394.html)): the court "shall presume that every digital record purporting to be an agreement containing the digital signatures of the parties was so concluded by affixing the digital signature of the parties". Whether an SMS-code agreement "contains the digital signatures of the parties" turns on §2.1, so this presumption cannot be counted on.
- **s.85B** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-51395.html)):
  - A secure digital record is presumed unaltered.
  - A secure digital signature is presumed affixed by the subscriber with intent to sign.
  - Otherwise "nothing in this section shall create any presumption relating to authenticity and integrity".
- **s.88A** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-51397.html)): the court may presume a digital communication arrived as sent, "but the Court shall not make any presumption as to the persons by whom such message was sent".
- **s.47A**: the Certifying Authority's opinion is relevant to a digital signature. It does not apply here, because there is no CA.
- **s.91** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-24/section-5107.html)): once a contract's terms are "reduced to the form of a document", only the document (or admissible secondary evidence) proves them. This is why an Agreement kept out by the Stamp Act cannot be proved another way.

**What proves an agreement made in the app, then:**

- the agreed paper as it was offered, its fingerprint, and the Offer's number;
- the log of the code being sent, the number it was sent to, when it was entered, and from which sign-in;
- the first meeting, where the NID was checked and the number recorded;
- the Portal Consent, which agreed the procedure;
- the acknowledgment SMS;
- the Owner's s.65B certificate.

What the farm has to prove is that the Investor, not someone holding their phone, entered the code. That is a question of fact, and the record above is what answers it.

## 4. Stamp Act 1899: the hard part

([bdlaws act-77](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77.html); Schedule I as substituted by the Finance Act 2022, [PDF](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/upload/act/2022-07-18-11-25-48-77___Schedule.pdf). The amounts and the Art. 5(c)/Art. 45 question are in [bangladesh-pooled-investment.md §3.1](bangladesh-pooled-investment.md).)

### 4.1 Is an Agreement made in the app chargeable?

- **s.3** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22673.html)): every instrument named in Schedule I that is **executed** in Bangladesh is chargeable.
- **s.2(12)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22677.html)): "executed" and "execution" "mean 'signed' and 'signature'". **s.2(14)**: "instrument" "includes every document by which any right or liability is, or purports to be, created, transferred, limited, extended, extinguished or recorded". **s.2(18)**: "paper" includes "any other material on which an instrument may be written".
- **"Document" is not defined in the Stamp Act.** The General Clauses Act 1897 s.3(16) defines it as matter "written, expressed or described upon any substance" ([bdlaws act-73](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-73.html)). ICT s.87 widened "document" for the Evidence Act and the Penal Code only. ICT s.6 nonetheless lets anything a law requires in writing be electronic.

**Two readings, neither tested:**

- **(A) Chargeable.** The Agreement made in the app is a document (ICT s.6, Evidence Act s.3) and is "executed" by the Investor's electronic act. Section 17 is then broken at the moment of agreeing, and s.35 applies. This is the safer reading to plan on.
- **(B) Not chargeable, or not "executed".** It is not a document "upon any substance". Or, if the SMS-code act is not a signature in law (§2.1), it was never "executed". Either way s.3 does not reach it. The cost of this reading is that it **weakens the farm's proof that the Investor signed anything at all.** The farm cannot argue "it is not signed" to the Collector and "it is signed" to the court.

**India settled the question by statute:** the Indian Stamp Act definition of "instrument" was amended in 2019 to include an electronic record. **Bangladesh has made no such amendment.** The bdlaws Stamp Act shows no change to s.2 after the Finance Act 2013, and the Finance Act 2026 has no Stamp Act chapter ([bdlaws act-1725](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-1725.html)).

### 4.2 Can it be "duly stamped"? Not by any route in force

- **s.2(11)**: "duly stamped" means the instrument "bears an adhesive or impressed stamp of not less than the proper amount". **s.10** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22694.html)): duty "shall be paid, and such payment shall be indicated on such instruments, by means of stamps", unless the Act provides otherwise or the government directs another way by rule.
- **s.11A** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22696.html)), inserted by the Finance Act 2000: challan, pay order or draft, but for **insurance policies** only.
- **Stamp Duties (Additional Modes of Payment) Act 1974** (Act LXXI of 1974, 28 November 1974, as amended by the Finance Act 2000 s.52; scanned on [IRD](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-ird/2024/12/7afde5a3599246fcb3fd2f68738a94e3.pdf)):
  - s.2(1): "where any instrument is required or intended to be registered under the Registration Act, 1908", duty may be paid in cash (≤ Tk 300), "by chalan in a Treasury or Sub-Treasury" or an authorized bank, or by account-payee pay order or draft.
  - s.2(2): on the registering officer's endorsement the instrument "shall be deemed to have been duly stamped".
  - **This covers registration only.** An Investment Agreement is not registered.
- **স্ট্যাম্প শুল্ক পরিশোধ (অতিরিক্ত পদ্ধতি) বিধিমালা, ২০০১** (S.R.O. 222, 13 August 2001, in force 1 September 2001; [IRD PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-ird/2024/12/1fc1a30ad52545dd9dba017f837cfa78.pdf)):
  - made under the 1974 law, and defined by reference to registration at a Sub-Registrar's office (r.2(গ), (ঘ));
  - duty above Tk 1,200 on a **registrable** document goes by pay order to head "1-1101-0020-1311 স্ট্যাম্প প্রশাসন-নন-জুডিসিয়াল" (r.4).
- **The 2010 Rules** (S.R.O. 21, 20 January 2010; [IRD PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-ird/2024/12/4c50cd5aff70435282466ebc5126d7f3.pdf)): company registration and share transfers at RJSC only.
- **IRD's page of stamp laws and rules** ([ird.gov.bd](https://ird.gov.bd/site/page/a564cf7b-072b-4140-964d-76fb5a0e7581/%E0%A6%B7%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%9F%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AE%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%AA-%E0%A6%86%E0%A6%87%E0%A6%A8)) lists the items above, the 2002 insurance-policy rules, the 1987 refund rules and 1953 supply rules. **It lists no e-stamp rule.** A private site's claim that "e-stamping was introduced in Bangladesh in 2019" names no law, operator or verification site ([notarybd](https://www.notarybd.com/e-stamp-faqs.aspx) **[SECONDARY, unreliable]**).
- **A-challan: decided, not gazetted.**
  - **The decision:** IRD decided at a 13 May 2026 meeting, chaired by the NBR chairman, to take non-judicial and insurance stamp duty by A-challan. Both ways are allowed from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027; from 1 July 2027 A-challan is the only way. A gazette was "expected in June" ([TBS, 6 June 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/govt-moves-make-challan-mandatory-stamp-duties-boost-revenue-1455211) **[SECONDARY]**).
  - **The draft:** the challan's printed receipt is attached to the original, the A-challan number is written on its first page, and ordinary cartridge paper or Law Ministry-approved legal paper replaces stamp paper ([Samakal, 7 June 2026](https://samakal.com/economics/article/355604/) **[SECONDARY]**). **It is drafted for paper.**
  - **Separately:** A-challan became mandatory for all government receipts from 1 July 2026 ([Financial Express, 30 June 2026](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/trade/a-challan-made-mandatory-for-all-govt-receipts-from-july-1) **[SECONDARY]**). That governs how money reaches the treasury, not how a stamp is "indicated" on an instrument.
- **The Collector's adjudication, ss.31–32** ([31](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22758.html), [32](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22759.html)):
  - Any instrument, executed or not, may be brought to the Collector (the Deputy Commissioner). The Collector determines the duty and, once it is paid, "shall certify by endorsement on such instrument" that it has been paid.
  - The instrument is then "deemed to be duly stamped" (s.32(3)).
  - **But the Collector cannot endorse an instrument brought more than one month after execution** (proviso (a)). An endorsement is a mark _on_ the instrument.
  - **This is the only route in force that does not depend on registration.** Whether a Collector would endorse a printout of an electronic Agreement was not found anywhere.

### 4.3 What s.35 means for an instrument with no stamp

- **s.35** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22775.html)):
  - No chargeable instrument "shall be admitted in evidence for any purpose by any person having by law or consent of parties authority to receive evidence, or shall be acted upon, registered or authenticated by any such person or by any public officer, unless such instrument is duly stamped".
  - **Proviso (a):** it "shall, subject to all just exceptions, be admitted in evidence on payment of the duty … together with a penalty of five Taka, or, when ten times the amount of the proper duty … exceeds five Taka, … a sum equal to ten times such duty".
  - **Proviso (c):** a contract "effected by correspondence consisting of two or more letters" is duly stamped if any one letter bears the stamp.
- **The cost, worked:**

  | Article (unsettled, see pooled-investment §3.1) | Duty     | Duty + penalty to admit it |
  | ----------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------- |
  | Art. 5(c) agreement not otherwise provided for  | Tk 300   | Tk 3,300                   |
  | Art. 45 partnership, capital ≤ Tk 1 lakh        | Tk 2,000 | Tk 22,000                  |
  | Art. 45 partnership, other                      | Tk 4,000 | Tk 44,000                  |

  If the Collector impounds it instead, the penalty is "not exceeding ten times" (s.40). The Collector may also refund penalty above Tk 5 (s.39).

- **s.36:** once admitted, the admission cannot be questioned later in the same suit on the ground of stamp.
- **s.41** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22781.html)): within one year of execution, a person may bring an unstamped instrument to the Collector of their own motion and pay the duty alone. This applies only if the omission was "occasioned by accident, mistake or urgent necessity". **An unstamped Agreement made in the app by design does not fit those words.**
- **s.62(1)(b)** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22818.html)): "executing or signing otherwise than as a witness any other instrument chargeable with duty without the same being duly stamped" is punishable "with fine which may extend to five hundred Taka" (raised from Tk 400 by the Finance Act 2022). Any s.35 penalty paid is set off. **Under reading (A) this offense would be committed by both parties at each agreement.**
- **Who is barred:** courts, and **the Arbitrator** (a person with authority to receive evidence by consent of parties). A bank is not a "public officer".
- **s.29** ([section](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-77/section-22755.html)): the Agreement may say who bears the stamp's cost. In the absence of agreement, for an instrument not listed in s.29, the person executing it bears it.

### 4.4 The ways the lawyer might choose

1. **Treat the Agreement made in the app as not chargeable** (reading B), and rely on the Contract Act, the ICT Act and the s.65B record. Cheapest. The risk is a court or arbitrator taking reading A, which costs the farm the 11× sum above and the s.62 fine.
2. **Pay the duty anyway and keep the receipt.** This is how the farm's "e-challan" works on paper today. It does not make the instrument "duly stamped" under any rule in force (§4.2), but it shows good faith. It might support a later s.41 application or penalty refund (s.39). Whether the treasury accepts a stamp-duty challan for an unregistered instrument with no endorsement is a practice question.
3. **Adjudication within a month:** print the agreed paper with its trail, take it to the Collector, pay, and get the s.32 endorsement. This is the only route in force that ends in "duly stamped". It brings back the trip the map wants to remove, though for the Owner only, not the Investor.
4. **Wait for the A-challan notification,** and ask that its "indicated on the instrument" rule cover an electronic instrument: the challan number recorded in it, and the receipt kept with it.
5. **Keep paper for the Agreement and the Amendment, and move only the Nomination into the app** (§4.5). This is the map's "paper stays available", made the default for the two chargeable papers.

### 4.5 The three papers, one by one, and a side-finding

- **Investment Agreement:** chargeable on paper (Art. 5(c) or Art. 45). The problem in §4.1–4.3 is its problem.
- **Amendment:** it changes the split and the Target Window of an existing Agreement. As a fresh agreement it would most likely fall under Art. 5(c) (Tk 300). It might instead be argued to be part of the original instrument. **Lawyer.**
- **Nomination (মনোনয়নপত্র):** a one-sided direction naming collectors. It is not an agreement, and no Schedule I article describes it. Chargeability is fixed by Schedule I (s.3), so it is **probably not chargeable**. That is consistent with CONTEXT.md ("Not stamped: it is a letter, not a contract"). If so, nothing in the Stamp Act stands in the way of one agreed in the app. **Lawyer to confirm.**
- **Side-finding about paper Agreements today.** CONTEXT.md says an Agreement's "stamp duty is paid on stamp paper or by e-challan". Under the rules in force (§4.2), a challan makes an instrument "duly stamped" only in two cases: a registering officer endorses it (1974 Act s.2(2)), or a Collector endorses it within a month of execution (s.32). **A paper Agreement paid by e-challan and kept with the challan number, with neither endorsement, may itself be "not duly stamped".** The A-challan notification, once gazetted, may cure this for agreements made after it. **This is for the lawyer now, whatever is decided about the app.**

## 5. What Bangladesh Bank, BFIU and the CCA do (practice, not binding on the farm)

- **e-loan, BRPD-1 Circular No. 11, 11 May 2026** ([bb.org.bd](https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/brpd/may112026brpd-111.pdf)), issued under Bank Company Act 1991 s.45, in force immediately:
  - small loans up to Tk 50,000 for 12 months, handled end to end digitally (para 2(ঘ));
  - **para 2(ঙ):** "instead of taking signatures (wet signature) on the loan application, sanction letter and charge documents, the customer's consent shall be taken with identity confirmed through biometric data collected digitally";
  - **para 2(ঢ):** onboarding through the bank's approved platform or the customer's registered SIM, with **OTP and 2FA/MFA** to confirm identity;
  - para 2(ঝ): loan features must be explained before consent.
  - **The circular says nothing about stamp duty on those charge documents.**
- **e-KYC, BRPD-1 Circular No. 08, 11 March 2026, in force 1 September 2026** ([bb.org.bd](https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/brpd/mar112026brpd-108.pdf)), replacing BFIU Circular 25 of 8 January 2020 for banks, finance companies and payment providers:
  - **Signature (onboarding step five):** "either electronic (signature using devices), digital, or image of wet signature … shall be preserved". "A digital signature or a Personal Identification Number (PIN) may be generated and used if the customer is unable to provide a wet signature. However, the use of digital signature or PIN is only allowed for low-risk accounts whereas wet or electronic signatures must be provided for high-risk accounts."
  - **OTP (§3.2.5):** a security measure, "checking the registered phone number (SIM)/email through one-time PIN code".
  - **Logs (§5.1):** the "digital footprint and the log" must be kept, and geo-location and IP addresses may be collected.
- **e-KYC for insurers and capital-market intermediaries, BFIU Circular No. 29, 30 March 2026** ([bfiu.org.bd](https://www.bfiu.org.bd/pdf/circular/aml/mar302026bfiu29e.pdf)), to be implemented by 31 December 2026. It has the same signature rule ("Using a digital signature or PIN will only be applicable for low-risk accounts"), and the OTP is used "checking the phone number".
- **CCA remote e-Sign:** "3 of the 8 licensed Certifying Authorities of Bangladesh are providing remote signature aka e-Sign to user level". BCC CA's flow is a phone OTP, then an NID photo, a selfie matched against the NID server, a PIN and device registration ([CCA e-Sign user manual](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-cca/2024/12/ac971233c56d4ee3b0c3cedfd3c37b01.pdf), undated, uploaded December 2024). The map put certificate signatures out of scope. This is the cheap way to a "subscriber" signature, and with it the presumptions in Evidence Act ss.67A, 85B and 85C, if the lawyer asks for one.

**The pattern to show the lawyer:** where a regulator lets consent replace a wet signature, it asks for identity proved by biometrics (e-loan) or keeps a signature in some form (e-KYC). The SMS code always proves the channel. A PIN stands in for a signature only at low risk. The farm's design has the identity proved in person at the first meeting, and the code only proves the channel afterwards. That fits the pattern, but no regulator's text approves it.

## 6. What this means for OpenFarm

**Constraints the spec should respect:**

1. **The Portal Consent should carry the agreed procedure** (ICT s.13(3)(ক), s.17(1) "with the consent of the parties"). In words the lawyer approves, it should say that an offer agreed from the Investor's own sign-in, with a code sent to the number recorded at the first meeting, is their acceptance. It should also say how they tell the farm a code was not theirs (s.13(4)).
2. **Bind the code to the paper** (s.2(1)(ঈ), s.17(2)): put the Offer's number, or a short form of the `paperHash`, in the SMS. Verify the code against that hash only. Never let a code agree to a paper laid out after it was sent.
3. **Acknowledge** (s.14): after the Investor agrees, send an SMS naming the paper and the day. Make the agreed paper downloadable from the portal, so the Investor holds an independent copy.
4. **Keep the trail s.65B needs:** code sent (number, time, gateway reference), code entered (time, sign-in, IP), the paper and its hash, and the Owner's approval. The Owner signs the s.65B(4) certificate if it is ever needed.
5. **Record how duty was met, honestly.** An Agreement made in the app must not show a stamp it does not have. The stamp line already says "agreed in the app". Whatever the lawyer chooses (§4.4) belongs there: paid by challan (number), adjudicated (date), or none.
6. **Do not turn on the Agreement switch on this research.** It stays the Owner's after the lawyer answers. The Nomination is the paper this research finds least obstructed.

## Take to the lawyer

1. **Chargeability:** is an Investment Agreement or Amendment agreed in the app an "instrument" that has been "executed" (Stamp Act ss.2(12), 2(14), 3)? Which reading (§4.1 A or B) should the farm plan on?
2. **Duty on an electronic instrument:** is there any lawful way today to make one "duly stamped"? Would the Collector adjudicate and endorse a printout under ss.31–32? Will the A-challan notification, once gazetted, cover electronic instruments?
3. **The farm's paper "e-challan" today:** does paying an unregistered Agreement's duty by challan, with no registering officer's or Collector's endorsement, leave it "not duly stamped" (§4.5)? If so, what should the farm do about Agreements already signed that way?
4. **The s.35 risk accepted knowingly:** may the farm deliberately run Agreements made in the app unstamped and pay duty plus ten times later if a dispute comes? What does s.62 mean for the Owner, and for the Investor? Does it matter that the Arbitrator, not a court, is the first forum?
5. **The SMS code:** under ICT ss.2(1), 5, 7 and 17, is it an "electronic signature", or a "secure" one, given that the farm's server knows the code? Or should the farm rely on attribution (s.13(3)(ক)) through the Portal Consent? Approve the Portal Consent's wording for the agreed procedure.
6. **Nomination:** confirm it is not chargeable, and that one agreed in the app by the Investor binds the farm's payment to the Nominees as a signed paper would.
7. **Amendment:** Art. 5(c), or part of the original instrument?
8. **A minor's Receiver:** may a Receiver consent by an SMS code to a number given at the first meeting, without a portal account?
9. **Remote e-Sign** through a licensed CA (§5): worth it for the Agreement alone, to gain the "subscriber" presumptions?
