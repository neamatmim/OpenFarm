# How pooled cattle-fattening investment schemes are structured (Bangladesh first; then Indonesia, Malaysia, Pakistan)

**Question:** How do existing schemes structure a pooled cattle-fattening investment? That means the unit of investment, the profit and loss split, which costs fall on the farm and which on the pool, animal death, unsold cattle, early exit, projects that are not fully funded, what investors are sent, and how they are paid. The farm wants its first contract to start from known practice.

**Researched:** 17 September 2026. Primary sources were preferred: the schemes' own pages, FAQs, terms and contract text, plus statutes and regulator pages. Pages that are gone were read from the Internet Archive (links below go to the dated capture). News, blogs and opinion pieces are marked **[SECONDARY]**. **[SNIPPET]** means only a search-engine extract was seen, because the page rendered client-side or blocked fetching. Where a scheme does not publish a figure, this note says "not published". No percentage here is an estimate.

**Not legal advice.** Section 4 lists Bangladeshi statutes that bear on raising money from many investors. A lawyer should read them before any money is taken.

**Headline:** Published schemes fall into three families:

1. **Profit-sharing pools (Mudarabah-style).** Examples: iFarmer's Shariah farms, WeGro, Freshie Farm "Project Borga", DeenAgro, nusaQu, MyLembu.
   - The investor provides all of the capital for animals and running costs. The farm or platform manages.
   - The profit is split. The investor's share is **30–50% of profit** wherever a figure is published.
   - A loss falls on the investors unless the manager was negligent.
2. **Fixed-return or cost-plus deals.** Examples: iFarmer's 2020 per-cow offers, biniyog.io/Ahlan Agro (Murabaha), DanaHub/Digiternak (murabahah), Investify's post-dated cheques.
   - The farm owes the investor a fixed amount, often secured by collateral or cheques.
   - This is debt, not a shared venture.
3. **Fixed monthly-return "agro" schemes.** Examples: Nazran in Bangladesh, Ponorogo in Indonesia, FURT in Pakistan.
   - Every one found **collapsed or ended in arrests**.

Within the first family, some terms are almost always published: the unit, the split, "investors bear loss", a certificate at the start, periodic photo or weight updates, and a single payout at the end to a bank account. Almost nothing published covers **unsold cattle, early exit, or a project that is not fully funded**. Those are the forks the farm must decide itself. **nusaQu** (Indonesia) is the only scheme found with a rule for unsold animals: the farm buys them at the Eid price. **WeGro** is the only one with a real early-exit rule.

## 1. Bangladesh: agri-crowdfunding platforms

### 1.1 iFarmer (i-Farmer Limited), 2019 to now

iFarmer is a retail "farm funding" platform (called "sponsorship" in 2019). In April 2026 the investment app was renamed **iharvst**, and it still takes retail funders ([iharvst product page](https://www.ifarmer.asia/product-iharvst), read 17 Sep 2026). The current [Terms of Service](https://www.ifarmer.asia/terms) list section headings such as "Consequence of Death and Legal Incapacity" and "Refund policy", but the body text is rendered client-side and could not be read. The cattle terms below are from iFarmer's own pages as archived. iFarmer ran **two different cattle structures**, which makes it the most useful single precedent.

#### (a) Per-cow offers, 2020–2022: fixed, then "projected", return

- **Unit: one cow.**
  - _Cow Farm Lalmonirhat-3_: BDT 40,000 "/cow", 12 months, "Return 22.0%", net profit BDT 8,800 ([archived 9 Aug 2020](https://web.archive.org/web/20200809113312/https://ifarmer.asia/farms/cow-farm-lalmonirhat-3)).
  - _Exclusive Cattle Farm 6_: BDT 55,000 "/unit", 12 months, 18% ([archived 24 Jan 2022](https://web.archive.org/web/20220124094952/https://ifarmer.asia/farms/exclusive-cattle-farm-6?locale=en)).
  - Each page states breed, sex (bull), average purchase weight (80 kg; 100–120 kg), source ("Local Cattle Market") and growth timeline.
- **Maximum:**
  - Lalmonirhat-3: "a maximum of 10 cows or on shares worth maximum BDT 4,00,000".
  - Exclusive 6: "a maximum of 4 cows or on shares worth maximum BDT 220,000".
- **Fixed or variable:**
  - 2020 page: "No, it's fixed return we are offering to our funders."
  - 2022 page, same question: "No, it's projected earning."
- **What the money covers:** "Team iFarmer purchases the cow for you, bears the cost of management, provides insurance for death or loss, maintains the farm, grass … supplementary feed and, medicine and vaccinations." Cows were reared by contract farmers in Lalmonirhat.
- **Allocation:** "you will be allocated a calf and issued an ownership certificate bearing your name and the unique number of your calf".
- **Death or theft:** the page contradicts itself.
  - The cost list says iFarmer "provides insurance for death or loss".
  - The FAQ says that where a loss cannot be avoided, "we will deliver relevant supporting documents (such-postmortem report) to justify your loss", and it "strongly advise[s]" funders to insure with Green Delta.
- **Cooling-off:** "iFarmer allows you to take back your funding within 1 week with 100% refund."
- **Payout:** principal plus return "in your designated bank account" at the end of the cycle.

#### (b) General funder terms

Source: [Terms of Services, archived 17 Sep 2021](https://web.archive.org/web/20210917025532/https://ifarmer.asia/en/terms_of_services).

- **What the funder pays for:** "costs related to the lease of land for farming, seedling/livestock, fertilizers, maintenance costs, and the cost of supervision and administration. All costs are included in your funding contribution."
- **Lock-in:** the contribution "is tied and unavailable for the entire duration of the farming cycle and neither iFarmer nor the farmers can or will refund your contribution until the successful completion of a farming cycle".
- **Split:** profits "shared between the Funder, the farmer and iFarmer in a 40%:40%:20% ratio respectively."
- **No ownership:** "You acknowledge that you do not own any of the farms cultivated and are only entitled to the proceeds".
- **Loss:** "Subject to the Terms of Insurance and any insurance claims settled … the Company has no obligation to reimburse or indemnify you for any cost, loss or liability which you incur as a result of the failure of a farm to yield according to expectations."
  - The funder also warrants they understand "the possibility of losing all the money you contribute".
- **Payout:** profit goes to an in-platform wallet at the end of the cycle. The funder can withdraw it to a Bangladeshi bank account in their own name, or put it into a new cycle.
- **Refund of a payment:** within 7 working days of the invoice, less a 2.5% transaction fee, bank to bank only.
- **Disputes:** Bangladeshi law; arbitration if 30 days of negotiation fail.

From the [FAQ, archived 17 Sep 2021](https://web.archive.org/web/20210917025828/https://ifarmer.asia/faqs?locale=en):

- **Documents:** a money receipt or invoice at payment. Then a "Farm funding Certificate" or "Certificate of ownership" arrives by email "within the following weeks", stating the tenure and end date.
- **Updates:** "written reports, pictures and videos"; the FAQ offers monthly updates for cycles longer than 6 months. Each funder gets a named farm manager and may visit the farm with notice.
- **Cycle:** 4–12 months.
- **Tax:** the [2019 FAQ](https://web.archive.org/web/20191112105725/https://ifarmer.asia/faqs) says tax deducted at source (TDS) comes off the profit only, and a TDS certificate is issued.
- **Loss:** the 2019 FAQ said iFarmer "will try our level best to compensate for your loss using the available benevolent fund".

#### (c) "Premium Shariah Compliant Cow Farm 2": Mudarabah, per share, pool of 20 cattle

Source: [archived 30 Nov 2022](https://web.archive.org/web/20221130100236/https://ifarmer.asia/farms/premium-shariah-compliant-cow-farm-2?locale=en).

- **Unit:** BDT 135,000 "/share", 6 months, "Estimated Earning 7.5% to 12.5%", maximum "2 shares/booking".
- **Contract:**
  - The funder is rabb-al-maal and provides "100 percent of the capital". iFarmer is mudarib and holds the management rights.
  - "The provider of capital has no control over the management of the project."
- **Loss:**
  - "If the business incurs a loss, it will be borne by Rabal-Maal (Financier) if there happens no negligence on the part of Mudarib (iFarmer)."
  - The financier "is not liable for the losses beyond the capital he has contributed."
- **Pool:**
  - "Each Farm contains 20 cattle", all bought within one month; the farm goes live on a set date.
  - "the funder gets a profit portion based on the return of the overall portfolio of 20 cattle of each farm, not on specific cattle."
  - Shariah-farm money is kept "separately through Shariah Compliant Banks."
- **Split:**
  - "Financiers get investment capital and 40% of the profit or loss if made. iFarmer gets 60% of the profit or loss including profit or loss sharing with farmers."
  - The rearing farmer is a sub-partner under a separate agreement with iFarmer.
  - The page's generic FAQ gives a different split: farmer 50%, funder 40%, iFarmer 10%. Its worked example is a cow bought at 45,000 and sold at 65,000.
- **Timing:** cattle sold within 5–6 months of purchase; return paid to the funder's bank account "in exactly 06 month from the date of payment."
- **Death or theft:** "we will try our level best to compensate your loss using the available benevolent fund". This is discretionary.
- **Exit in kind:** "Can I keep the funded cattle for myself? No … iFarmer works on ground level with bulk buyers only."

#### (d) What else is known

- **iFarmer's own [MIT Solve submission](https://solve.mit.edu/challenges/tiger-challenge-bangladesh/solutions/14638), 18 Sep 2019:** an "upfront service charge of BDT 3K for every cattle been sponsored", plus a share of profit after the sale. That is **a fee first, then a split**. By 2021 the published terms show only a profit split.
- **[Future Startup, 15 Nov 2020](https://futurestartup.com/2020/11/15/ifarmer-enabling-investment-and-creating-wealth-through-farming-and-agriculture/) [SECONDARY]:**
  - Bangladesh has no P2P or crowdfunding regulation. iFarmer had sent Bangladesh Bank a concept note and treated the model as traditional share-cropping needing no licence.
  - One farm's underperformance could be "cover[ed] … from other farms" in the portfolio.
  - Insurance (Green Delta, Pragati) was optional, typically taken by investors putting in BDT 200,000 or more.
- **[TBS, 17 Jan 2020](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/agriculture/ifarmer-bridge-between-farmers-and-capital-37129) [SECONDARY]:** minimum hold 4 months; monthly dashboard updates with photos; the insurance premium was described as high.
- **Regulator action or default:** none found.

### 1.2 WeGro (WeGro Technologies Limited), 2021 to now

**Sources:**

- WeGro's public FAQ data that feeds [wegro.global/faq](https://www.wegro.global/faq) (JSON at `https://api.admin.wegro.global/api/faqs`, read 17 Sep 2026; the page renders answers client-side).
- The [projects page](https://www.wegro.global/projects).
- The [Terms & Conditions](https://www.wegro.global/terms).

**Terms:**

- **Contract forms:**
  - "Mudaraba … profit-sharing modality with pre-agreed terms on the share of profits. Applicable mostly on rearing and harvesting projects."
  - "Murabaha … a fixed profit is negotiated earlier. We keep a certain service charge and share the rest of the profit with financiers. Applicable mostly on trade-based projects."
- **Unit:** a fixed unit per project; "Investment/unit starts from BDT 5,000".
  - The only cattle project live on 17 Sep 2026 is [Cattle Trade – 33](https://www.wegro.global/projects/486) in Sirajganj: BDT 200,000 per unit, 4 months, "Variable Return", projected 6.1%–8%, 54 units, capital and profit paid together at the end.
- **The investor does not own animals:** "Am I buying land, poultry, or livestock here? No … You will be financing a portion of available agricultural projects."
- **Split:** "Initially, the total profit of the project is calculated. 40% of the total profit is allocated for our investors."
  - In 2022 the press reported a farmer–investor–WeGro split of 45-45-10 ([Daily Star, 13 Oct 2022](https://www.thedailystar.net/tech-startup/news/wegro-technologies-breath-fresh-air-farmers-and-investors-3142406) [SECONDARY]).
- **Loss:** "investors also bear the associated business risks, including potential losses. WeGro does not guarantee returns or bear investment losses."
  - The only insurance mentioned covers the _farmer's_ death.
  - After "unavoidable circumstances" WeGro "will provide relevant supporting documents to justify your loss."
- **Early exit (Terms, "Modality"):**
  - "In case of premature closure of a project by a financier; no profit will be entitled to the investor."
  - "A period of 30 working days has to be provided to ensure replacement of finance."
  - Closure before 20% of the tenure: full refund, no charges.
  - Closure after 20%: "a service charge of 2% of the financed amount or BDT 2,500 (whichever is lower)".
- **Booking:** payment is due within 15 business days of booking. No rule for under-subscribed projects was found.
- **Documents:**
  - A "Project Financing Certificate" and invoice by email.
  - A stamped agreement on request: free up to BDT 300,000, a BDT 360 stamp charge above that.
  - A money receipt for cheques.
- **Updates:** on request, through the app ("My Farm" → "Get Updates").
  - Other WeGro project pages promise weekly field-officer visits, photos and geotagging. The cattle project page does not.
- **Money in:** account-payee cheque or pay order, BEFTN/RTGS, or bank deposit.
- **Money out:** to a Bangladeshi bank account "within 3 to 6 business days after the project is completed" (the "settlement period").
  - No monthly profit except on instalment projects.
  - A bank account is mandatory. Non-resident Bangladeshis may invest but are paid only into a Bangladeshi account.
  - A non-cattle WeGro project page also mentions payout to a "mobile financial account".
- **Regulator action or default:** none found.

### 1.3 Other Bangladeshi agri-investment platforms

**Investify.fund, "Agro: Qurbani Eid Cattle Project 2026", Natore** ([project page](https://www.investify.fund/product/agro-investment-qurbani-2026/)):

- **Unit:** "BDT 20,000 (1 Share)"; the page states both minimum and maximum as ৳20,000. Target BDT 40,00,000; 93.25% raised.

**biniyog.io / Ahlan Agro** ([Ahlan Agro 3](https://biniyog.io/campaign/ahlan-agro-3/), [Ahlan Agro 2](https://biniyog.io/campaign/ahlan-agro-2/), both [SNIPPET]):

- **Structure:** Bai-Murabaha, not profit sharing. Ahlan Agro "buys bulls from investors at a markup price and pays after Eid-ul Adha". biniyog acts as the investors' agent (Wakalah).
- **Return:** round 3, 5% for 4 months; round 2, 15.5% over 11 months, "completed repayment in Jul'22".
- **Platform:** minimum Tk 5,000.
- **[Future Startup, 7 May 2026](https://futurestartup.com/2026/05/07/the-biniyog-io-story/) [SECONDARY]:**
  - About 80% of campaigns are Murabaha. Contracts are on stamp paper. The non-performing rate is 3.75%.
  - One borrower repaid "nearly two years after the original schedule". Cases were filed under the Negotiable Instruments Act, i.e. over bounced cheques.
  - The founders say they operate "without a formal crowdfunding or marketplace lending framework in Bangladesh".

**Shohoz Fintech** ([sfl.com.bd/invest](https://sfl.com.bd/invest), [SNIPPET]): cattle, fish and goat Shariah projects "with up to 25% returns"; "50-50 profit sharing model between investors and farmers"; money locked until the project ends.

## 2. Bangladesh: farm-run cattle investment offers

### 2.1 Freshie Farm, "Project Borga" (Porsha, Naogaon): the closest match to our model

Source: [freshie.farm/project-borga](https://www.freshie.farm/project-borga/), in Bengali, read 17 Sep 2026; English below is a paraphrase.

- **Name and idea:** "বর্গা" (borga) is the village practice of giving a cow to someone else to rear for a share. Freshie calls this "bringing village cow-borga online".
- **Unit:** a fixed share of **Tk 50,000**. Investors buy "as many Tk 50,000 shares as needed".
- **Batch:**
  - Batch 13 runs **August 2026 to 28 February**, a 7-month project.
  - Payment "starts from 15 March, Inshallah".
  - Target 600 cattle; "once the new target is filled we will stop taking investment for this batch".
- **Pooling:** "a share-based investment in the project; everyone's investment is combined to buy cattle. The profit is likewise combined, averaged and shared."
- **Profit:** after all the batch's cattle are bought and sold and costs are deducted, profit is averaged "and distributed equally among that batch's shareholders".
  - "If we guaranteed a %, that would become interest (সুদ). We do not want to pay or take interest."
- **Costs:**
  - Charged to the pool: "the cattle's feed, staff salaries, treatment, electricity bill will come from this fund."
  - Paid by the farm: "shed and permanent structures are Freshie Farm's cost."
- **Death:** "since these are living animals … a cow may die. We run batch-wise, where everyone's share is a unit. If a cow dies, it is accounted for across the whole batch." **The pool bears it.** No insurance is mentioned.
- **Reporting:**
  - A web app with an investor profile, "weight gain of our cattle and other updates", and investor documents (amount, nominee, payment information).
  - Viewing the cattle online "any time" is "work in progress".
- **Payout:** "principal with profit returned to your bank account."
- **Deposit:** by bank; investors upload payment proof in the app ([how-to page](https://www.freshie.farm/how-to-invest-in-project-borga-as-a-new-investor/)).
- **Track record:** the Batch 11 summary shows "5170 টাকা লাভ", "10.5 কোটি+" and "৬০০ গরু". The page does not say what the 5,170 is per; if it is per Tk 50,000 share, that would be about 10% over the batch, but this is not stated.
- **Not published:** the farm's own share of profit or any management fee; how a loss (selling below cost) is handled; unsold cattle; early exit; an under-filled batch; audited accounts.
- **Legal:** only a trade licence (TRAD/DNCC/001842/2024) is shown. The site's terms cover the e-commerce shop, not the investment.

### 2.2 DeenAgro (Shibganj, Bogura; founded 2024): a published Mudarabah contract summary

Source: [deenagro.com](https://deenagro.com/), the section "মুদারাবা চুক্তি", described as "approved by IFA Consultancy". Clause numbers are the site's own; English is a paraphrase.

| Clause | Text (paraphrased) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 Parties | 1st party = mudarib (manages the business); 2nd party = sahib-ul-maal (investor). |
| 3 Term | A fixed period "e.g. 1 year, 6 months"; can be extended in writing by mutual consent. |
| 5 Management | "Full control of the business rests with the 1st party", who "shall regularly report income and expenditure to the 2nd party." |
| 6 Collateral (optional) | By agreement the mudarib may give a cheque or asset as security. The investor may recover a loss from it; it is returned at the end of the term. |
| 7 Profit | "In an agreed ratio (e.g. 60% : 40%)". An "estimated monthly profit (optional) may be given, but is not final". The final account is at year end on actual income and expenditure. |
| 8 Loss | "In a normal business loss, the 2nd party bears the loss in proportion to capital. If fault or negligence of the 1st party is proven, he must compensate." |
| 9 Termination | Not before the term ends. Earlier only by mutual written notice. Profit or loss is shared at the end. |
| 11 Fraud | If fraud or breach of trust is proven, the investor may cash the collateral and take legal action. |
| 14 Disputes | Arbitrators nominated by both parties; Arbitration Act 2001 if needed. |
| 15 Death | The contract is not cancelled by a party's death. If the investor dies, the nominee or heirs step in. |
| 16 Zakat | Paid by the investor. |

The home page also claims "12%+ annual return", "500+ active investors", a quarterly Shariah audit and monthly profit distribution. These are unverified. The contract says nothing about animal death, insurance, unsold cattle or the payout channel.

## 3. Bangladesh: failures and enforcement

All of these are **[SECONDARY]** news or opinion.

- **Nazran Fisheries & Agro Project, Dhaka:**
  - Promised Tk 3,000 a month per Tk 1 lakh, "doubling in 33 months". Paid early returns, then closed its office.
  - About Tk 400 crore taken from about 15,000 people. The Criminal Investigation Department (CID) arrested one member on 5 May 2026 ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/crime/cid-arrests-suspect-tk400cr-investment-scam-1430601); [Bangla Tribune](https://www.banglatribune.com/others/945233/)).
  - **Why it failed:** a fixed monthly return paid out of new deposits.
- **Sothostha Food / Priya Women's Society, Pabna:**
  - Moved into cattle and meat and promised Tk 10,000 a month per Tk 1 lakh; about Tk 10 crore from 100+ people.
  - No police action, for lack of a written complaint ([Samakal, 27 Jul 2024](https://samakal.com/whole-country/article/247809/)).
- **Destiny-2000 / Destiny Tree Plantation:**
  - Sold shares in an agricultural asset (trees) through a multi-level marketing (MLM) network.
  - Leaders sentenced to 12 years in 2022 and 2025 ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/crime-and-law/destinys-rafiqul-sentenced-to-12-years-in-prison); [TBS, 15 Jan 2025](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/court/misappropriating-tk25778cr-destiny-md-among-19-sentenced-12-years-prison-1043386)).
- **Jubok:**
  - Bangladesh Bank served notice in 2006 over taking deposits from the public and ordered them returned.
  - A later probe found Tk 2,147 crore owed to 2.67 lakh claimants ([Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news-detail-172259)).
- **"এগ্রিভেঞ্চার" (Agriventure):** an opinion column alleges that a platform of this name ruined about 70 small investors, that "about 50 crore taka" was embezzled, and that 50–70 crore taka a year moves through unregulated agri-crowdfunding ([Digi Bangla, 29 Apr 2026](https://digibanglatech.news/175564), opinion column [SECONDARY]).
  - These claims are **not corroborated**, and the column does not identify the company further. Do not confuse it with similarly named platforms.
- **Not investor schemes, but often cited:**
  - Sadeeq Agro sold local cattle as foreign breeds, leading to a CID money-laundering case ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/crime/cid-investigate-sadeeq-agro-owner-illegal-cattle-import-and-money-laundering)).
  - Evaly and Alesha Mart Qurbani cows were advance-paid retail sales ([New Age](https://www.newagebd.net/article/163028/alesha-mart-customers-start-getting-refund)).

No regulator notice was found against iFarmer, WeGro, Freshie Farm, DeenAgro, Investify or biniyog.io.

## 4. Bangladesh: the legal frame for pooling investors

These are statute texts, read on bdlaws unless noted. They are here so the contract is not designed in a vacuum; whether they apply to our structure is a lawyer's question.

- **Companies Act 1994, s.4** ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-788/section-32008.html)):
  - **4(2):** unless registered as a company (or formed under another law), no company, association or partnership "of more than twenty persons" may be formed to carry on any non-banking business for profit.
  - **4(4):** every member of an entity that breaches this is personally liable for its debts.
  - **4(5):** each member can be fined up to Tk 5,000.
  - **This bears directly on a pool of more than 20 investors** if it is structured as a partnership (Musharakah) rather than as investors contracting individually with the farm.
- **Bank Company Act 1991** ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-751.html)):
  - **s.5:** "banking business" includes taking deposits from the public "repayable on demand or otherwise".
  - **s.31(1):** no one may do banking business without a Bangladesh Bank licence.
  - **ss.51–53:** Bangladesh Bank can investigate, publicly declare an entity an illegal banking business, and order it to stop.
  - A **guaranteed principal or fixed return** makes a scheme look far more like a deposit.
- **Multi-Level Marketing Activities (Control) Act 2013** ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-1128.html)):
  - MLM (commission or profit promised through two or more levels of a network) needs a government licence.
  - s.15 bans pyramid-style selling.
  - Penalty: 5–10 years' imprisonment and up to Tk 50 lakh fine (ss.24, 26).
  - **Relevance: never pay investors a referral commission.**
- **Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC):**
  - The Alternative Investment Rules 2015 allow private placement only, a minimum of Tk 10 lakh per investor, eligible investors only, and at most 200 investors ([rules PDF](https://sec.gov.bd/storage/laws/Notification_24.05.20221.pdf)). That does not fit a retail cattle pool.
  - **No crowdfunding rules were found** in BSEC's list of acts and rules ([sec.gov.bd](https://sec.gov.bd/act-rules-and-regulations), checked 17 Sep 2026).
  - Both iFarmer (2020) and biniyog.io (2026) say publicly that no crowdfunding framework exists.

## 5. Indonesia, Malaysia, Pakistan

Bangladesh already gave a usable spread, so this section adds only what Bangladesh lacked: a rule for unsold animals, a mutual death pool, a published failure with actual loss figures, and regulator action.

### 5.1 nusaQu (PT Nusa Sentosa Indonesia, Bogor): per-animal Qurban fattening

**Sources:** the [partnership page](https://nusaqu.id/kemitraan) and [Q&A](https://nusaqu.id/qna), both read, plus the "Prospektus 2025" PDF linked from the partnership page ([Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1QekFiq_1aIMM0u8QdpH4VR3QVgUHRZBd/view)). The PDF facts come from a research agent's reading of it.

- **Unit:** one whole animal. Cattle "Min. Investasi 1 Ekor (± Rp 15 Juta)"; sheep or goat about Rp 2 juta. The maximum is limited only by pen capacity (prospectus).
- **Split:** "Bagi hasil 70:30 (Manajemen:Mitra)", i.e. management 70, investor 30.
  - The prospectus breaks the 70 into "40% operasional" and "30% nusaQu".
  - A reseller fee (Rp 500k, 750k or 1.5m per head by weight band; also on the Q&A page) is deducted before profit.
- **Farm side:** vets, pen team and marketing. Feed and medicine are not itemised.
- **Death:** "Asuransi Gotong Royong", a self-run mutual pool, not a licensed insurer: "Setiap hewan terjamin oleh sistem asuransi mandiri nusaQu".
  - The investor pays a per-head premium when choosing the animal: Rp 150k, 200k or 250k by weight band (prospectus).
  - A dead, lost or stolen animal is replaced. A sick animal that must be slaughtered is topped up to its value. Any money left in the pool after Eid goes to the company's school.
  - Investors receive three documents: a cooperation agreement, a certificate of animal ownership, and the animal's insurance policy (partnership page).
- **Unsold at Eid:** the farm "will buy the cattle … at the Eid price" (prospectus). **This is the only published unsold-animal rule found.**
- **Target weight:** the Q&A guarantees the Qurban weight band; an under-weight animal is swapped for one in the right band.
- **Early exit:** not allowed (prospectus).
- **Reporting:** app with photo, ID code, breed, health history and **monthly weight**; ear or neck tag; e-signed contract with digital stamp duty; pen visits "24 jam".
- **Payout:** within 2 weeks of Eid (prospectus). The channel is not published.
- **Regulator:** no registration with Indonesia's financial regulator (OJK) is mentioned.

### 5.2 MyLembu (Malaysia): per-calf, farm bears all running costs

Source: [mylembu.com/our-model](https://mylembu.com/our-model/), read.

- **Unit:** one calf at RM2,500, one-time payment, 12 months.
- **Worked example:** sale RM4,500, gain RM2,000. "Farming fee of 50%" = RM1,000; "mylembu.com fee of 20%" = RM400; "Balance for the Member of 30%" = RM600, i.e. "24%" over 12 months.
- **Farm covers:** "We will nourish and take care of your calf at our own expenses including rising, feeding, sheltering in our cowshed, provide medical care with 24/7 security".
  - **This is the structure where the farm bears feed and medicine and takes the larger share.**
- **Exit in kind:** the investor can take the meat instead of a cash sale.
- **Not published:** death, unsold cattle, early exit, reporting, payout channel, company or licence.
- **Regulator lists:** not found on the Bank Negara Malaysia consumer alert list or the Securities Commission investor alert list (research agent, 17 Sep 2026).

### 5.3 Vestifarm (Indonesia): slot-based, now defunct; a documented loss

All **[SECONDARY]**. vestifarm.com is now an unrelated gambling site.

- **Unit:** each cow split into 6 slots ([Kontan, 2 Aug 2017](https://industri.kontan.co.id/news/membiakkan-dana-di-peternakan-sapi-vestifarm)).
- **Split:** investor and Vestifarm 50% each; the farmer paid a fixed Rp 6–7 juta per cycle (Kontan).
- **Death:** insured with Jasindo (a state insurer) at 85% of sale value (Kontan).
- **Reporting:** promised monthly weight and photos. A 2019 investor received only three updates in three months and complained ([tulisandokter](https://www.tulisandokter.com/post/pengalaman-investasi-vestifarm)).
- **Loss:**
  - A 2017 Qurban project lost **38.13% of capital** when the post-Eid market was weak.
  - The loss was "compensated" with vouchers usable only for future investment, not cash ([investor blog](https://vannashara.wordpress.com/2017/12/26/rugi-investasi-di-vestifarm/)).
  - In 2020 funded businesses defaulted and payouts stopped (tulisandokter).
- **Regulator:** described as "masih dalam proses pendaftaran OJK, belum terdaftar", i.e. still applying to OJK and not registered ([wisatadidunia](https://wisatadidunia.com/vestifarm/)).
- **Lessons:** a Qurban-timed sale window that is missed turns straight into a capital loss, and non-cash compensation destroys trust.

### 5.4 DanaHub / Digiternak (Indonesia): murabahah with land collateral

Source: [project page](https://www.danahub.id/business/pendanaan-digiternak-bag-1-qurban-1444-h).

## 6. What Mudarabah standards say

If our contract is called Mudarabah or Musharakah, investors who care about the label will test it against standards like these.

**Bank Negara Malaysia, Shariah Standard on Mudarabah** (BNM/RH/GL 012-4, 2012; [copy](https://www.islamicfinance.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/shariah_std_mudarabah.pdf); quoted by the research agent). "S" clauses are binding; "PG" clauses are practice guidance. BNM's later policy document may supersede it (not verified).

- **S 13.2:** "Loss shall be borne by the rabbul mal up to the capital value."
- **S 13.3:** the mudarib is not liable "unless such loss is due to the mudarib's misconduct (ta`addi), negligence (taqsir) or breach of specified terms."
- **S 13.4:** "in the case of loss the mudarib shall furnish the reason for the occurrence of loss." **This is the basis for a settlement statement that explains a loss.**
- **S 13.5:** with several investors, loss is borne "proportionate to his capital contribution."
- **S 11.10, S 14.1:** the mudarib shall not guarantee capital or profit.
- **PG 14.2:** collateral may be taken, but called only on misconduct or negligence; a third-party guarantee is allowed.
- **PG 11.15, S 11.16:** direct expenses may be charged to the venture if "identifiable and measurable".
- **S 12.19:** the mudarib "shall not earn any additional fee" for work integral to the venture.
- **S 12.6, S 12.7:** no fixed profit amount, and no profit as a percentage of capital.
- **PG 12.25:** lower or no profit on early withdrawal is allowed if agreed at the start.

**Turkish participation banks association (TKBB), Mudarabah Standard No. 5** ([PDF](https://tkbb.org.tr/upload/Mudarabah%20Standard_ENG.pdf)):

- **2.4.6:** the capital provider bears the loss, and "any agreement to the contrary is not permissible".
- **2.3.6 / 2.3.9:** the manager pays from their own funds for hired help doing the manager's own work, and gets nothing beyond the profit share.

**AAOIFI Shari'ah Standard No. 13 (Mudarabah):** exists ([AAOIFI](https://aaoifi.com/s-13-mudarabah/?lang=en)), but the text is paywalled and was not read.

**What this means for our model:** under these standards the farm, as manager, cannot charge the pool a separate management fee for its own work. Shed, staff and utilities the farm provides are its side of the bargain and are paid for through its profit share. Direct, measurable costs (cattle, feed, medicine, transport, haat fees) can be charged to the pool. Freshie Farm's practice of charging "staff salaries" and "electricity" to the pool is the looser end of this.

## 8. Starting point for our contract

### 8.1 What the published schemes mostly agree on (reasonable defaults)

1. **Fixed-price shares in a batch, not "any amount".**
   - Freshie (Tk 50k), iFarmer Shariah (BDT 135k), WeGro (per-project unit) and Investify (BDT 20k) all use a fixed unit.
   - A unit makes pro-rata profit and loss arithmetic exact. A per-person cap (iFarmer's "max 2 shares/booking") stops one investor dominating.
2. **Profit-sharing, no guaranteed return.**
   - Every profit-sharing scheme says so explicitly (Freshie: a guaranteed % "would be interest"; WeGro: "does not guarantee returns").
   - The schemes that promised fixed monthly returns all collapsed.
   - A guaranteed return also makes the pool look like deposit-taking under the Bank Company Act (§4).
3. **The investors' share of profit, where published, is 30–50%.**
   - iFarmer 40, WeGro 40 (45 in 2022), nusaQu 30, MyLembu 30, Vestifarm 50, Shohoz "50-50" [SNIPPET].
   - The lower end (30%) goes with the farm bearing feed, shelter and care itself (MyLembu). The 40% cases have a third party (the rearing farmer) also taking a cut.
   - No scheme publishes a split for the case where the farm is itself the rearer, as ours is.
4. **Loss falls on investors in proportion to capital, capped at capital, except for the farm's negligence or breach.**
   - iFarmer Shariah and DeenAgro publish this. It is the rule in the BNM and TKBB standards.
5. **Direct costs are charged to the batch; the farm's own contribution is not billed.**
   - Freshie: farm pays the shed, pool pays feed and treatment. MyLembu: farm pays everything and takes a larger share.
   - The standards allow identifiable direct expenses to be charged but no extra fee for the manager's own work.
6. **Documents at the start:**
   - A certificate naming the investor, units, batch, start and end date (iFarmer, WeGro, nusaQu).
   - A signed agreement, stamped on request (WeGro: free up to BDT 300k).
   - Nominee details (Freshie, DeenAgro).
7. **Reporting:** periodic updates with photos and weight. Monthly is the most common promise (iFarmer for longer cycles, nusaQu weights, Vestifarm). Farm visits are allowed with notice.
8. **Payout:** once, at the end, principal plus profit, into a **bank account in the investor's own name**.
   - Timing: WeGro 3–6 business days; nusaQu 2 weeks after Eid; Freshie about 2 weeks after the batch closes.
   - bKash or Nagad appear for deposits on some platforms but rarely for payouts.
   - Tax deducted at source comes off the profit, with a certificate (iFarmer 2019).
9. **Money is locked for the batch**, with a short cooling-off period (iFarmer: 1 week, full refund).
10. **No referral commissions** to investors (MLM Act 2013).

### 8.2 The real forks the farm has to decide

**F1. Contract form.**

- (a) **Mudarabah:** investors provide all capital, the farm manages and contributes shed, staff and utilities in kind; closest to Freshie and iFarmer Shariah.
- (b) **Musharakah:** the farm (or the Owner) also puts cash in and shares loss on that cash too.
- (c) **Fixed-return or Murabaha-style** (biniyog, DanaHub, iFarmer 2020): simpler for investors, but the farm carries all market risk and it looks like debt or deposit.
- **If the Owner invests personally,** decide whether the Owner is simply another share-holder in the batch (same unit, same terms) or the farm's capital contribution under (b). The two must not be mixed.
- **More than 20 investors in a partnership-style pool** meets Companies Act s.4 (§4). Get legal advice on whether each investor contracts separately with the farm company.

**F2. Which running costs are charged to the batch.**

- **Everyone agrees:** cattle purchase, feed, medicine and vet go to the batch.
- **They split on staff salaries and electricity:**
  - Freshie charges them to the pool.
  - MyLembu's farm bears them and takes 50% plus 20%.
  - Our plan (farm provides sheds, staff, utilities) is the MyLembu side, and justifies a **larger farm share**.
- **Still open:** transport, haat or market fees, broker commission, and any reseller fee (nusaQu deducts a per-head reseller fee before profit). Under either choice, list every chargeable category in the contract, because the standards require charged costs to be "identifiable and measurable".

**F3. Management fee first, or split only.**

- iFarmer charged BDT 3,000 per animal upfront in 2019; nusaQu takes a fee before profit.
- Everyone else uses a split only, and the Mudarabah standards bar the manager from a separate fee for their own work.
- A fee first guarantees the farm's costs are covered but moves the scheme away from the label investors are buying.

**F4. The split itself.** No published figure matches "farm is both rearer and manager, and bears shed, staff and utilities". The range to choose within is investors 30% (farm bears everything) to 50%. The farm has to price it from its own cost of shed, staff and utilities per head per month.

**F5. Unit of account: pooled batch, or named animal.**

- **Pooled share** (Freshie, iFarmer Shariah, WeGro): one death is spread over everyone; simple settlement.
- **Named animal** (iFarmer 2020, nusaQu, MyLembu): investors see "their" cow's weight; a death hits one investor unless there is a mutual pool.
- A batch share with **weight updates reported per animal** gets most of both.

**F6. Animal death.**

- (a) **Loss to the batch** (Freshie, iFarmer Shariah).
- (b) **Commercial insurance charged to the batch:** Green Delta has a 4–6-month beef-fattening cover for death by disease or accident, not theft; the premium is not published; Tk 3,000–4,000 per animal in 2022 [SECONDARY].
- (c) **Farm-run mutual pool or replacement** (nusaQu): not a licensed insurer.
- Under every option, a death caused by the farm's negligence is the farm's loss (standards). The contract needs a **postmortem or vet certificate rule**, since iFarmer and WeGro both promise "supporting documents".

**F7. Cattle unsold at the end of the window** (e.g. after Eid). Only nusaQu publishes a rule. Options:

- (a) **The farm buys them** at a stated valuation: Eid price, market price on a date, or live weight × published rate. This is a related-party sale, so the valuation method must be fixed in advance.
- (b) **Extend the batch** by a set number of weeks, with feed cost continuing to the batch.
- (c) **Settle the rest later** as they sell.
- Vestifarm's 38% loss after a weak post-Eid market is the risk.

**F8. Early exit.**

- (a) **No exit** (iFarmer, nusaQu; DeenAgro only by mutual consent).
- (b) **WeGro's rule:** full refund in the first 20% of the tenure; after that, a charge of 2% or BDT 2,500 and no profit, with 30 working days' notice.
- (c) **Transfer the share** to another investor at an agreed price.
- **Death of the investor:** nominee or heirs step in (DeenAgro); name this separately. The current iFarmer terms have a section for it but it could not be read.

**F9. A batch that is not fully funded.** No scheme publishes a rule. Freshie closes at target; WeGro gives 15 business days to pay after booking. Options:

- (a) A **minimum raise and a deadline**; refund in full if missed.
- (b) **Proceed with fewer cattle**, same share maths.
- (c) The **farm or Owner tops up** to the target (this becomes F1(b)).

**F10. The settlement document.** No scheme publishes one. The standards require the manager to "furnish the reason" for a loss. Decide what it contains, at minimum:

- Head bought and sold, each with purchase price, sale price, weight in and out.
- Deaths and postmortems.
- Each cost line charged to the batch.
- Gross profit, split, tax deducted, and payout per share.

Also decide whether it is signed and when it is sent relative to the payout.

**F11. Payout channel.** Bank only (iFarmer, WeGro, Freshie), or also bKash or Nagad. MFS (mobile financial service) wallets have transaction limits a share-sized payout may exceed; bank transfer leaves the clearer record.
