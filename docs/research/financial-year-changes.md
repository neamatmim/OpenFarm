# When the financial year itself changes

**Question:** OpenFarm reads every year from one month, `OPENFARM_YEAR_STARTS` (ADR 0016). What if the government changes the fiscal year? What does Bangladeshi law say the year is today, is a change coming, how have other countries bridged a change, and what does an accountant expect for the odd-length year in between?

**Researched:** 4–5 October 2026. Primary sources where they could be reached (statutes on bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd, the NBR's gazetted English text of the Income Tax Act 2023, the Irish Oireachtas, US Code, Indonesian statute text, the Riksbank, the IFRS Foundation, FRC Bangladesh). Where only a newspaper or a law firm's note could be found it is marked **[SECONDARY]**. Neither the Cabinet Division's notice nor the Press Information Department's handout on the 2026 decision could be found online (the Cabinet Division's notifications page lists no such notice, and the PID site loads its notes by script). The Bangladesh decision below therefore rests on the state news agency BSS and several national papers, all of which agree.

**Headline:** This is no longer hypothetical. **On 17 August 2026 the Cabinet decided to move Bangladesh's fiscal year from July–June to April–March.** FY2027-28 will be a **nine-month transition year, July 2027 to March 2028**, and FY2028-29 starts on 1 April 2028. It is a decision, not yet law: the Constitution (art. 152) and the General Clauses Act 1897 (s.3(19)) both still say the year begins on 1 July, and a five-member committee set up on 23 August 2026 is working out the amendments. The Income Tax Act 2023 has not been changed. Elsewhere, transition periods have lasted 3, 6, 9 and 18 months, some countries changed twice, and some were settled only weeks before, or even after, the odd period began.

## 1. Bangladesh: the year today

### 1.1 The law that fixes July

- **Constitution, art. 152:** "financial year" means a year commencing on the first day of July ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-367/section-24717.html)).
- **General Clauses Act 1897, s.3(19):** "financial year" shall mean the year commencing on the first day of July and ending on the 30th day of June. The clause was substituted by the General Clauses (Amendment) Order 1972 (P.O. 147 of 1972) ([bdlaws](http://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-73/section-19961.html)). The wording before 1972 was not retrieved.

### 1.2 The Income Tax Act 2023: "income year" (আয়বর্ষ)

The NBR published an Authentic English Text by S.R.O. No. 404-Law/2025, dated 8 October 2025 and gazetted 16 October 2025. It includes amendments up to the Finance Act 2024 ([NBR PDF](<https://nbr.gov.bd/uploads/acts/3190-SRO-404-NBR-16_October_2025(10455-10741).pdf>)). Section 2(15) reads, in substance:

- **The rule:** "income year" means the financial year immediately preceding the assessment year. Through the General Clauses Act, that is **1 July to 30 June**. The "assessment year" (s.2(24)) is the 12 months from 1 July.
- **Short income years are already built in.** (a) A new business's first income year runs from the day it is set up to the next 30 June. (b) Newly arising income is treated the same way. (c)–(e) The last year of a business, partnership or company runs from 1 July to the day it ends. **The tax law has always had years shorter than twelve months.**
- **(f) Banks, insurers and finance companies** (and their subsidiaries) use "the period of 12 months commencing from the first day of January", that is **January–December**. The Finance Act 2024, s.14(a), replaced "financial institution" with "finance company" from 1 July 2024.
- **Proviso: foreign-parent companies.** "Subject to the approval of the Deputy Commissioner of Taxes", a different 12-month period may be the income year for a subsidiary of a parent incorporated outside Bangladesh, its own subsidiary, a holding company, or a branch, representative or liaison office, if it needs a different year in order to consolidate with the parent.

ICAB's slides on the Finance Ordinance 2025 show no change to s.2(15) ([ICAB](https://www.icab.org.bd/icabadmin/uploads/ckeditor/4053Finance%20Ordinance%202025_Income%20Tax_ICAB_3Sept25.pdf)). The Finance Act 2026 was not checked for this clause.

**A precedent from Bangladesh itself:** the Finance Act 2015 required every company except banks, insurers and financial institutions to use a July–June year from 1 July 2016. Before that, a company could choose its own year-end. Advisers say no guidance was issued on whether to bridge with a **6-month or an 18-month** period ([FM Consulting](https://fmcibd.com/blog/117-universal-income-year-for-companies-in-bangladesh) **[SECONDARY]**). The 2015 Act's own text was not retrieved.

**Company law allows a long year.** Under the Companies Act 1994, s.183(4), a company's "financial year" "may be less or more than a calendar year, but shall not exceed fifteen months", or eighteen with the Registrar's special permission ([unofficial English text](https://cdn1.ogrlegal.com/files/rjsc/Bangladesh-COMPANIES-ACT.pdf)).

### 1.3 Proposals before 2026 (none adopted)

- A change was attempted in 2010 but never made. This is the Finance Ministry's account as reported by [TBS, 13 Aug 2026](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/govt-plans-shift-fiscal-year-july-june-april-march-1513576) **[SECONDARY]**.
- In July 2017 Finance Minister AMA Muhith said the year would not change soon: "It cannot be done quickly, it might need two to three more years". He added that most people wanted a January–December year ([bdnews24, 12 Jul 2017](https://bdnews24.com/economy/bangladesh-not-changing-fiscal-year-now-finance-minister) **[SECONDARY]**).
- On 9 June 2026, Jamaat-e-Islami's shadow budget for FY27 proposed **January–December** ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/politics/412176/jamaat-unveils-tk8.39-lakh-crore-shadow-budget) **[SECONDARY]**).

## 2. How such a change is made

- **In Bangladesh:** a Cabinet decision (made), then **a constitutional amendment to art. 152** and **an amendment to the General Clauses Act s.3(19)** (both pending). Since the Income Tax Act defines the income year as "the financial year" before the assessment year, it would follow the new definition. However, s.2(15)(a)–(e) name **30 June and 1 July in words**, and s.2(23)/(24) name **30 November, 15 September and 1 July**. Those must be amended too, most likely in a Finance Act, although that is not confirmed. The **transition shape chosen is short (9 months), not long (21 months)**. No source discusses a long transition for Bangladesh.
- **Elsewhere:** by statute (US 1974, Ireland 2001, Indonesia 2000, Canada 1906), by a government decision approved by parliament (Myanmar 2017), or by central-bank letter and ministry notification for one sector first (Myanmar 2021). See §3.

## 4. What an accountant expects for the odd year

- **IAS 1 paragraph 36** (Frequency of reporting): "An entity shall present a complete set of financial statements (including comparative information) at least annually. When an entity changes the end of its reporting period and presents financial statements for a period longer or shorter than one year, an entity shall disclose, in addition to the period covered by the financial statements: (a) the reason for using a longer or shorter period, and (b) the fact that amounts presented in the financial statements are not entirely comparable." Paragraph 37 allows a 52-week year. **IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1 for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027.** The IASB's reference material carries paragraph 36 into IFRS 18 (as paragraph 28) with almost the same wording ([IFRS 18 reference material](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/project/primary-financial-statements/ifrs-standard/reference-material-for-ifrs18.pdf)).
- **What this means in practice:** the odd period is reported **as it was**, at its true length, next to a 12-month comparative. It is not restated or annualised, and the notes say why it is a different length and that the figures are not comparable. Sweden's official statistics do the same with 1923 and 1995/96. Annualising, as Ireland's 74% and Myanmar's doubling did, is a **tax** device for thresholds, not an accounting one.
- **Bangladesh adopted IFRS.** The Financial Reporting Act 2015 s.40(1)(a) has the Financial Reporting Council set standards "in conformity with" the IASB's. FRC Bangladesh says it "adopted IFRS (without modifications) in 2020", has also adopted **IFRS for SMEs**, and that the standards bind **Public Interest Entities** ([FRC presentation to the IFRS Foundation, Nov 2025](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/meetings/2025/november/eeg/ap7-frc-bangladesh-presentation.pdf); [IFRS jurisdiction profile](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/jurisdictions/pdf-profiles/bangladesh-ifrs-profile.pdf)). A company is a PIE if its revenue was at least ৳50 crore, or if it meets two of these: liabilities of ৳10 crore or more, assets of ৳30 crore or more, 50 or more staff. A 100–500 head farm may or may not cross these. Either way, the accountant will expect IAS 1.36-style treatment. Whether FRC has adopted IFRS 18 was not verified.

## What this means for OpenFarm

1. **A real change is scheduled for this farm.** If it becomes law as decided, the farm's year will be: …2026-27 (Jul–Jun), **2027-28 (Jul 2027 – Mar 2028, nine months)**, 2028-29 (Apr 2028 – Mar 2029)… One `OPENFARM_YEAR_STARTS` cannot describe that. The year has to become **a history of start months, each with the date it took effect**, or a list of explicit year boundaries. Past years must be read with the rule that applied to them.
2. **Three shapes must be modelled:**
   - a **short transition year**: 3, 6 or 9 months (USA, Myanmar, Ireland, Indonesia, Canada, Bangladesh 2027-28);
   - a **long transition year**: 15 to 18 months (Sweden 1995/96; allowed for Bangladeshi companies by Companies Act s.183(4); debated for the 2016 company change);
   - **more than one change over time** (Sweden 1923 and 1996; Myanmar 2018 and 2022, which went back to the old year).
   - Also, possibly, a **stand-alone transition period that belongs to no year** (the US "transition quarter").
3. **Names can collide.** ADR 0016 names a year by the calendar year it starts in. That holds for Bangladesh's change: 2027 is Jul 2027 – Mar 2028 and 2028 starts Apr 2028. It breaks when the start moves **later** in the calendar inside one year. Myanmar's April–September 2018 interim and its October 2018 year both start in 2018, and so do Sweden's "1923" (Jan–Jun) and "1923/24". A long year leaves a **gap**: Sweden goes from "1995/96" to "1997", with no year starting in 1996. Names must come from the boundaries, and the label must allow "2027-28 (9 months)".
4. **Past years stay as they were.** Never re-cut a closed year into the new shape. Every authority reports the odd period at its true length and says it is not comparable (IAS 1.36, the Riksbank). Screens comparing years must show **how many months** each covers. Any "per year" figure (a year's net, per-head-per-year averages) must use the year's real length. Annualising, if offered at all, should be a labelled extra.
5. **The change can come with little notice, or after the period has begun.** Indonesia had 11 days. Myanmar's banks had 3 weeks. Myanmar fixed its 2019 private-sector income year on 15 May 2019 for a period that began on 1 April. Announced changes can also be reversed (Madhya Pradesh) or never happen (Bangladesh 2010 and 2017). **The Owner must be able to record a change on the day it is announced and correct it later**, without a code release, and nothing derived from a year should be stored in a way that cannot be recomputed.
6. **Different years for different parties.** Bangladesh already runs three income years at once: July–June for most taxpayers, January–December for banks, insurers and finance companies, and a DCT-approved year for foreign-parent subsidiaries. Myanmar moved banks a year before everyone else. The farm's own year (its accountant's) is the one OpenFarm should follow. It may not move at the same moment as the government's budget year. **Keep "the farm's financial year" separate from "the national fiscal year".** Nothing in the farm should assume the two are the same, or that a bank or Investor's year matches either.
7. **Revisit when:** the Constitution and General Clauses Act are amended; the NBR publishes the transition rule for the 2027-28 income year (length, scaled thresholds, and whether banks change); or the farm's accountant says how they will close the books for July 2027 – March 2028.
