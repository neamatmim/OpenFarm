# Suppliers keep invoices and credit separate from cash

Accepted, 2026-10-12.

The farm buys feed repeatedly from the same suppliers and sometimes pays later. A delivery's full price is needed to value feed and animal consumption, but that price cannot also claim cash left the farm when the supplier has not been paid.

Supplier profiles extend the existing counterparty identity. They keep contact details, a contact person, credit days, notes, a version and a retirement date. The same trader remains one person when also buying from the farm. Existing feed suppliers are brought into the directory by migration; old receipts retain their original fully-paid meaning. No historical invoices, debts or cash movements are invented. Contacts and terms are edited with a reason and an optimistic version check. Retirement and restoration are the owner's decision. A supplier with active purchase requests or orders cannot be retired. Retirement preserves history and allows existing invoices to be paid.

A supplier invoice has a supplier's number, invoice date and due date. Numbers are unique within a supplier and farm, ignoring case; one invoice may cover several deliveries, provided its dates agree. Its value is the sum of its actual feed receipts, rather than an additional expense. An optional invoice image is stored separately from ordinary list responses and is accessible only to owners and managers. Attaching or replacing a copy requires a reason and is audited. Copies are images; this release does not upload PDF attachments.

The receiving form offers fully paid or credit/partial payment. An invoice is required for credit and optional for fully paid purchases. An invoiced delivery records exactly what was paid at its arrival, including zero. The stock and costing retain the full price. Only the amount actually paid creates a `feed_in` cash event. Later supplier payments are explicit, invoice-linked records under the original feed category; they never create stock or a second feed cost. Owner payments use a personal session, ordinary bank/mobile-account references and the existing cash-book safeguards. They are recorded payments, not instructions to a bank.

Until explicitly entered, the invoice date follows the receiving day and the due date follows that day plus the supplier's credit days (the order's captured terms for a linked delivery). Changing the day or supplier updates these suggestions without overwriting manually entered dates. Display, validation and submission use the same dates.

For another delivery under an existing bill, the receiving form offers a searchable saved-invoice picker scoped to the chosen supplier and farm. Its read returns only invoice headers and balances, without payment history or attachments, and is limited to owners and managers. Only invoices dated on or before the receiving day are offered. A selected invoice keeps its recorded number and dates read-only; starting a new invoice restores editable dates. Changing suppliers clears the saved reference. The invoice's existing balance and this delivery's unpaid amount are labeled separately, and selecting a bill never copies earlier payments into the new delivery.

Requests, deliveries, payment recording and relevant corrections serialize under the farm lock. Payments cannot exceed the outstanding delivered value, and cannot predate their invoice or use a later receipt to justify a backdated payment. Client identifiers make retries idempotent. Receipt corrections and voids must preserve valid invoice balances and the existing venture settlement protections. Changing an invoiced receipt's initial payment is owner-only. Later payments can be corrected or voided by the owner with a reason; their audit history remains and the cash book changes with them.

Feed → Suppliers shows contacts, purchase history, invoices, payments, current balances and overdue aging. Open dues remain visible for retired suppliers. Owner and manager home screens flag overdue supplier balances; the owner's all-clear badge cannot overlook them. Supplier payments and their invoice references appear in the money register and accountant exports.

Money → Supplier payments provides a payment planner across all unpaid feed invoices, including retired suppliers. It groups invoices by supplier, ordered by earliest due date, with supplier/phone/invoice search and filters for overdue, the next seven or thirty calendar days, and month-end. The server's farm day anchors every window; seven/thirty days include today and exclude overdue balances, and the thirty-day total includes the seven-day total. Month-end includes overdue balances. Totals use integer paisa and actual delivery and payment balances, exclude paid bills, and do not depend on the money register's reporting period. The planner returns only invoice headers, supplier identity and balances; full invoice history and attachments are read separately when requested. Owners use the existing payment and correction flows, managers review invoice details, and successful writes refresh the plan. The overdue notice links straight to the planner. Planned totals create no cash movement and include no promised deliveries or unapproved purchases.

The farm generates purchase orders, payment vouchers and supplier balance statements through the existing bilingual paper renderer and isolated A4 printing flow. The browser can save these as PDF. Purchase orders require approval, identify their system number and revision, and carry the supplier credit days captured when requested. A later directory edit cannot silently alter those terms. Both initial delivery payments and later payments have vouchers; bank references and the farm account are printed where recorded. Paper exports are audited snapshots. A purchase order is a request to supply goods; the supplier remains the issuer of the supplier invoice.

This release manages feed supplier invoices. Supplier advances, refunds, credit notes, non-feed payables, automatic bank transfers and external reminders are separate work. Invoice balances reflect recorded deliveries rather than promised goods.
